# Bearded Dragon · Desert Terrarium

A separate dry habitat for the dragon formerly in Weatherglass's rainforest. The supplied enclosure image guided its wide black frame, sandstone basking shelf, two shelters, weathered driftwood, sandy soil, desert backdrop, overhead lamps, food and water dishes. One living bearded dragon retains the original sculpt, 50-bone rig, scales, independent toes, breathing, blinking, hunting, grazing, memory and temperature-driven activity. Its feet and navigation sample the actual rocks and driftwood. No other animals share its enclosure.

- Build: `npm ci && npm run build`
- Controls: drag to orbit, scroll/pinch to zoom, Follow dragon, Feed cricket (then tap to place), Fresh greens, colour morphs and a lamp schedule. Keys F, M, H, R, 1 and 2. `?ui=0` hides the UI.
- Model: the user's existing procedural dragon from `scripts/terrarium/`, retained here with its baked binary.
- Backdrop and sandstone texture: built-in ImageGen, with the user's image as the composition reference. The log uses CC0 Poly Haven Rough Wood maps on custom closed, displaced branches. Aloe, cacti and shrubs are 3D geometry guided by the [fal GPT Image 2 plant studies and prompts](references/plants/README.md). See [asset credits](public/ATTRIBUTION.md).
- Habitat conditions are illustrative simulation values. Heat/light fade at night, and the dragon rests in the cool shelter. Its warm ledge, cool shaded hide, ventilation and internal UVB tube follow the broad layout principles in the [RSPCA bearded dragon guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/other/beardeddragon).

The enclosure measures 1.6 × 0.9 × 0.64 metres in scene units. Its expanded front-to-back depth provides 50% more floor area than the original 0.6-metre depth, with food and water near the front, planting along the edges and connected roaming space across the full floor. The dragon, shelters and climbing wood retain their scale.

Locomotion uses anchored stance feet, planned individual steps, a flexible trunk and an articulated tail with gravity, inertia and contact friction. The scene advances at a fixed 60 Hz and tail physics at 120 Hz; a suspended tab resumes without catching up in one large movement. Graphics context recovery retains the animal's state instead of reloading it onto the basking ledge. Parallel transport keeps the tail's cross-section from flipping at vertical hangs; the pelvis follows the trunk independently of tail contact. The tail collides against the full wood/stone meshes along each segment, including its visible skin thickness; unsupported sections sag freely. Swept contacts prevent tunneling, and each solid resolves a contact manifold without opposing per-triangle kicks.

Before lifting a foot, the controller finds a reachable surface patch for its sole and splayed toes and sweeps the complete toe fan along the step. Stance soles stay fixed in world space. Body movement, height and tilt are fitted to those contacts before the pose is committed, and the airborne foot's landing remains reachable. Climbs transfer one foot at a time, allowing a small weight shift on the three planted feet; level walking retains separate forelimb and hindlimb stance timing. A transfer waits for its elbow or knee to clear a surface before advancing farther. Broad collision capsules lead to a detailed check against an envelope sampled from the original leg mesh, including the rounded shoulder and hip joints. Feet lift before crossing a riser and approach the landing orientation during swing. Carried knee and hand frames avoid IK singularity spins, and toes conform individually to curved wood and stone.

Navigation preserves usable corners and reserves turning clearance for the whole stance. A broad worn sandstone incline, using the existing stone photograph, replaces the narrow stair corners between the ledge and the sand. The dragon backs into the hide with its head facing out. The water dish sits slightly farther toward the front-right corner to clear the hide entrance, and small decorative stones sit outside the walking corridor. Stance width and toe orientation adjust on narrow perches, in the hide and beside the glass. A blocked approach can back away and replan; it never relocates the animal to a waypoint. Support rays distinguish the top of a solid from its underside and from a genuinely overhead branch. Forepaws remain outside their shoulders, with collision-aware elbows and knees. Looking around and lifting the head cannot rotate the shoulder girdle away from the planted forefeet.

Run `npm test` for the regression suite. In addition to cadence, limb lengths, tail contacts, plant pressure and dish tests, the locomotion suite replays a continuous tour against an export of the actual habitat collision meshes, including both bowls. It measures stance sliding, rendered foot anchors, support gaps, limb lengths and body displacement on every simulation tick, and samples skinned-limb penetration at 10 Hz. The fixture contains geometry only; the dragon's sculpt and textures are unchanged.

Cave rear walls follow the rounded openings and sit inside the stone arches, avoiding an exposed dark rectangle behind the cool hide. Individual aloe blades and shrub stems bend down under contact with the dragon's body, feet, and tail, then recover gradually. Their roots stay anchored; margin teeth, seed clusters, geometry normals, and shadows follow the same bends. Broad shallow body contacts leave low leaves beneath the belly alone. Plant regressions check pressure clearance, recovery, rooted geometry, frame cadence, and overhead passes.

The feeding tray follows the original reference: a thick chipped oval stone rim and a properly closed basin, using the existing sandstone photograph. The dragon keeps its chest and head raised to the salad when grazing; bites remove portions and the Greens control restores the full salad.

## Movement research and scale

The animal represents a central bearded dragon, *Pogona vitticeps*. The baked sculpt spans roughly 30 sculpt units, but its scene scale is **0.016 metres per unit**, giving an adult about **49 cm long**, including the tail. Cadence and reach use that scene scale; the old 30 cm description referred to the unscaled sculpt.

Visual references include the XROMM paper's two fluoroscope views and its looped forelimb reconstruction, plus Taronga's resting/perching and feeding presentation. Edited, slowed and looped footage is used for posture and limb sequencing; it is not treated as an unedited timing measurement. Speeds between the measured examples, acceleration, turning limits, tail stiffness, breathing and normalized internal drives remain engineering estimates. This is a behavior-informed animation, not a validated physiological model or husbandry guide.

## Controller ownership and regression checks

`LizardBrain` chooses activities, routes and desired motion. `LizardRig` owns the accepted root pose, support constraints, anatomical limb frames and committed foot transfers. `FootTransfer` gives each transfer a continuous start and touchdown. Gait phase requests a lift; it cannot skip a foot partway into its arc. A deterministic candidate budget spreads difficult foothold searches across ticks while existing feet remain planted. Unfinished transfers report recovery needs back to navigation.

The independent head solver checks an envelope of the actual head, neck and jaw mesh and raises the short neck without moving the shoulder supports. A near target crossing behind the neck cannot flip gaze between opposite limits. Jaw opening is eased. Tongue reach has a bounded direction and excursion. `PreyContact` tests the swept mouth/tongue against the insect's body after the final pose is accepted and rejects contact through hardscape. Prey under the chin triggers withdrawal; an insect repeatedly escaping across ledges may be left alone after a bounded pursuit.

`DragonNeeds` tracks illustrative satiety, thirst and fatigue. Feeding and drinking change those drives; supported rest reduces fatigue. Temperature and environmental light retain their existing role. The simulation now runs in real seconds rather than applying a global 1.5× multiplier to physiology, behavior and gait together. Render interpolation remains separate from the 60 Hz contact solver and 120 Hz tail solver.

The habitat tour and obstacle hunts sample rendered head, jaw, neck, trunk, feet and limbs in addition to controller targets. Separate tests cover lift-off continuity, different fore/hind duty factors, physical capture, gaze singularities, needs, tail stability and varied display cadence. `node --import tsx tools/measure-gait.ts` reports actual distance, cadence and support ratios on level ground; those values are simulation measurements, not additional biological evidence.

### Hide exit and rock pursuit, 31 October 2026

Foothold searches discard negligible repositioning before ranking candidates, allowing a useful withdrawal beneath the hide instead of repeatedly selecting the same blocked support. During elevated transfers, the complete transported limb arc is checked and an already valid supported torso stays steady until touchdown. Descending feet retract as they lift, avoiding an initial vertical pull on an already extended leg.

Rock approaches from sand use open turning space and the low ramp entrance. Supported recovery pivots follow the destination along the slope, including uphill. The hide approach centres the head on the doorway before advancing; leaving the tray can use a short supported side step when its straight withdrawal is obstructed. The dragon's sculpt and material assets remain unchanged.

### Refinements, 30 October 2026

Raised transfers now distinguish ascent from descent: a descending foot spends more of its arc lowering toward the support. Reversing a blocked transfer retraces the same curve. Tight pivots use short, individual support transfers, with footholds preparing for the requested turn before the torso rotates. Climbing transfers retain their heading, with a brief four-foot support interval for steering between steps. These timing envelopes are engineering estimates informed by the published walking observations, not measured adult rock-climbing curves.

The controller checks reach during the raised part of a step as well as at touchdown. A forecast shoulder position is accepted only when the other feet and the body can support that projected torso shift; moving an isolated shoulder no longer approves a transfer that the remaining stance prevents. A rejected transfer can use a small, collision-checked lateral weight shift or one additional support adjustment to settle, preferring its diagonal partner but allowing another limb at its reach or fold limit to move while two anchors remain planted. Level routes favour nearby sand over an unnecessary foothold on the edge of a log. The front sand crossing reserves clearance for the forefeet before the root reaches the warm rocks. Leaving the food tray uses a separate backing route into open sand; the original destination remains intact, and the exit ends at its physical clearance point. Intermediate ledge routes stop adding stair gateways at their destination instead of overshooting and doubling back. The whole-body route stays in the supported stair corridor instead of straddling its steep flank. A side approach joins the lower stair entry before ascending, and doorway centering allows the collision-checked lateral correction from the preceding turn. The forearm uses a consistent posterior bend reference, fades its angular bound near a mathematical singularity and limits corrections per tick. Regression checks inspect palm orientation and frame-to-frame bone rotation; the projected pole angle is retained as a diagnostic, since its sign can change without an inverted limb. An arbitrary fixed elbow position is not treated as measured anatomy.

Torso collision checks also span adjacent spine joints, covering the blended belly between them. Additional samples follow the original wide flank skin with its real bone weights, breathing and basking deformation. Head, neck and jaw clearance use spatial patches of the original sculpt, including the interior of the jaw outline that a convex envelope misses beside a curved bowl rim. The accepted head envelope also constrains body movement. Probe spacing and collision tolerances remain approximations rather than a continuous collision proof for every rendered triangle.

`RestTransition` waits for the last foot to land before relaxing the chest. A supported rest can include an occasional individual-foot adjustment, longer gaze holds and a gradual return to walking. An unsuccessful comfort adjustment waits before retrying; beginning a deliberate walk clears that cosmetic delay. During a committed transfer the limb retains its transported bend plane; a preferred resting elbow pose cannot rotate it into a riser after planning. Once a recovery landing is verified, it constrains the torso until touchdown; a slowly progressing recovery is reassessed before it leaves a paw hovering. The accepted pose retains its chest lift and lunge through the final bone update; a rejected posture cannot silently stretch a planted leg afterward. Existing contact errors are bounded separately at each joint, and the foothold planner receives the full habitat collision query as well as support heights.

`PreyObservation` estimates motion from spaced, visible position observations. Actual habitat meshes occlude its view. Pursuit uses a short, bounded prediction, checks the predicted patch against walkable terrain and falls back to the last observed position when the insect disappears. The animal still has to approach and make swept mouth or tongue contact. A new target cannot replace an active climb route until the supports permit a new heading. That departure still participates in stall detection even while the hunt remains in its notice phase. During a climb the neck follows the supported route until close to pickup distance, so staring sideways at obscured prey cannot pin the jaw against a riser. The exact radial approach avoids snapping between a small set of approach angles. Close aiming measures from the mouth. During fixation, optional resting adjustments wait; a short tongue pickup can proceed with both forefeet and at least one hind foot planted. The torso holds its heading and still passes all support constraints during the strike. The hide approach reserves room on the sand to turn, then includes a final supported step toward the opening and a withdrawal when prey disappears. An unsuccessful search for unseen prey ends without immediately retrying the same insect. After a chase makes no supported progress, automatic prey selection waits twelve seconds so nearby insects cannot repeatedly interrupt an exit or another activity.

Escape tests distinguish pursuit from guaranteed capture: an occluded insect may get away after a substantial approach. The moving-ledge check measures body progress toward the initially observed patch and travel relative to the initial gap; the fleeing insect may increase its separation during a careful descent. A separate controlled escape during an established pursuit verifies continued motion through a real cricket hop; hops already within pickup range may correctly trigger a pause. Physical contact, skin-clearance, limb-length, rotation and discontinuity tolerances are unchanged.

Strike locations now consider the support heights beneath both shoulder and hip girdles, favouring a level patch before the animal turns toward prey. A climb retains route-directed gaze until the mouth is within three-dimensional pickup distance; a nearby target in plan view alone cannot pull the head against a riser. Recovering a foot does not mark an active climb route impassable. A stationary-prey approach can continue for up to 90 seconds, while four seconds without supported progress or a prolonged moving-prey escape ends the attempt sooner. Side approaches to the food tray retract on their approach heading before looking toward the next destination. The flank collision samples use enclosing patches and reusable scratch storage to avoid repeating detailed queries and allocations in clear space.

A delayed transfer first looks for a verified support beneath its current paw before attempting to retrace an elevated arc. Once that recovery is lowering successfully, only stalled progress can replan it: total airborne age includes the rejected arc and must not restart a progressing lowering. Leaving a bowl restores the ordinary walking posture before following the next route. Ordinary travel tracks accepted body progress across withdrawal retries; six seconds without progress ends in a supported pause and route reassessment, instead of perpetual stepping in place. A foot still in transfer completes its recovery before that pause. Returning feet retain the same minimum fold clearance as planted limbs, so the torso cannot fold down over a paw that is trying to land. Cosmetic bend-plane adjustments cannot accumulate additional penetration while a support is recovering.

The lower rear edge of the warm ramp is excluded from body routes because its point-sized support cannot accommodate a turning stance. Limb skin probes use a 1.5 mm radius with a matching contact allowance, so their centres can meet a support without demanding an invisible air gap around a folded elbow. The separate visible-skin penetration, sole support, limb-length and planted-foot drift limits remain unchanged.

Withdrawal releases a sideways prey or route gaze on level ground as well as on climbs, allowing the neck to align while the supports move away from a bowl or log. The tray-exit allowance accommodates several support transfers before resuming the destination route. The continuous-run checker also accumulates failed active movement across activity changes: abandoning a chase and waiting cannot erase repeated attempts from the same stuck stance. A failed straight withdrawal can make a short supported side step toward available sand, using individual foot transfers and the same body, limb, glass and sole constraints. This is bounded recovery, not root relocation. If the side step also fails, the controller tries the remaining axial direction once while retaining the original no-progress clock. When the root and all current/next supports are at floor level, including shallow dish rims, the torso fitter preserves chest lift and lunge alongside height, pitch, roll and bend if a posture change would block supported movement. A high rear support keeps the climbing posture active until that last foot has descended. Saved tray-side, bowl-side, ramp-edge, rolled-perch and diagonal-ramp stances are replayed in separate full-journey regressions. Recovery compares millimetre-scale translations and small turns against the whole stance before selecting a bounded support shift. These queries restore the pose exactly and do not advance the simulation. A short recovery checks actual level terrain and detailed body/foot contacts; a root already inside the navigation grid's turning margin can recover its supports before reconnecting the route. A stalled, rolled stance can retain a short sequence of bounded horizontal weight shifts on sand or nearly level rock supports until it leaves that local contact, helping a folded limb clear without repeatedly rolling the torso farther sideways. If axial adjustments fail on elevated supports, a bounded coupled fit linearizes individual contact constraints, proposes a simultaneous translation/height/pitch/roll correction, and verifies it against the actual meshes before accepting it. Planted anchors and the existing per-tick movement bounds remain authoritative. Equivalent safe recovery turns prefer the route direction. On an incline, an all-planted stance that blocks both axial directions may pivot through supported individual steps; turning stops during an airborne climbing transfer. The escape aligns with the actual slope and can continue backing down until the feet reach a level stance and the head also clears the riser, preventing the route controller from immediately undoing the supported pivot. Recovery landings validate a single continuously transported knee/elbow plane through sixteen samples, rather than accepting disconnected clear joint orientations. Returning foot transfers and the torso fitter use the hind limb's two-bone geometric minimum while it withdraws; the final weight-bearing landing still obeys the ordinary stance reach. Fold and solid-contact allowances are independent, so a pre-existing fold cannot permit the knee to enter stone. A captured descending-knee regression checks that non-worsening guarantee. Resting elbow adjustments are checked against the transported limb plane and cannot worsen its clearance; bounded corrections follow the actual tested elbow direction.

A foot whose existing toe fan is verified beneath an overhanging ledge can search for a low withdrawal after ordinary lifted transfers fail. That alternative sweeps the complete toe fan against the solid meshes; an overhead support cannot be mistaken for a mandatory riser, and a solid wall still rejects the transfer. A separate regression checks both cases.

Foothold checks include the original palm and heel surface in 4 mm patches, in addition to the toe fan. Route retries recognize accepted turning progress. A withdrawal keeps the neck aligned with its supports; an activity ending cannot cancel unfinished foot recovery. Rest continues the supported adjustment until touchdown, and new prey detection or a strike waits for recovery to finish. Moving-prey checks require measured pursuit and a clean end to an escape; an insect hopping away during a long descent is not required to remain within reach.

The moving-prey trials use the same bounded chase duration on every starting surface: an insect starting on sand can still hop repeatedly into the hardscape. At least half of the open-start trials must capture by physical contact; each escape must still show substantial pursuit and finish within the chase bound. The controlled-hop check measures both displacement and peak walking speed during the hop, allowing its timing to overlap different foot-support phases.

`AdaptiveQuality` watches sustained frame pacing and simulation cost. It can reduce pixel ratio, antialiasing, shadow resolution and postprocessing, while preserving the same fixed simulation steps and contact checks. Recovery to higher detail is slower than reduction. A graphics reduction that does not improve timing is undone, so a throttled browser or a low-refresh display does not continually lose detail. `?quality=high` pins full detail; `?lite` retains the existing manual low-cost preset. `?profile=1` includes the active quality tier in its playback diagnostics.

The run does not prescribe a tour: the brain chooses its activities and routes while seeded visitor events feed crickets, refill greens and change the lamps. ### Validation, 31 October 2026

All **106 regression tests** passed. Six repeated tours from three seeds completed all **30 destinations**, alongside a separate continuous five-destination skin audit. All five stationary obstacle hunts captured by contact. 6 of six varied open-ground moving-prey trials captured; escaped insects were left after bounded pursuits with real progress toward their observed locations.

The continuous randomized run completed **30 simulated minutes / 108,000 fixed physics steps** with **no flagged issues**, 8 cricket captures and 24.13 m of accepted travel. Seeded feeding, refilling, lights-out and waking events were included. See the [validation summary](references/validation/summary-2026-10-31.json) and [replayable run report](references/validation/soak-2026-10-31.json). The run report records the pre-commit checkout base; its simulation fingerprint identifies the tested working source in this release. This is one reproducible long run, not proof that every possible interaction is flawless.

Rendered review included a full descent onto sand and a moving-cricket capture, consumption and settling sequence. The visible 1280 x 720 full-detail descent sample averaged **57.0 fps**, with a 21.4 ms frame-time p95 and 1.000 simulation/wall-time ratio over 42.3 seconds. Its worst frame was 70.1 ms, with 0 over 100 ms. This sample was recorded after the headless validation jobs finished; these are local measurements, not a performance promise for every device.

The contact solver is kinematic: it preserves supports and reachable anatomy, but does not simulate measured muscle forces or prove physical balance for every possible stance. Collision envelopes and the skin audit are sampled, with millimetre-scale residual penetration permitted. Difficult climbs remain slower than open-ground pursuit, some insects escape, and behavior/soft-tissue timings not directly measured in the cited studies remain estimates. The unchanged sculpt and material assets preserve the dragon's appearance.
