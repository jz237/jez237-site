A small world, made visible.

The Living Aquascape is a cinematic, image-based interactive exhibit. Original photographic artwork supplies the fine planting, wood, stone, water and equipment detail. WebGL adds restrained local movement, animated animal cutouts, circulation paths and educational layers.

The camera pans and magnifies a photographic scene. This is not a freely orbiting volumetric aquarium, a path-traced renderer, or a measured digital twin. The leaf macro is a separate authored close-up. Equipment mode provides a photographic inspection guide rather than a physically exploded mechanical assembly.

What the ecosystem model means

The illustrative 180 L freshwater model tracks temperature, dissolved CO₂ and oxygen, organic waste, ammonia-N, nitrite-N, nitrate-N, biofilter maturity, plant biomass and a generic algae index. Nitrogen readings are mg nitrogen/L, not nitrate-ion concentrations. The CO₂ control is a supply setting with an equivalent concentration scale; it is not the instantaneous dissolved reading.

Integration steps are capped at one simulated minute. Gas exchange and temperature respond over hours, while biomass and algae change over days to weeks. Waste mineralization feeds two stages of nitrification, which consume oxygen. Light, carbon, nutrient supply and circulation limit modeled photosynthesis. Fish stay at a comfortable viewing speed when ecosystem time accelerates.

The balance score weights oxygen (35%), dissolved nitrogen (35%), circulation (15%) and algae (15%). Open Chemistry to see every factor. The score is an educational summary, not a livestock health assessment.

The pH display uses pH = 7 − log₁₀(CO₂ / (3 × KH)), a carbonate-only approximation. Active aquasoils, organic acids and non-carbonate buffers can invalidate it. The model does not resolve GH, conductivity, phosphate, individual trace nutrients, species-specific tolerances or complete carbon and nutrient mass balances. Its coefficients are illustrative, not fitted to a real aquarium.

Visual interpretation

Streamlines are analytic teaching paths, not computational fluid dynamics. Root lines represent uptake zones and scale with modeled biomass; they are not reconstructed root anatomy. Generic algae tint and small plant-image shifts illustrate change without simulating individual leaf topology. The scene does not diagnose specific algae or microorganisms. Fish use local steering, separation, schooling and feeding targets with deforming photographic cutouts; their anatomy is not a complete 3D model.

Performance uses lower pixel density and fewer bubbles. High is the default; Ultra increases pixel density. Photography pauses the simulation for composition and PNG capture. None uses hardware ray tracing or progressive offline accumulation. These settings do not change ecosystem state. Reduced motion keeps the view and animals still while the numerical ecosystem can continue.

Sound is off by default. Its quiet synthetic filter hum and filtered water noise follow the flow control. No narration or music is required to understand the exhibit. If WebGL or a visual asset fails, a static scene remains available with the ecosystem controls.

Scientific background

  • Oxygen enters water through atmospheric exchange and photosynthesis; temperature influences its saturation concentration. USGS: Dissolved oxygen and water.
  • Photosynthesis and respiration contribute to daily oxygen and pH cycles. USGS: Diurnal variations in water quality.
  • Nitrification sequentially oxidizes ammonia to nitrite and nitrate. EPA: Nitrification.

Artwork and source

Scene, species artwork and leaf macro were generated specifically for this exhibit with built-in image generation, guided by the owner's supplied aquarium concept. They are illustrative artwork, not field photographs or scientific identification records. No artwork from the earlier Aquarium Laboratory demo is loaded.
