# Cleaner shrimp compatibility and model study

Added two adult Pacific cleaner shrimp, *Lysmata amboinensis*, to the virtual
reef. A pair is a conservative social/display choice, not a stocking calculation
from an assumed aquarium volume. The scene has no calibrated physical capacity.

## Compatibility decision

- The existing community contains a blue tang, one *Chaetodon semilarvatus*, two
  ocellaris clownfish, seven female lyretail anthias, eight blue-green chromis,
  one royal gramma and one mandarin dragonet, plus corals and anemones.
- Adult cleaner shrimp are a reasonable addition to this fish community. This
  does not guarantee the behavior of every individual in a real aquarium.
  WetWebMedia's first-hand husbandry advice specifically includes semilarvatus
  with cleaner shrimp (Butterflyfish Compatibility, December 30, 2008). A
  separate February 8, 2010 report describes golden butterflies and two cleaner
  shrimp coexisting. These are expert/aquarist observations, not a controlled
  compatibility study.
- **Christmas tree worms were intentionally omitted.** The SAIAB species
  account lists polychaetes among semilarvatus prey. Christmas tree worms are
  sedentary polychaetes with exposed feeding crowns. Avoiding that combination
  follows the user's explicit compatibility condition; no existing fish was
  removed. It is an inference about predation risk, not proof that every
  butterflyfish will attack every Spirobranchus colony.
- No real animals, products or paid services were purchased.

## Anatomy and behavior

The editable Blender model contains a continuous cephalothorax, six overlapping
abdominal segments, a five-lobed tail fan, rostrum teeth, stalked eyes, ten
pereiopods, small anterior claws, feeding maxillipeds, swimmerets and setae.
Two antennae plus the four branches of paired antennules appear as six long
white sensory filaments. Red lateral dorsal bands flank a narrow white stripe.
The Aquarium of the Pacific page contains a contradictory “ten pairs” sentence;
the model uses the anatomically correct five pairs of walking legs.

One vertex-colored skinned mesh has 24,435 vertices and 104 articulated bones.
The two animals share the geometry but have independent skeletons and clocks.
Feet are placed against live-rock ray intersections. One leg at a time adjusts
its footing; the other feet remain planted while the body rocks. Antenna chains
carry small traveling bends, feeding appendages and swimmerets have separate
rhythms, and nearby fish increase the invitation display. There is no fabricated
parasite removal, forced fish encounter, feeding credit, or chemistry effect.
Night softens the motions; pause stops every appendage and decision.
