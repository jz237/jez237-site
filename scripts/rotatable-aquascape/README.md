# Rotatable Living Aquascape

Independent comparison to the photographic Living Aquascape. Public route: `/demos/rotatable-aquascape/`. The original lives at `/demos/living-aquascape/`; its scene and application bundle are unchanged by this project.

## Run

Requires Node 22.13 or newer. From this directory:

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
npm run build
npm test
```

The production build is in `dist/`. Copy its contents into `demos/rotatable-aquascape/` before site publication. Vite uses relative asset URLs; the demo can also be served independently from any static web server. No API keys, external CDNs, paid services, or external API calls are needed.

## Scene and controls

Three.js perspective scene with a limited front-and-side orbit. Drag to rotate; wheel/pinch or buttons to zoom. Front, three-quarter, and side presets ease to their destination. Pause freezes fish, food, water, plant sway, and bubbles while the camera remains usable. Evening dims the lighting. Feeding releases actual sinking particles that individual fish perceive and consume.

The landscape combines procedural glass, substrate, sand, planting, plumbing, light fixture and cabinet with freely licensed scanned hardscape. Poly Haven Dead Tree Trunk 02 retains its scanned geometry, UVs and physical material maps while being bent into asymmetric branches. Rock Moss Set 01 supplies independently placed irregular stones. Fern 02 supplies textured curved fronds with alpha-tested edges. The botanical plant meshes have modeled blades and petioles, varied proportions, tissue textures, venation and restrained current movement. See public/models/ATTRIBUTION.md for authors and CC0 source links. Water uses an actual two-sided planar scene reflection with moving distortion; the underside reflects planting and the top reflects the light fixture. The tetra skin reuses the user's existing `living-species.png` artwork; no new assets were purchased or generated using paid services.

## Fish

`Tetra3D` is adapted from the existing Living Aquascape mesh. The photographic depth-mask shader is removed; normal depth testing handles occlusion. `TetraKinematics`, `TetraSwimming`, `FishBrain`, `FishCollisions`, and `SchoolRoute` retain the established traveling body wave, steady head, independently animated fins, bursts/glides, inspections, upright turns, individual needs, memory, schooling, and continuous depth movement. The scene maps the behavior coordinates uniformly into world units; fish do not change scale as a fake substitute for perspective. `TankSpace` adds world-space hardscape contact and confines plant geometry to the glass.

31 automated tests cover the reused locomotion, body/fin motion, needs, food perception, schooling, depth and spacing, plus world-coordinate conversion, solid contact, leaf confinement, bounded reflection capture, and collision coverage of every vertex of the six tapered wood scans and all nine placed rock scans. Browser checks cover the camera presets, drag, zoom, feeding, pause, lighting, responsive controls and comparison navigation.

## Limits

This is a real-time 3D composition using both procedural planting and photogrammetric hardscape, not a path-traced reconstruction of the reference photograph. The reference-driven realism goal remains active: the plant materials, wood junctions, illumination and optical detail still fall short of the reference. The fern assets are artistic ornamental stand-ins, not species-accurate aquatic specimens. Glass reflections, surface ripples and caustics are approximations; the tank is not a fluid simulation. The LED area light supplements a shadow-casting spotlight; the area light itself does not cast shadows. Hardscape avoidance uses conservative spherical envelopes, and small leaves are not individual collision obstacles. The fish behavior is illustrative, not a biological research model. The detailed scene needs WebGL and will be more demanding on older mobile GPUs.

## Realism iteration checks

The scan files are bundled locally; their download MD5s were checked against the source manifest. GLTF loading completes before the loading overlay is removed. Front and side views were inspected with the new meshes. All 27 fish/tank tests still pass. The development build exposes FPS, synchronous renderer time, rendered triangle count and fish positions as data attributes on the scene element for inspection; those diagnostics are absent from the production build. Carpet blades use 24 triangles, slender stem leaves 48, red leaves 64 and rounded leaves 80 to retain their different silhouettes.

The planting pass adds uneven colonies at different depths, curved stems, forked growing tips and varying leaf-node spacing. `PlantCurrent` deforms each plant around its root, keeping leaves, stems and cast shadows attached. Thin-leaf backlighting uses the point/spot/directional lights' attenuated and shadowed irradiance. `EpiphyteMoss` samples the scanned triangle surfaces for attached patches of modeled moss. Surface ripples now displace the water mesh and distort the scene reflection according to the bent reflection ray, including ripples near the filter return. A rectangular light follows the physical LED strip and dims with evening mode.

The revised scene was visually checked at desktop and 720×1280 portrait sizes, including the front and side presets, feeding, pause and evening mode. The in-app preview measured approximately 60 fps on the development machine; this is not a performance guarantee for other devices. Background Chrome was throttled to approximately 1 fps and is unsuitable for motion assessment in that state.

The optics pass adds filtered, mipmapped reflection captures to suppress glittering at grazing angles. Water displacement fades to a fixed contact line at the glass. `AquariumGlass` models thicker clear panes, green edges and live internal side-wall reflections that fade with viewing angle. `ReflectionPool` prevents recursive scene capture; secondary mirrors use their last complete image. `WaterDepth` computes the viewing ray's distance inside the water and applies subtle RGB absorption, replacing most of the previous room-wide haze. Glass transmission samples the already-shaded scene, so it is excluded from that second attenuation step. These effects approximate aquarium optics rather than tracing every refracted light path.

The optics build passed all 29 tests, including two reflection recursion/recovery checks. Front, three-quarter and side views were inspected; camera reflections continue updating while the fish are paused. The preview remained near 60 fps on this machine with no browser warnings or errors. In development, `?inspect=reflection` displays the actual water reflection buffer for visual diagnosis; the inspection view is unavailable in production.

The leaf/substrate pass orients each upper leaf surface toward the light, mixes rounded and slender blades, reduces their specular highlight strength and adds green lower growth beneath warmer new leaves. Low rosettes soften exposed lower stems. `Substrate` uses a shared irregular channel boundary for sand and carpeting, world-scaled grain textures, 12,000 modeled quartz grains and 2,700 darker soil granules, replacing the smooth strip and oversized scattered pebbles. `ScannedBranch` retains substantial wood bodies while tapering the ends into finer tips. Its collision envelopes follow the actual deformed vertices; a test checks all six branches against the bundled scan and confirms that shaping does not mutate the shared source mesh. All 30 tests pass.

The contact-lighting pass renders scene color and depth together into a multisampled floating-point buffer. `AquariumLighting` reconstructs normals from that depth for a restrained, denoised GTAO pass, so real plant deformation and alpha-tested fern outlines remain aligned. Glass and water retain `depthWrite=false`; no override material converts them into opaque walls. The final output pass applies exposure, ACES tone mapping and sRGB conversion once. This is screen-space occlusion, not full indirect illumination: off-screen geometry and reflected views cannot contribute, and the subtle result is composited over transparent highlights too. Development-only `?inspect=contact` and `?inspect=unshaded` views support comparison.

A shaded studio floor replaces the unlit black slab. A broad room light, soft stationary cabinet-footprint approximation, and distant fade create a pool of illumination without a sharp horizon. The footer has stronger contrast over the lit floor. Front and side lighting, evening mode and the actual occlusion buffer were checked visually; the preview remained near 60 fps on this machine. All 30 behavior/geometry tests pass. The realism goal remains active; repeated planting silhouettes, wood joins, and optical detail still differ visibly from the reference.

The stem-morphology pass replaces the shared triangular mature-leaf profile with full-size lower blades and compact growing tips. Opposite leaf pairs have individually varied inclinations and node rotation. Most stems now carry a lateral shoot, with occasional second forks at different heights; the paths remain rooted and use the existing shared current deformation. Broad Ludwigia-like red leaves join the slender red and rounded green types, with greener mature growth below warm new tips. Every species has three modeled blade variants with different curvature, asymmetry and cupping, and normals are computed from each actual geometry. Front, three-quarter and side views were compared; an overly flat initial leaf-angle trial was corrected before publication. This remains an artistic planting composition, with further photographic fidelity work outstanding.

The hardscape pass adds a restrained swelling collar and vertex-colored crevice shade to the bases of scanned branches, with darker bark and retained UV detail. Stone uses a less saturated mineral albedo and lower interface reflectance to restrain white underwater highlights. `ScannedRock` orients each scan before sizing and grounding it; the old order could bury or lift a tilted base. The main stone presents a more upright, fractured face. Collision spheres now enclose actual oriented scan vertices instead of shrinking a box-derived sphere. A new test checks all nine placements for finite geometry, grounding, containment inside the glass, full vertex coverage and an unchanged source scan. All 31 tests pass. The local `/rock-study.html` page compares the six bundled scans for authoring; it is not an entry in the public build. No new assets were downloaded or purchased.
The left sword rosettes sit farther behind the main stone, and shorter, sparser rock moss leaves more scanned surface exposed. Wood moss retains its previous density.

The water-ray pass attaches depth textures to both real scene-reflection captures. Each surface fragment follows its reflected wave direction through captured depth (28 bounded steps with four refinement steps), then samples the color where that ray meets visible geometry. This brings plant colors into the underside ripples instead of merely nudging a flat reflection image. The matrices are copied from the actual reflection camera after its oblique clip-plane modification; depth reconstruction uses that final inverse projection. Missed rays fall back to a restrained planar sample. Slightly stronger shallow waves retain fixed glass contact edges. The method remains limited to geometry visible in the capture and is not full ray tracing or a fluid simulation.

The reflection capture, front and three-quarter views were inspected during development. All 31 existing behavior/geometry/recursion tests pass; the shader effect is verified visually rather than by those unit tests. The preview remained around 60 fps on the development machine, with increased synchronous rendering time around 8 ms. No new dependencies, downloads or paid services are used.
The visible LED emission now dims with the evening transition, matching the light intensity and its reflection. Paused side-view reflections continue responding to camera movement.

The blade-lighting pass gives small modeled leaves a smoother silhouette and continuous petiole transition, with gentler cupping, varied tip droop and torsion. The tissue map retains veins without multiplying the entire planting by an overly dark base; red new growth shifts from muddy orange toward rose. Shader normals now follow both the local flutter and the rooted current bend, including the attenuation near glass, so moving leaves and stems carry their lighting with them. These remain modeled artistic leaves rather than scanned aquatic specimens.

Front, three-quarter, side and 720x1280 evening views were inspected with no browser warnings or errors. The development preview measured around 58-60 fps and 8.3 ms synchronous render submission in the three-quarter view on this machine. Smoother blades increase geometry cost, so this is not a mobile performance guarantee. The production build and all 31 existing behavior/geometry tests pass; visual fidelity and GPU shading were checked in the browser, not proved by the unit tests. No fish behavior, controls, original photographic assets, dependencies or paid services changed.

The clear-glass pass replaces the uniformly bright green rails with rounded pane edges and narrow, translucent silicone joints. Broad pane faces composite the physical reflected light with a Fresnel-weighted alpha, preserving the sharp scene and water reflections underneath. This is a thin-glass approximation: flat faces do not refract the background. Polished edge faces retain tinted physical transmission for a sense of material thickness. The pane shader is excluded from water-distance attenuation so its reflected light is not tinted as submerged scenery.

The studio softbox moved above the viewing axis after inspection traced an unwanted middle-of-tank reflection to the former low light.
