# Joust cabinet: sources and scope

The third cabinet reuses the existing site's **retro Joust** engine, original decoded sprites and original sound recordings from `games/2026-07-05/joust/retro/assets/`. The games-page installation remains unchanged.

- `src/joust/{data,sprites,engine,render}.js` are vendored copies with their UMD wrappers converted to ES modules. The simulation and renderer remain the existing implementation. `JoustShow.ts` supplies an autonomous pilot, fixed-rate clock, wave transitions, automatic restart, HUD, and exhibit pause/power integration. It does not load the interactive game's menus or submit leaderboard scores.
- All **23 sound samples** in `public/joust/audio` are byte-identical to the existing Joust samples. The manifest records their SHA-256 hashes and source path. The existing audio module identifies these as rendered Williams **VSNDRM4** sound-board effects. `JoustSound.ts` uses those files directly, including alternating flap/run effects, with cabinet positioning and master mute downstream. No synthesized fallback or title music is used.
- The original Williams marquee is reproduced from [the archived marquee artwork](https://wall.alphacoders.com/big.php?i=652516), downloaded from `https://images6.alphacoders.com/652/thumb-1920-652516.jpg`. Williams attribution is retained in the image.
- The yellow/blue control arrangement follows [the surviving upright photographed by Coin Op Stuff](https://www.coinopstuff.com/items/1982-williams-joust-upright-arcade-machine-5910/), particularly `joust_04.jpg`: two horizontal movement sticks, two flap buttons, and two central red start buttons. Side and panel artwork are code-drawn interpretations, not factory scans. The second player's controls remain idle during the one-player autonomous demo and are available for close inspection.
- Board labels identify the reconstructed Joust CPU/video, ROM, input and VSNDRM4 sound assemblies. [Williams documentation](https://www.robotron-2084.co.uk/williams/documents/joust) is linked from the service manual. Physical placement, cabinet dimensions, monitor fitment and wire routing are approximate; this is not fabrication CAD or cycle-accurate hardware emulation.

All three machines keep independent simulations. Selecting a cabinet closes the previous inspection, moves the cabinets to separate foreground/left/right positions, and routes sound only from the selected cabinet. Hero, Exploded, X-Ray, Watch, service access, mechanical demonstrations, chip notes, reduced cabinet motion and mobile controls remain shared.

Validation: `node --test tests/*.test.mjs`, TypeScript/build checks, and browser review of all three machines across desktop/mobile modes, rotation, sound, selection, swapping, service panels and chip close-ups. Joust tests cover six minutes of unattended combat/egg collection, progression, pause/power, two-way control construction and original sample identity.
