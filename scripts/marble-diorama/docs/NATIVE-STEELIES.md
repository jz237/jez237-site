# Recovered black-marble controller

This is local native-board work. The public campaign still uses the earlier
proximity-pursuit reconstruction. Full enemy and campaign parity is not complete.

## Original evidence

The course header's +0x18 resource feeds the original marble-sized enemy system
at global 0x19cd0. It is separate from the scripted scenery/obstacle catalog.
Its two Beginner entries, one Aerial entry and one Ultimate entry correspond to
the black marbles seen in the Amiga recordings. It uses the marble movement and
drawing routines, with the non-player drawing mode at 0x1ecde.

Each eight-byte entry contains a camera interval, route pointer and region.
Routes contain six-byte nodes: column, row and four neighboring node indices.
Spawns sit at cell centers: column*8+4, row*8+4, with height sampled from terrain.

| Guard | Starting cell | Region | Load interval | Unload interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner upper | 40, 35 | 1 | 0–31 | 0–15 |
| Beginner lower | 57, 69 | 7 | 18–37 | 18–37 |
| Aerial | 67, 53 | 4 | 15–32 | 15–32 |
| Ultimate | 79, 83 | 9 | 19–45 | 19–45 |

The first Beginner guard has a special 16-band reduction in its unload boundary
at 0x1987c. Using its load interval for both operations would miss this rule.

Relevant original routines:

- 0x197d2: camera loading, cell-center spawn and state initialization.
- 0x195f4: contact timer and update dispatch.
- 0x19cd8–0x1a134: patrol, direct pursuit, route pursuit, return and target choice.
- 0x19a94 / 0x19bc8: nearest neighbor / nearest route node.
- 0x13122: desired velocity and one-thirty-second steering response.
- 0x14d28 / 0x14e7e: five-unit planar speed limit.
- 0x14b20, table 0x201a: speed-dependent neutral-ground rolling resistance.

Original binary resources, route extracts and diagnostic fixtures remain private.

## Restored behavior

The optional `nativeSteelie` enemy definition selects a graph and region.
It requires native dynamics at the same update rate as the native camera.
The older Beginner/Ultimate fixtures still used ordinary gravity; their guards
could skid out of turns because available ground traction was too low. Updating
these fixtures to the previously recovered native gravity fixes that mismatch.
Camera events activate/deactivate both the visible sphere and its rigid body.
Loading starts the guard at its source-derived spawn, with zero velocity.

Patrol follows each node's first link. Route pursuit chooses the neighbor
closest to the eligible player using the original approximate distance metric
and first-match tie order. Direct pursuit starts within 40 source units when
the guard is grounded. Eligible targets share its region; inactive players,
falling players and excluded original animation states are rejected. With two
eligible players, greater column+row progress wins; player one wins ties.

Leaving direct pursuit first returns to the nearest graph node. Patrol, route
pursuit and direct pursuit select desired speeds 7, 8 and 12 source units per
update. Approaching within four units reduces the stored desired speed to one.
The separate planar clamp limits actual source velocity to approximately five
units, retaining the original fixed-point rounding.

Initial contact starts a 150-update timer once. Later contacts do not restart
it. Expiry returns pursuit to the graph. Regions 7 and 9 retain direct pursuit
after expiry; other guards return to patrol. Contact is generated by an actual
Rapier manifold; proximity cannot trigger this transition.

The physical adaptation applies steering and recovered rolling resistance as
torque. It preserves the sphere's actual rotation and collision momentum and
does not snap it onto route points. An early longer patrol check exposed missing
rolling resistance: ordinary contact friction alone allowed overshoot and a
fall. Restoring the original resistance table corrected that failure.

## Validation and remaining scope

- Ten regressions cover graph choices, target eligibility/ties, mode changes,
  timer expiry, special camera boundaries, fixed-point steering, rolling drag,
  import validation, real rolling/contact and deterministic paired restoration.
- An independent Python transcription of the recovered branches agrees on
  10,000 state/steering observations across all four original graphs. This is
  source-branch comparison, not execution of the original game.
- The recovered Aerial fixture loads the guard at band 15, simulation tick 402.
  A local player/guard encounter exercises pursuit, physical contact and return
  with zero player falls. This is not a complete race.
- A separate 7,200-tick (60-second) unattended Aerial patrol visits all six
  patrol nodes, remains active and supported, has no player contact and no falls.
- All four guards also pass 7,200-tick isolated patrol diagnostics and visit
  every node on their respective first-link patrol loops. These diagnostics
  inject a camera load boundary and disable the racing player; they verify
  sustained guard/terrain behavior, not ordinary player approaches.
- Local browser inspection checks the black sphere, physical encounter and
  absence of console warnings/errors.

The full suite passes 293/293 tests (283605.0042 ms). The final import-validation
requirement for matching native dynamics was then verified with all 13 native
steelie/dynamics tests (341.366 ms). Build and diff checks pass.

Open: original collision impulses, airborne-state details, defeat thresholds
and paired reward attribution, other surface-specific resistance modes, exact
update cadence/difficulty calibration and complete normal-input course runs.
Rapier integrates continuous rolling at 120 Hz; the original applies its
discrete resistance after position integration. The torque response is a
physical adaptation, not an assertion of identical trajectories. Existing
last-contact defeat awards remain a reconstruction. Native boards still need
the other enemy families, geometry polish and full release acceptance.
