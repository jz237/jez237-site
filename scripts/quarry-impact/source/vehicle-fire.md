# Vehicle fire, smoke and sound

This presentation increment builds on `f21a7502d202555920aaeb6981c1148f05d94d00`. Shared physics, collision, health, scoring and all 23 deployed Worker inputs remain unchanged. No internet multiplayer test or server deployment is included.

Original Three.js code in `src/vehicle-fire.ts` integrates a turbulent density field through sorted instanced billboards. A deterministic 64-cubed R8 noise texture is generated locally; no downloaded or AI-generated visual media is used. Density attenuation, sun-facing shading, hot soot, flame temperature colors, airflow, buoyancy and two nearby point lights give the puffs depth. Quality settings use nine, seven or five volume steps. There are at most 640 volume particles and two fire lights, in addition to the existing 1,800 spark/dust particles and 36 loose parts. The pool is reused, and repair/restart clears it.

Smoke starts below 48% condition and flames below 24%. The cosmetic lifecycle advances at 60Hz. A blow of at least 14 damage that disables a car can trigger one brief fuel burst per repair; repeated disabled contacts cannot retrigger it. Actual hit severity is retained independently of remaining health. It neither applies a new physical impulse nor causes extra damage. Emission follows each car's engine-bay transform, including rollovers, while emitted smoke travels in world space. Cars freeze for the five-second wreck inspection; fire, embers and sound continue. Focus loss and explicit pause freeze effects and suspend audio. Manual inspection freezes the scene.

Three new original ElevenLabs effects are bundled locally: `fire-roar`, `fire-crackle` and `vehicle-burst`. Their prompts, duration/loop settings, source and runtime hashes, and processing are recorded in `public/audio/manifest.json`. `tools/fire-audio.py` is an explicit offline generation tool, never invoked by gameplay or the build. It refuses to generate unless both overage flags are false and at least 5,000 included credits remain; successful files are cached and uncertain requests are not automatically retried. The three requests used **170 included credits**, with no additional spending. The original 35 recordings are preserved unchanged.

Fire loops use a 180ms rotated seam crossfade, mono 44.1kHz Vorbis and -23 LUFS normalization. Burst attack is preserved, with a 5ms leading and 100ms trailing fade. Fire sound sources track the engine bay and route through the existing effects volume, distance attenuation, master mute and compressor. Burst/debris layers share the 18-shot cap; loops start once per event, and modest per-car playback differences reduce repetition. Restart stops outstanding shots and old loop nodes.

ElevenLabs endpoint/settings reference: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-sound-effects/convert . Generated audio contains no intended music or speech. No credentials or account usage snapshots are shipped.

Reproduction: `npm ci`, `npm run build`, the selected solo tests, `tools/vehicle-fire-qa.mjs`, `tools/wreck-hold-qa.mjs` and `tools/fire-stress-qa.mjs`. Give QA tools fresh output paths. `source/fire-revision.json` and compressed prior sources preserve earlier immutable regression fixtures. Runtime effects are volumetric billboard approximations rather than CFD fire simulation; they do not cast volumetric shadows, thermally deform structures or model real fuel systems. Overall photorealism remains an unfinished goal.
