ADSORPTION AT AN AIR–WATER INTERFACE

The surface matters.

Some surface-active molecules associate with a bubble’s interface. The microscope shows a simplified amphiphilic molecule: the water-avoiding tail faces the air and the water-compatible head stays toward the water.

Water-avoiding tailWater-compatible head

Small bubbles offer more interface.

For equal total air volume, smaller bubbles provide more total surface area. Contact time, foam stability and the water’s composition also matter.

Drain water. Lift foam.

The rising foam loses liquid as it travels through the neck. When it crosses the lip, the foam and collected material enter the cup.

Wet versus dry.

A wetter setting can produce more, paler liquid. A drier setting can produce less, darker skimmate. Cup darkness alone cannot establish which removed more organic mass.

Part of the system.

This exhibit represents marine equipment. Specialized freshwater foam fractionators also exist. A skimmer does not remove every dissolved substance or replace the aquarium’s biological treatment.

The bubble and molecules are enlarged independently. Colors identify teaching roles. This is a conceptual adsorption diagram, not a molecular dynamics simulation.

OPERATION PAUSED / SERVICE STUDY

Keep the interface working.

Cup and neck

Empty collected liquid before the cup overfills. Clean the cup and neck according to the actual manufacturer’s instructions.

Air path

Inspect the airline, injection fitting and silencer for restrictions or salt deposits. Reduced air delivery can occur while water circulation appears normal.

Pump and rotor

Disconnect power before dismantling real equipment. Inspect the needle wheel and use only the manufacturer’s approved cleaning procedure.

Stable installation

Maintain the specified sump depth and keep the return unobstructed. After adjustments or cleaning, observe the response before making further changes.

The exposed rotor is a teaching cutaway, not an operating procedure. The model assumes open ventilation and contains no ozone system.
