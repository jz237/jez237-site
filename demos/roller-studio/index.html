PHYSICAL SEPARATION, THEN EXPORT

Fibers make the difference.

Nonwoven, not a net.

Fleece contains many overlapping fibers. A particle may be intercepted on the surface or within the depth of this network. The enlarged view shows the principle; fiber spacing and particle sizes are illustrative.

Resistance is the signal.

As solids accumulate, the web offers greater resistance. With water still entering, the level inside rises. A sensor can use that rise to request clean fleece, without measuring each particle.

Remove it from the water.

Captured material is wound onto the used roll above the water. Physical export reduces the time retained debris spends decomposing in the circulating system.

It is one treatment stage.

A fleece roller does not remove every dissolved substance or replace biological filtration. It traps suspended solids. Skimming, biological treatment and chemical media serve different purposes.

OPERATION PAUSED / SERVICE STUDY

A roll is consumable.

Replace and re-thread.

Remove the spent fleece, fit the correct replacement and follow the actual unit’s threading and setup instructions. Confirm that the strip travels straight and that the drive is engaged.

Check the cause of high water.

Dirty fleece, a jam, installation depth and high sump water can all affect operation. Do not assume every high-water condition needs more advancing.

Inspect sensing surfaces.

Keep the level-sensing areas clean and correctly positioned according to the manufacturer. Inspect the inlet hose, bypass and outlet paths for obstruction.

Service the real unit safely.

Follow the manufacturer’s electrical and maintenance instructions before accessing the drive. The exposed gears are an educational cutaway. Our paused-flow roll-change animation is simplified; the ReefMat is designed to allow fleece replacement without removing the whole unit or stopping the aquarium pumps.
