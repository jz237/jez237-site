How the body comes apart.

Anatomy Studio takes the open Z-Anatomy model, 2,270 named pieces across ten systems, and separates it the way an engineering exploded view separates a machine: the skeleton stays put as the axis, and every other system moves outward by layer. Seventeen structures can be opened on their own. The studio opens in X-ray, with the skeleton solid and every other system translucent; switch to Skin (an opaque body with pores and a soft subsurface sheen, glossy corneas, fibrous irises and hair, showing only the surface until the body starts to open), Realistic, Organs & vessels (the realistic finish with the skin, muscles, fascia, skeleton, ligaments and nerves hidden, leaving the viscera, heart, brain, lymphoid organs and blood vessels; the external genitalia count as skin and are hidden too), Colour-coded or Clay in the Finish panel.

Ten systems, one frame

Every piece belongs to one asset file, loaded in three stages. The core stage (skeleton, ligaments and discs, viscera, heart, brain and sense organs) loads first; the muscles follow; vessels, nerves, lymphatics and skin regions load last. Phones stop after the core stage and offer a button for the rest.

The exploded view

The slider moves every piece along a fixed vector, so any position between assembled and fully separated is a legitimate view, and reassembly is exact. Each system has its own rule so the separation reads anatomically rather than as a random scatter.

Skeleton stays as the axis

Bones, discs and ligaments move only a few centimetres. Everything else is measured outward from the trunk axis, or from the axis of the limb the piece belongs to.

Muscles by depth

Each muscle moves radially away from its axis; superficial muscles travel farther than deep ones, so the layers open like a dissection. Limb muscles also move away from the trunk.

Viscera forward, brain up

Organs of the thorax and abdomen move forward and slightly down; the heart sits in front of the lungs. The brain lifts out of the skull; the eyes come forward and outward.

Vessels, nerves, lymph, skin

Arteries move laterally and forward, veins laterally and back, nerves back and outward, lymphatics forward. Skin regions travel farthest, as a translucent outer shell. Over the last part of the slider a gentle fan de-clumps the small pieces.

Seventeen nested studies

Each study hides everything except its members and replaces the whole-body vectors with its own. Labels appear once the structure is open, and “Label every piece” names all of them. If the studio is in X-ray, a study renders in the realistic finish and returns to X-ray when you leave it. The eye, hand, knee, shoulder and foot studies have a Left / Right toggle (also ?side=R in the link). Press the same button, Esc or Return to the body to reassemble.

Inspecting pieces

Click any piece, or search the list by name, system, region or hierarchy. The inspector shows the Z-Anatomy description (from Wikipedia, trimmed), the hierarchy path, the bounding size, the triangle count and, for merged pieces, the structures that were folded in. Isolate frames the piece alone; the all-parts board shows every loaded piece individually scaled on one non-overlapping grid.

Descriptions are matched by name with fallbacks (dropping side, qualifier or the “of …” clause, then the parent structure). About 89 % of pieces match a specific article; the rest show their parent's or system's text. Treat all text as teaching material, not clinical guidance.

How it was built

The skin is the exception to the one-piece-one-mesh rule. Each Z-Anatomy surface region is a closed thin shell: an outer sheet, an inner sheet a few millimetres beneath it and a rim joining their borders, and neighbouring shells stop short of each other. The build keeps only the outer sheets (the sheets that can see open air along their normals), bridges the gaps between neighbours, fills pinholes, slits and the open finger and toe tips, welds everything into one surface, subdivides it (once for the base tier, twice for the full tier) and splits the pieces apart again carrying shared smooth normals. That is what keeps the face and torso continuous instead of showing a ridge at every regional border.

Z-Anatomy ships one FBX file per system, exported from Blender with a naming convention: .l/.r sides, .g group empties for the hierarchy, and small placeholder meshes for labels. A headless Blender 4.5 script imports each file, drops the placeholders and muscle-attachment overlays, joins small structures into their parent, decimates each system to a triangle budget, centres each piece on its bounds and exports one GLB per system with the anatomical metadata as glTF extras. A gltf-transform step welds, quantises and meshopt-compresses the files and writes the manifest. Total: about 4.7 million triangles in 35 MB for the base tier, plus a 9 million-triangle zoom tier streamed per system, with vessels and nerves kept individual to the seventh and sixth levels of their trees. Rendering adds ground-truth ambient occlusion on desktop and procedural surface detail (bone grain, muscle fibres, organ vasculature, skin pores) generated in the browser. A second, undecimated tier of the same pieces (8.4 million triangles, 55 MB across ten files) streams in per system whenever a piece grows large on screen, so the closer you zoom, the finer every piece becomes; the Zoom detail toggle controls it.

The runtime is plain ES modules on three.js 0.180 with no build step, the same engine family as the Model S Studio. A Playwright suite checks the piece counts, explosion and exact reassembly, each nested study, the board layout, the mobile layout and reduced motion.
