    About this map

    Reading the board

    Topology

    The gateway sits at the center of the board. The internet uplink beacon hangs above it, and each district — Office, Wireless, Media, IoT — fans out around the core. Solid traces are ethernet; dashed traces are Wi-Fi.

    Traffic

    Particles ride the traces in the direction data flows, and a trace glows brighter as it carries more. Colors mark what kind of traffic it is — streaming, browsing, gaming, backups, IoT chatter, calls.

    Depth

    Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan. Hover a device for a quick chip; click it to open the inspector with its address, live rate sparkline, and connections — then click a connection to go one level deeper.

    Data

    Traffic here is a time-of-day-aware simulation seeded per device (the real LAN isn't reachable from a static page). Topology and behavior live in network/data.js, and traffic-sim.js documents an adapter for wiring in a real exporter later.

    How real is this?

    The topology is real — devices, districts, and connections come from an actual scan of the network. The traffic is simulated: each device follows a behavior profile (a TV streams in the evening, the NAS backs up overnight, IoT gadgets chirp all day) driven by the real time of day, but the numbers are generated in your browser, not measured. Use the timeline at the bottom of the map to preview any hour.

    A live version exists, but it's owner-only by design: real-time rates would reveal when the house is occupied or asleep, so the public site's security policy blocks all outside data sources. The owner can run the map locally against a small LAN exporter (see network/exporter/) — the badge in the corner switches from SIMULATED to LIVE when that's active.
