A computed film for Rush · A Show of Hands (1989)

A radar station on the DEW Line, a snowcat crossing the ice toward it, and a sky that listens. The aurora follows Geddy Lee’s voice and the synthesizers. Neil Peart’s kick drum sends rings out from every station and his snare sets off the lightning. Alex Lifeson’s solo runs as a filament of light down a tunnel whose walls are the recording’s own spectrum. When the song ends, the crowd’s applause glitters on the snow at sunrise.

In the corner, a ghost plays the drum part on a double-bass kit. Every stroke of the sticks and every kick of the two pedals was transcribed from the isolated drum track, so each one lands on its drum or cymbal at the moment you hear it. The kit never sits still: it assembles itself in the intro, turns, tumbles, flies apart into an exploded view, and twice leaves the floor to circle in orbit while the sticks keep playing.

Nothing here is filmed or generated from pictures. The recording was separated into drums, bass, voice, guitar and keyboards, and every frame was computed from those parts.
