Freshwater / Practical guide

Trim stem plants above leaves and replant healthy shoots

Keep useful cuttings, thin crowded growth and renew weakened stems instead of repeatedly shortening the same dense patch.

The Aquarium Journal · 2 min read

On a suitable stem plant, cut the longest shoot just above a lower leaf and replant the cut shoot in the same group; the rooted stem left behind can produce new shoots. Trim in small sessions. Different growth forms need different methods, so one severe haircut for every plant in the tank won't suit them all.1

Decide whether the patch needs shortening or renewal

Look below the tips. Repeated trimming encourages branching, but a dense group can leave its lower stems shaded and weak, and shaving the top again won't fix that. Thin out selected stems. Where a stem's lower part has deteriorated, remove it, discard the weakened base and keep the sound upper portion for replanting.2

Plan where the cuttings go

Pick the gap you want to fill before you cut. We suggest a small tray beside the aquarium, with shoots to keep on one side and discards on the other. It makes it easier to finish one group deliberately and avoids an unplanned heap of cuttings.

Put kept shoots where the group is less dense; an already crowded center doesn't need them. For a sloping group, set shorter cuttings toward the front and longer ones behind. Replanting lets you rebuild the shape, so stems don't have to go back where they were.2

A species example: Bacopa caroliniana

You can propagate this Bacopa by taking a side shoot and planting it in the substrate. Tropica describes it as relatively slow-growing for a stem plant, a reminder to judge the individual plant before assuming a stem group needs frequent cutting.3

Record what you changed

Photograph the group from the same viewing position before and after. Note which patch you shortened, which you thinned and where the cuttings went. Next time, compare those areas specifically as well as the overall silhouette. Shortening, thinning and replanting are different decisions, and a record keeps you from repeating one out of habit.

Groups don't need matching heights or matching dates on the calendar. Base the next session on the plants in front of you.
