Freshwater / Practical guide

Rinse and divide tissue-culture plants before planting

Remove the growing medium, then follow the species’ planting needs: hairgrass goes into substrate, while Anubias needs an exposed rhizome.

The Aquarium Journal · 2 min read

Tropica’s 1-2-Grow cups hold young plants meant to be divided into portions before they go into the aquarium.1

1. Decide where it belongs before opening

Read the species label first. Anubias barteri nana and Eleocharis pusilla 'Mini' both come in tissue-culture cups but go to different places: Tropica recommends stone or wood for the Anubias, and the Eleocharis is a foreground carpeting plant. The grower also rates the Eleocharis as medium in light and CO2 demand, against low for the Anubias.34

Dennerle Plants says to keep its Plantit cups sealed until you are ready to work. We'd also set out tweezers, a small work surface and the planting plan first, so you aren't hunting for tools with the plant already out of its container.2

2. Remove the growing medium gently

Ease the plant material out and rinse away the liquid or gel around it. Dennerle specifies lukewarm water and a gentle touch, and Tropica tells users to rinse the medium off under the tap. Follow your cup's own directions. Once the medium is gone, stop; the small plants don't need scrubbing.12

3. Divide to suit the planting

For small foreground plants, Tropica suggests six to eight portions planted with tweezers, though that number does not apply to every species. Its Eleocharis guidance calls for small portions spaced across the planting area so the carpet can grow in, rather than one solid plug.14

Anubias goes onto suitable stone or wood. Tropica warns against burying its rhizome (the thick stem the plant grows from) because it can rot. Follow the species information here even if the cup's general instructions show tweezers pushing plants into substrate.3

4. If a portion floats up

If a portion planted in substrate keeps floating up, Dennerle suggests checking whether it is too large and dividing it further. For a new setup, Dennerle recommends planting with little or no water in the tank, then filling slowly without disturbing the substrate. That is setup advice; don't drain an occupied tank to follow it.2

What the sealed cup promises

Tropica guarantees that its own 1-2-Grow range is free of snails, algae and pesticides. That is a manufacturer's statement, not an independent test of every tissue-culture product, and it says nothing about whether the aquarium stays algae-free after planting.1
