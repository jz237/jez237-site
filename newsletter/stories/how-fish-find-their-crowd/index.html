How fish find their crowd

When water gets murky, sticklebacks change how they move together. New research explores that social response.

Source published: September 2, 2026
Selected for this preview: November 1, 2026

University of Bristol researchers studied how groups of three-spined sticklebacks responded to food in clear and cloudy water. In murkier conditions the fish generally took longer to approach food, but they also changed their shoaling behavior.

Small groups in cloudy water found food faster when they stayed closer and aligned their movements. In larger groups in clear water, tighter coordination could instead slow the response. The effect depended on both the environment and the group.

What it means for a fishkeeper

Fish behavior can change with environmental conditions. This is not a recommendation to cloud aquarium water, increase stocking, or apply stickleback results to every aquarium species.
