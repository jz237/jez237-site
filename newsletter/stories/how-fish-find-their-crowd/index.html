Stickleback pairs in murky water approached food faster when coordinated

The advantage depended on the conditions: larger groups in clear water could respond more slowly when tightly coordinated.

The Aquarium Journal · 2 min read

When fish draw closer together with their bodies pointing the same way, it looks like better teamwork. A University of Bristol study published on September 2, 2026, found that whether that coordination helped depended on the water and the group. The subjects were three-spined sticklebacks, not a mixed community of aquarium fish.1

Before the food appeared

Bristol reports that in cloudy water the fish generally moved closer and aligned more strongly before food appeared, and small groups were especially responsive to nearby fish. Once the feeding opportunity arrived, though, cloudiness still slowed the response overall. Adjusting their behavior did not cancel out the effect of reduced visibility.1

The paper measured this from the fishes' positions and orientations in the second before the food stimulus appeared, and from changes in swimming speed between neighbors during the preceding minute. In pairs in turbid water, fish responded to a neighbor's speed changes with less delay than in clear water. That difference was not evident in larger groups.2

When tighter groups did better

In small groups in turbid water, closer spacing and stronger alignment were associated with quicker attacks on the food stimulus. In larger groups in clear water, the same features were associated with slower responses. The study treats them separately: cohesion is how tightly the fish gather, polarization how similarly they orient, and a casual glance would bundle both together as schooling.2

The reversal is what we find most interesting, since the neatest formation was not always the most effective. It is a reason to watch your own fish more carefully. Clouding a home aquarium or adding fish to reproduce a laboratory result would misread it, and the experiment does not establish a stocking rule or a welfare score for aquarium species.2
