Match aquarium lighting to your plants’ needs

Choose light for the species you keep and where each one sits in the tank. A brighter fixture is not automatically a better match.

Aquarium News · November 1, 2026 · 3 min read
  • Plants
  • Equipment
Link: jez237.com/newsletter/stories/a-greener-kind-of-aquarium/

Before turning up the light, make a plant list. Do you want a few leafy accents on wood, an open foreground, or a carpet? Put each plant's requirements beside its name. The equipment decision becomes much easier to think about when you know what you want it to grow.

Check each plant's own rating

Two compact plants can ask quite different things of a tank. Tropica rates Anubias barteri nana as low in light demand, while its Eleocharis pusilla 'Mini' has a medium rating. Both can suit a small layout; they are not interchangeable lighting projects. The useful question is not whether a plant looks beginner-friendly, but what its individual care information says.12

Look past watts

Watts tell you about electricity use, not a directly comparable amount of light across different technologies. Tropica's lighting guide makes that distinction and offers lumen-per-liter bands as a starting point. It also says those recommendations grew out of experience with T5 fluorescent lighting. Treat them as a grower's guide, not a measurement of what reaches a particular leaf under your LED.3

For a closer look, an underwater PAR meter measures the light arriving at a planting position. Its reading is called photosynthetic photon flux density: how many photons arrive per unit of area and time in the conventional 400–700-nanometer range. Apogee's documentation explains why the sensor must also account for the immersion effect. Use a meter intended or correctly configured for underwater measurements; waterproofing a sensor alone is not enough.4

Before buying a fixture

  • Where will the demanding plants sit? Tropica's measurements show that distance below a lamp and position toward the corners can change the available light substantially. A specification for the fixture does not describe every planting position.
  • What else does the plant need? Tropica distinguishes plants that can manage without supplementary CO2 from more demanding choices. A brighter fixture does not remove the need to consider carbon and other nutrients.
  • Is the schedule deliberate? Tropica's growing-in guide recommends a shorter lighting period during a new planting's early weeks, then a longer one later. That is establishment guidance, not a single timetable for every established aquarium.356

Keep a lighting log

Our practical preference is to keep a small lighting log: plant names, fixture setting, on-and-off times, and a photograph from the same position. Write down what you change rather than relying on how bright the tank looked last weekend. If you are comparing settings, resist changing the whole arrangement at once. Give yourself a record you can actually interpret.

And leave room for a modest ambition. A few well-chosen plants around a piece of wood can be the finished design, not an unfinished version of a carpeted aquascape. Choose the aquarium you want to live with, then choose its light.
