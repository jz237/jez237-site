Match aquarium lighting to your plants’ needs

Choose light for the species you keep and where each one sits in the tank. A brighter fixture is not automatically a better match.

The Aquarium Journal · 3 min read

Before turning up the light, make a plant list: a few leafy accents on wood, an open foreground, or a carpet. Write each plant's requirements beside its name. Choosing equipment is much easier once you know what it has to grow.

Check each plant's own rating

Two compact plants can ask quite different things of a tank. Tropica rates Anubias barteri nana as low in light demand and its Eleocharis pusilla 'Mini' as medium. Both can suit a small layout, but they need different light. Go by each plant's care information, not by whether it looks beginner-friendly.12

Look past watts

Watts measure electricity use. They do not compare light output across different lamp technologies, a point Tropica's lighting guide makes before offering lumen-per-liter bands as a starting point. Tropica also says those bands grew out of experience with T5 fluorescent lighting, so treat them as a grower's guide. They do not measure what reaches a particular leaf under your LED.3

An underwater PAR meter measures the light arriving at a planting position. Its reading, photosynthetic photon flux density, counts the photons in the conventional 400 to 700 nanometer range that arrive per unit of area and time. Apogee's documentation explains that the sensor also has to account for the immersion effect, so waterproofing a sensor is not enough on its own. Use a meter intended or correctly configured for underwater measurements.4

Before buying a fixture

  • Where will the demanding plants sit? Tropica's measurements show that available light changes substantially with distance below the lamp and toward the corners, which no fixture specification can describe for every position.
  • What else does the plant need? Tropica separates plants that can manage without supplementary CO2 from more demanding ones. Under a brighter fixture you still have to plan for carbon and other nutrients.
  • Is the schedule deliberate? Tropica's growing-in guide recommends a shorter lighting period for a new planting's early weeks and a longer one later. That advice covers establishment; it is not a timetable for every established aquarium.356

Keep a lighting log

We like a small log: plant names, fixture setting, on and off times, and a photo taken from the same spot each time. Write down each change instead of relying on how bright the tank looked last weekend. When comparing settings, change one thing at a time so the record means something.

A modest plan is fine. A few well-chosen plants around a piece of wood can be a finished design in its own right. Choose the aquarium you want to live with, then light it.
