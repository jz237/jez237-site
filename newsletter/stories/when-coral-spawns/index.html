Growing the next generation of reefs

Why coral restoration researchers are looking beyond fragments to breeding and genetic diversity.

Source published: July 21, 2025
Selected for this preview: November 1, 2026

NOAA describes a restoration effort that combines coral breeding, rearing and new technologies to help Florida's reefs recover after severe bleaching. One focus is raising new generations from spawning events, including offspring of parents selected for heat tolerance.

Growing fragments can increase coral cover, but those fragments may be genetically identical. Sexual reproduction offers a route to new genetic combinations. Researchers are also investigating beneficial algae, bacteria and ways to improve young corals' survival.

What it means for a fishkeeper

This is a conservation research program, not a home-aquarium treatment or a promise of bleaching-proof corals. It explains why both propagation and genetic diversity matter.
