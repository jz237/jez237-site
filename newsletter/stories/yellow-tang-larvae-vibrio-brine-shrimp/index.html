Vibrio peaked in yellow tang larvae as brine shrimp joined their diet

A Hawaiʻi Pacific University team tracked one cohort of larvae for 76 days. Gut microbes shifted with each new food.

Aquarium News · November 1, 2026 · 1 min read
  • Fish
Link: jez237.com/newsletter/stories/yellow-tang-larvae-vibrio-brine-shrimp/

Hatcheries raising sought-after reef fish such as the yellow tang struggle to get enough larvae through to the juvenile stage. A study published in PeerJ on September 21, 2026, looked at one part of that early stage: the microbes in the gut. Researchers at Hawaiʻi Pacific University and its Oceanic Institute followed a single cohort in a recirculating system from hatching to 76 days, sampling the larvae, their food and the rearing water at eight points.12

The gut community changed with each stage of development, and its biggest swings lined up with changes in food. Bacteria of the genus Vibrio made up 97.2% of the gut sequences at 36 days and 94.2% at 55 days. Both peaks came when enriched Artemia brine shrimp were added to the diet.1

These are proportions from one cohort in one system. They show a timing pattern and do not establish a cause or any effect on the fish’s health. The authors say live foods may shape a young fish’s microbes and that the Vibrio species carried in feed need closer study.1
