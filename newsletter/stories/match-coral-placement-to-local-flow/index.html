Match coral placement to the coral, not one turnover target

Use species and grower guidance to choose a position. Pump output alone doesn't tell you how the water moves at that spot.

Aquarium News · November 1, 2026 · 2 min read
  • Coral
  • Equipment
Link: jez237.com/newsletter/stories/match-coral-placement-to-local-flow/

Choose a coral's position from its care requirements and the flow at that location, not a single tank-wide turnover target. EcoTech Marine identifies livestock, rockwork, aquarium size, pump placement and other flow sources as factors in choosing a pump mode. Our practical conclusion: a pump specification is a starting point, not a placement map.1

One grower, two corals, two placements

ORA describes its Duncanopsammia axifuga as an LPS coral and recommends moderate-to-strong flow, medium-to-high lighting and a middle-to-upper position. The page concerns ORA's particular cultured Duncan, including its tightly clustered growth form. It is useful starting information for that stock, not permission to assign every large-polyp coral the same place.2

For its Montipora setosa, an SPS coral, ORA instead lists low-to-moderate flow and bottom-to-middle placement, again with medium-to-high lighting. The grower describes tolerance of a range of conditions. Taken together, these examples show why “SPS means strongest flow” and “LPS means weakest flow” are inadequate purchasing instructions. Ask for the actual coral's care information rather than stopping at its broad hobby category.23

Keep a placement record

  1. Sketch the rockwork and pump positions. Note other flow sources and obstructions; EcoTech explicitly identifies these as relevant.
  2. Write the coral's identity and grower's flow and lighting guidance beside the proposed location. Keep separate entries for different corals.
  3. Save a photograph and the pump settings before making a change. Our preference is to document one adjustment at a time rather than rearrange everything together.1

Take the sketch to the supplier

Instead of asking only “Is this high flow?”, ask the supplier which coral the advice concerns and how its current position compares with yours. Bring your sketch. An answer tied to a named animal and an actual layout is more useful than an unexplained pump percentage.

Treat the record as an ongoing husbandry conversation, not proof that the first placement is correct. If the coral appears unwell, describe the observations and seek species-specific help rather than repeatedly increasing pump power. This guide deliberately supplies neither a universal turnover number nor a promise that one setting suits every colony.
