Match coral placement to the coral, not one turnover target

Use species and grower guidance to choose a position. Pump output alone doesn't tell you how the water moves at that spot.

The Aquarium Journal · 2 min read

Place a coral by its care requirements and the flow where it will sit, which a tank-wide turnover target can't tell you. EcoTech Marine lists livestock, rockwork, aquarium size, pump placement and other flow sources as factors in choosing a pump mode, which is why we treat a pump specification as only a starting point for placement.1

One grower, two corals, two placements

ORA describes its Duncanopsammia axifuga as an LPS coral and recommends moderate-to-strong flow, medium-to-high lighting and a middle-to-upper position. That page is about ORA's own cultured Duncan, with its tightly clustered growth form. It is useful starting information for that stock and says nothing about where every large-polyp coral belongs.2

For its Montipora setosa, an SPS coral, ORA lists low-to-moderate flow and bottom-to-middle placement, again with medium-to-high lighting, and describes it as tolerant of a range of conditions. The shorthand “SPS means strongest flow, LPS means weakest” gets both of these named stocks wrong. Ask for the actual coral's care information as well as its broad hobby category.23

Keep a placement record

  • Sketch the rockwork and pump positions, and mark other flow sources and obstructions. EcoTech identifies these as relevant.
  • Beside the proposed spot, write the coral's identity and the grower's flow and lighting guidance, with a separate entry for each coral.
  • Save a photo and the pump settings before any change. We prefer to make and record one adjustment at a time.1

Take the sketch to the supplier

Ask which coral the advice is about and how its current position compares with the spot you have in mind. An answer tied to a named animal and a real layout helps more than an unexplained pump percentage.

The record doesn't prove the first placement was right; keep adding to it. If the coral looks unwell, write down what you see and get species-specific help instead of turning the pump up again. This guide gives no universal turnover number and makes no promise that one setting suits every colony.
