Pulsar — The Sweeping Lighthouse

A source-backed illustrated explanation of pulsar beam geometry, tilted magnetic and rotation axes, and the observer-dependent lighthouse effect.

Research behind the infographic

  • A pulsar is a rapidly rotating magnetized neutron star. [1] [2]
  • A neutron star is the remnant core of a massive star after an explosion. [1]
  • Pulsar emission includes radio waves; beams emerge in the directions of magnetic poles. [1] [2] [3]
  • The magnetic axis can be offset from the rotation axis, causing beams to sweep through space as the star rotates. [3]
  • A beam crossing Earth makes the pulsar appear to switch on and off like a lighthouse; regular observed pulses reflect rotation. [1] [2]
  • Strong magnetic fields and associated electric fields accelerate particles that emit radiation. [2]
  • The displayed curved field lines and opposing beams are schematic representations of magnetic geometry, not visible solid structures. [2] [3]
  • A schematic observer signal has a peak when a beam crosses the sightline and a low interval when it points away; geometry controls whether the observer sees pulses. [1] [2]
