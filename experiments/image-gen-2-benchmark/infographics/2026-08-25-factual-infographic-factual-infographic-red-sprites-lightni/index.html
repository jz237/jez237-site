Red Sprites — Lightning Above the Storm

A source-verified direct-canvas infographic showing how some powerful lightning events can trigger streamer-like red sprites high above thunderstorms, with their altitude, morphology, duration, and observation methods.

Research behind the infographic

  • Red sprites are short-lived red electrical discharges above thunderstorms and are connected to lightning. [1]
  • Sprites typically begin roughly 70 to 80 kilometers above thunderstorms and can develop downward over tens of kilometers. [1] [3]
  • Sprites can show long vertical tendrils, jellyfish-like forms, and grouped events. [1]
  • Sprites last only milliseconds and are difficult to see from the ground because the parent thunderstorm often blocks the view. [1]
  • A conventional sprite explanation involves brief thunderstorm electric fields after some positive cloud-to-ground lightning strokes producing streamer-like air breakdown in the middle atmosphere. [2]
  • Sprites are upper-atmosphere events distinct in location and form from the ordinary lightning occurring in or below the thunderstorm. [1] [2]
  • Sprites have been recorded from the International Space Station, aircraft, spacecraft, and distant ground viewpoints with low-light or high-speed cameras. [1] [3]
  • Airborne sprite research has used high-speed imaging and slitless spectroscopy to study spatial, temporal, and spectral structure. [3]
  • A useful scale comparison places the thunderstorm in the troposphere and the sprite high above it in the stratospheric and mesospheric region. [1] [2]
  • Not every lightning flash produces a sprite; the infographic must present the parent-lightning relationship as conditional rather than universal. [1] [2]
