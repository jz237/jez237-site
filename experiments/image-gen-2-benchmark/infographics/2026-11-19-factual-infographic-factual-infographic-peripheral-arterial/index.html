Peripheral Arterial Disease — Leg Blood Flow

Anatomical atlas of lower-limb PAD, claudication, assessment, management and urgent ischemic warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Peripheral arterial disease (PAD) commonly involves plaque buildup narrowing arteries and reducing blood flow to the legs and feet. [1] [2]
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart; show a schematic leg arterial supply and a magnified plaque-narrowed lumen with reduced downstream flow, not a vein or a clot as the usual cause. [1] [2]
  • Intermittent claudication is leg muscle pain with walking that eases after a few minutes of rest; the calves are commonly affected. Some people have no symptoms. [1] [2]
  • Other symptoms can include cold feet, weakness or numbness and sores that do not heal. [2]
  • Evaluation includes clinical assessment and comparing blood pressure at the ankle and arm with an ankle-brachial index. [2]
  • Management may include an exercise programme guided by a clinician, stopping smoking, prescribed medicines and, in selected cases, procedures to improve blood flow. [2]
  • Control blood pressure and cholesterol; people with diabetes should manage blood sugar and look after their feet. [2]
  • PAD is associated with higher heart attack and stroke risk. [1] [2]
  • Acute limb ischemia is an emergency: a foot paler or colder than the other, inability to feel or move the foot, or rest pain/pins and needles requires immediate medical help; call 911. [3]
