Bocydium globulare — The Globe-Bearing Helmet

Adult anatomy, developmental context, leaf-feeding habitat, and unresolved function of the globe-bearing treehopper's elaborate pronotum.

Research behind the infographic

  • Bocydium globulare is a taxonomically recognized species of the treehopper genus Bocydium in the insect family Membracidae. [1]
  • Adult Bocydium are 4.6-7.5 mm long and have a small dark, delicate body beneath the enlarged pronotum. [1]
  • The Bocydium pronotum bears slender suprahumeral horns with inflated bulbs and a simple spine-like posterior process arising from a central pronotal stalk. [1]
  • Bocydium globulare is distinguished in the adult key by a wide separation between its lateral pronotal globes relative to the humeral angles. [1]
  • The pronotal surface is punctate, each pit bears a long narrow seta, and the surface between pits is finely tuberculate. [1]
  • The head has unstalked compound eyes and ocelli, while the legs are ordinary walking legs and the hind tibia carries rows of cucullate setae. [1]
  • Bocydium globulare has been recorded on Miconia and feeding from the undersides of leaves a few feet above the ground. [1]
  • The genus Bocydium is recorded across tropical parts of Central and South America, including Costa Rica, Trinidad, Venezuela, the Guianas, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Brazil, and Paraguay. [1]
  • The biological function of the globe-bearing pronotal structure remains unknown; proposed resemblance to seeds or spiny fruit is a hypothesis, not an established mechanism. [1]
  • In treehoppers, the pronotum is the dorsal plate of the first thoracic segment and can become enlarged into elaborate species-specific forms. [2]
  • Treehopper pronotal shape begins differentiating during nymphal development, and the final molt is associated with a major expansion of the pronotum and wings. [2]
  • Treehoppers develop through nymphal instars before the adult stage rather than through a pupal stage. [2]
