Diabolical Ironclad Beetle — Jigsaw Armor

An insect-first direct-canvas infographic explaining how the flightless diabolical ironclad beetle uses fused layered elytra, a jigsaw-shaped medial seam, frictional microtrichia, and progressive delamination to resist crushing.

Research behind the infographic

  • The diabolical ironclad beetle is the real species Phloeodes diabolicus. [3]
  • The adult is flightless and its forewing-derived elytra have evolved into a solid protective shield. [1] [2]
  • Adults occur in western North America, including the U.S. Southwest and Sierra Nevada foothills, and can live beneath rocks or squeezed between tree bark and trunk. [1] [3]
  • The beetle feeds on fungi associated with tree bark. [3]
  • A supported adult scale is about one inch long. [3]
  • The elytra consist of chitin layers in a protein matrix. [1]
  • The two elytral halves meet at a medial suture shaped like interlocking jigsaw-puzzle pieces. [1] [2]
  • Under compression, layered material inside the interlocking blades delaminates progressively instead of failing through a sudden catastrophic neck fracture. [1]
  • Rodlike microtrichia on the blade surfaces are interpreted as frictional pads that resist slipping. [1]
  • X-ray microtomography at the Advanced Light Source visualized the internal architecture and interlocking regions during compression. [1] [2]
  • Compression testing found that the beetle can withstand a force of about 39,000 times its body weight. [1] [2]
  • Researchers built beetle-inspired composite joints to test the engineering value of the geometry and lamination. [1]
  • A larval stage of Phloeodes diabolicus has been formally described, but this infographic does not assert an exact developmental duration or seasonal schedule. [4]
