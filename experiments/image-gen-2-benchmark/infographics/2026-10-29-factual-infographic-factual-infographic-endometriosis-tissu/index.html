Endometriosis — Tissue Outside Its Usual Home

Source-backed pelvic anatomy, endometriosis lesions, variable symptoms, clinical evaluation, individualized care and general pelvic-pain warnings.

Research behind the infographic

  • Endometriosis is a chronic disease in which tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus, causing inflammation and scar tissue. [1] [2]
  • The endometrium lines the uterus; ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, bladder and bowel form pelvic anatomical context. Endometriosis lesions commonly occur on ovaries, fallopian tubes, behind the uterus and on bowel or bladder surfaces. [2]
  • The cause is unknown; no known method prevents endometriosis. [1]
  • Symptoms vary: severe period pain, persistent pelvic pain, painful sex, painful bowel movements or urination, and difficulty conceiving; some people have no symptoms. [1]
  • Lesion size and location do not reliably correspond to pain severity or location. [2]
  • Clinical history and imaging such as ultrasound or MRI help evaluation; laparoscopy may confirm a diagnosis but is not always required before treatment. [1]
  • Treatment is individualized to symptoms, preferences and pregnancy goals: pain medicines, hormonal treatment, selected surgery and fertility care may help. Hormonal methods may not suit people trying to conceive. [1]
  • There is no known cure; symptoms or lesions can recur after treatment. Multidisciplinary pain care including physiotherapy and emotional support can help. [1]
  • Pelvic pain has other possible causes and should not be self-diagnosed as endometriosis. Seek emergency care for severe or worsening pelvic pain, fainting or heavy vaginal bleeding; seek urgent assessment for pelvic pain with fever or possible pregnancy. [3]
