Somniphobia — Extreme Fear of Sleep

Artwork-led educational chart on Somniphobia, distinguishing fear of sleep from insomnia and qualifying PTSD-related mechanisms and treatment evidence.

Research behind the infographic

  • Somniphobia means extreme fear of sleep; even thinking of going to sleep may provoke extreme anxiety. [1]
  • Anticipatory anxiety means anxiety before the feared situation; thoughts of going to sleep can be sufficient to provoke fear. [1]
  • Phobia symptoms can include palpitations, chest tightness and trembling; responses vary between people. [2]
  • In PTSD-related fear of sleep, anticipated nightmares and inability to remain vigilant while asleep can contribute; this is not an explanation for every person. [3]
  • Hypervigilance means remaining on guard; fear of sleep is associated with greater hypervigilance in studied adults with PTSD and insomnia. [3]
  • Avoiding sleep-related reminders may occur in trauma-related fear; reliance on avoidance can make trauma symptoms worse and impede recovery. [3] [4]
  • Fear can be disproportionate to a threat; the intensity of a fear response is not itself evidence of current danger. Real sleep symptoms warrant individual assessment. [2] [3]
  • Talking therapies including CBT can help phobias; seek a health professional when symptoms affect daily life. [2]
  • CBT-I reduced fear of sleep in a trial of adults with both PTSD and insomnia; that finding is specific to that studied group, not a universal promise. [3]
  • Fear of sleep is not identical to insomnia; in the studied PTSD sample it related to nightmares and hypervigilance rather than classic insomnia symptoms. [3]
