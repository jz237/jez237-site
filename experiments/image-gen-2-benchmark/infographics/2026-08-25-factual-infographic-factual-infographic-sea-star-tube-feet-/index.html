Sea Star Tube Feet — A Hydraulic Walking System

A source-verified museum infographic explaining how a sea star's water vascular system, ampullae, soft tube feet, and reversible adhesive secretions coordinate underwater locomotion.

Research behind the infographic

  • Sea stars are echinoderms, and their adult bodies typically have a five-part radial plan around a central axis. [1] [2]
  • A sea star's water vascular system includes a central ring canal and radial canals extending along the arms. [1]
  • Rows of tube feet project along the oral underside of each arm and connect to the radial canals. [1]
  • Individual tube feet are controlled by bulb-like ampullae and extend or retract as fluid volume changes. [1]
  • The water vascular system provides hydrostatic force used in locomotion and prey manipulation. [1]
  • The madreporite lies on the aboral surface and connects through a stone canal to the ring canal. [1]
  • Tube feet also contribute to feeding, attachment, tactile sensing, and chemical sensing. [1]
  • Sea star tube feet use specialized adhesive cells for temporary attachment to submerged surfaces. [2]
  • Attachment and release use different secretions, enabling repeated rapid adhesion and detachment during movement. [2]
  • A sea star coordinates many tube feet to move across the seabed rather than walking on a small set of jointed legs. [1] [2]
