Iron-Deficiency Anemia — Oxygen Delivery Under Strain

Research behind the infographic

  • Iron deficiency can impair production of healthy red blood cells; low hemoglobin in anemia reduces oxygen carriage from lungs to other organs. [1] [2]
  • Iron-deficiency anemia can show small red blood cells with pale centers on a blood smear; low mean cellular volume is a common laboratory finding. [2]
  • Blood loss removes iron; heavy menstrual bleeding and gastrointestinal bleeding are causes requiring evaluation. [1] [2]
  • Low intake of iron-containing foods can contribute; celiac disease and stomach or intestinal surgery can impair iron absorption. [1] [2]
  • Mild or moderate iron-deficiency anemia may have no symptoms; more serious disease can cause fatigue, dizziness, pale skin, cold hands and feet, shortness of breath, or chest pain. [1]
  • Evaluation includes medical and diet history, a complete blood count with hemoglobin and cell size, ferritin, and other iron studies; further testing depends on suspected cause. [1] [2]
  • Low ferritin indicates reduced iron stores; iron deficiency can occur without anemia, so hemoglobin alone does not establish iron-store status. [2]
  • Treatment depends on cause and severity; clinician-guided oral iron is common, and intravenous iron may be used for poor absorption, intolerance, severe deficiency, or chronic blood loss. [1] [2]
  • Oral iron may cause upset stomach or constipation; significant symptoms should be discussed with the treating clinician. [1] [2]
  • Iron replacement must be paired with finding and treating the underlying cause; transfusion is reserved for selected severe cases and does not completely correct iron deficiency. [2]
  • Beans, tofu, lean red meat, fortified cereals, and dark leafy greens provide iron; vitamin-C-rich foods such as oranges can support absorption. [1]
  • Treating blood loss or absorption problems may help prevent iron-deficiency anemia; diet supports iron intake but is not a universal substitute for treatment. [1] [2]
  • Untreated iron-deficiency anemia may lead to heart problems, pregnancy complications, or developmental delays in children. [1]
