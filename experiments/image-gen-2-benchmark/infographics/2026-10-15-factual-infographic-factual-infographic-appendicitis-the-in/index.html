Appendicitis — The Inflamed Cul-de-sac

An artwork-led anatomical explanation of appendicitis, variable symptoms, urgent assessment and evidence-based treatment.

Research behind the infographic

  • The appendix is a small pouch attached to the bowel in the lower right abdomen; appendicitis means an infected, swollen appendix. [2]
  • Blockage by hardened stool or enlarged tissue in the appendix wall are possible causes; in many cases the cause is unclear. [1]
  • Abdominal pain may start around the belly button, move to the lower right, worsen over hours and worsen with movement or coughing. [1] [2]
  • Loss of appetite, nausea or vomiting and fever may occur; not everyone has typical symptoms or pain location. [1] [2]
  • Untreated appendicitis can lead to a burst appendix, peritonitis, abscess or sepsis; these are possible complications, not inevitable outcomes. [2]
  • Suspected appendicitis requires immediate medical assessment; severe abdominal pain, confusion or difficulty breathing require emergency care. [1] [2]
  • Diagnosis uses clinical assessment and may include blood and urine tests, ultrasound or CT; no single test always identifies appendicitis and other conditions can cause similar pain. [2]
  • Usual treatment is surgical removal of the appendix (appendectomy), with antibiotics; selected mild cases may be treated with antibiotics alone under medical supervision, but surgery remains standard care. [2] [3]
