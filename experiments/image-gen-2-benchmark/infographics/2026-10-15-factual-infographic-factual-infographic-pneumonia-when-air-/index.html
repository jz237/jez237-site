Pneumonia — When Air Sacs Fill

How pneumonia affects lung air sacs, its common causes and symptoms, clinical assessment, cause-specific care, risk reduction and emergency warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Pneumonia is an infection of one or both lungs; alveoli are air sacs that can fill with fluid or pus. [1]
  • Bacteria and viruses commonly cause pneumonia; fungi can also cause it. [1] [2]
  • Symptoms may include cough, fever, chills, shortness of breath, chest pain and tiredness; symptoms vary. [1] [2]
  • Clinical assessment may include a chest examination, chest X-ray and blood tests; symptoms alone do not establish the diagnosis. [1] [2]
  • Bacterial pneumonia is usually treated with antibiotics; treatment depends on cause and severity, so antibiotics are not a universal treatment. [1] [2]
  • Rest and fluids support recovery; severe illness may require hospital care and oxygen. [1] [2]
  • Severe trouble breathing, blue or pale lips or skin, sudden confusion, or coughing blood require emergency care. [2]
  • Worsening symptoms need prompt medical assessment. [1]
  • Recommended vaccines can prevent some infections that cause pneumonia; handwashing and not smoking help reduce risk. [1] [2]
  • Young children, older adults and people with weakened immunity or chronic lung disease have higher risk. [2] [3]
