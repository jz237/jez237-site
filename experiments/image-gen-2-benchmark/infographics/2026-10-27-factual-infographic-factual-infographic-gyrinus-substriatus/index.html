Gyrinus substriatus — Eyes Above and Below

Whirligig split-eye vision, swimming adaptations and freshwater life history, with qualified generalized internal anatomy.

Research behind the infographic

  • Gyrinus substriatus is a whirligig beetle, with a black body, yellow legs and adult length 5–7.5 mm. [2]
  • The pronotum has a lateral lip and inner groove; mid and hind tarsal claws are not darkened; the elytral rim is approximately parallel along its length. [2]
  • Gyrinus substriatus occurs in pools and ponds and is common and widespread in Britain. [2]
  • Whirligig beetle eyes are divided: the upper parts see above water while the lower parts see below water simultaneously. [1]
  • Whirligig beetles have short thick antennae, long grasping forelegs and short flattened paddle-like middle and hind legs. [1]
  • Whirligig beetles whirl at the surface, often in groups, seeking dead or injured insects, and dive when disturbed. [1]
  • Whirligig larvae are aquatic predators of aquatic invertebrates, have abdominal gills, and leave the water to pupate in a cocoon above the waterline. [1]
  • Both larvae and adults of Gyrinus substriatus are predatory; this species pupates on land in a cocoon of plant matter and sand grains. [2]
  • The internal digestive study is explicitly a generalized insect schematic, not a species dissection: mouth to foregut to midgut to hindgut to anus is the direction of food passage. [3]
  • In the generalized insect digestive system the midgut is a principal site of digestion and nutrient absorption; the hindgut recovers water and salts. [3]
