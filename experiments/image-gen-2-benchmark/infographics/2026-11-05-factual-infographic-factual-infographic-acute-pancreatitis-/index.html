Acute Pancreatitis — An Inflamed Pancreas

Source-backed anatomy, symptoms, causes, urgent warning signs and hospital management of acute pancreatitis.

Research behind the infographic

  • The pancreas lies behind the stomach near the duodenum; it makes insulin and digestive enzymes that normally help digest food in the intestine. [1]
  • Acute pancreatitis is sudden inflammation of the pancreas; digestive enzymes can damage pancreatic tissue. [1]
  • Gallstones can block the opening of the pancreas; gallstones and heavy alcohol use are common causes, while some medicines and other conditions can also cause it. [2]
  • Symptoms include sudden persistent severe upper abdominal pain that may spread to the back, nausea, vomiting and fever. [2]
  • Sudden severe persistent abdominal pain needs urgent medical evaluation; severe pain spreading to the back, fast heartbeat or difficulty breathing warrants emergency care. [2]
  • Evaluation uses history, examination, blood tests and sometimes imaging such as CT. [2]
  • Hospital treatment includes monitoring, IV fluids and pain relief; treating the cause may include gallstone surgery or alcohol cessation support. Antibiotics are for infection, not a routine cure. [2]
  • Most people recover, but severe illness may cause tissue death or infection and can be life-threatening. [2]
  • Reducing or avoiding alcohol, stopping smoking and following clinician dietary advice may help prevent recurrence. [2]
