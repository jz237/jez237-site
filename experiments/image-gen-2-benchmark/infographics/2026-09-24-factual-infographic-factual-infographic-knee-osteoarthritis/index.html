Knee Osteoarthritis — The Joint Under Load

A whole-joint anatomical plate showing knee OA tissue changes, variable symptoms, risk associations, movement and clinician-guided care.

Research behind the infographic

  • The knee joint includes cartilage covering bone ends, bone, synovium, meniscus, and supporting ligaments and tendons; osteoarthritis can affect multiple joint tissues. [1]
  • Osteoarthritis-related tissue changes develop over time and may include cartilage damage and bony edge growths called osteophytes. [1]
  • Knee osteoarthritis may cause movement-related pain, stiffness after rest, swelling, a sense of instability, and reduced movement; severity varies by person. [1] [2]
  • Older age, previous joint injury or repetitive stress, obesity, and family history are associated with greater osteoarthritis risk; age alone does not make it inevitable. [2]
  • Joint pain can reduce activity; inactivity may weaken muscles, potentially adding stress on the joint. [1]
  • Physical activity can reduce arthritis pain and improve function; appropriately paced movement is part of self-management. [3]
  • For people with overweight or obesity, weight management can reduce arthritis-related pain and disability. [3]
  • A clinician can assess symptoms and discuss physical therapy, medicines, supportive devices, and, if other treatments have not worked, joint replacement surgery. [2] [3]
  • Osteoarthritis has no cure, but symptoms can be managed; educational diagrams are not a personal diagnosis. [2]
