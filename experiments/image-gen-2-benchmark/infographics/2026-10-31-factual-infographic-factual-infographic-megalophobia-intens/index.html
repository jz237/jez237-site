Megalophobia — Intense Fear of Large Objects

An artwork-led clinical explainer of megalophobia with a safe architectural setting and individualized professional treatment context.

Research behind the infographic

  • Megalophobia is an established name for intense fear of large objects, discussed clinically as a specific phobia rather than a separate named DSM diagnosis. [1]
  • Large buildings, statues and vehicles can trigger fear; thinking about large objects can also provoke anticipatory anxiety. [1]
  • Symptoms can include rapid heartbeat, shortness of breath, dizziness and an urge to escape; experiences vary. [1]
  • Phobic fear is out of proportion to the actual danger of the object or situation. [1]
  • Avoidance can provide short-term relief but prevents learning that fear can be managed and that feared outcomes often do not happen. [1]
  • Anxiety symptoms can interfere with daily routines and activities; seek professional assessment when fear limits daily life. [1] [2]
  • Cognitive behavioral therapy helps change responses to anxiety; gradual exposure therapy is tailored to the person by a therapist or psychologist. [1]
  • Exposure treatment can begin by talking about large objects, then pictures, then being near an object; the pace is gradual and individualized. [1]
