Green Orchid Bee — The Perfume Workshop

Research behind the infographic

  • The green orchid bee's accepted common and scientific names are green orchid bee and Euglossa dilemma, and it is an orchid bee in family Apidae and tribe Euglossini. [1]
  • Adult Euglossa dilemma are metallic green, roughly honey-bee sized, with dark transparent wings and a long tongue; males have brush-like front tarsi and enlarged hind tibiae. [1]
  • Male orchid bees collect fragrant volatile compounds from environmental sources including flowers, fungi, and decaying vegetation. [1] [2]
  • Males collect fragrance material with mop-like or brush-like structures on the front tarsi and transfer it into enlarged hind tibiae. [1]
  • A hole in each enlarged male hind tibia opens into a spongy internal compartment that stores collected fragrance compounds. [1]
  • Male orchid bees release the resulting perfume bouquet during wing-fanned courtship displays at perching sites. [1] [2]
  • Perfume chemistry and collection behavior can differ between closely related orchid-bee species and contribute to species-characteristic sexual signaling, but perfume should not be presented as a guaranteed mating outcome. [2]
  • Female Euglossa dilemma have pollen-carrying corbiculae on the hind legs rather than the male fragrance-storage tibiae. [1]
  • Female green orchid bees build resin nest cells in enclosed cavities and provision developing young with nectar and pollen. [1]
  • Euglossa dilemma belongs to a tropical New World orchid-bee lineage, occurs in Central America, and is established as a naturalized species in South Florida. [1] [2]
  • Orchid bees are important Neotropical pollinators, while Euglossa dilemma can collect fragrance from non-orchid sources and does not require a matching orchid to persist in naturalized Florida. [1] [2]
