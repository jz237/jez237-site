The Tilt-Window Trap — Compression in a Narrowing Gap

Explains tilt-window entrapment geometry, vascular and nerve compression, and the need for urgent veterinary attention.

Research behind the infographic

  • Bottom-hung windows tilt inward and create a narrowing side gap in which cats can become trapped. [1] [2]
  • Attempts to escape can cause a cat to slide downward in the window gap; entrapment may occur between the last rib and pelvis or involve a hindlimb. [2]
  • Compression of blood vessels reduces blood flow to muscles, nerves and spinal cord; direct nerve compression also contributes to injury. [1] [2]
  • Neurological injury may cause hindlimb weakness or paralysis. [1] [2]
  • Restoration of blood flow can cause reperfusion injury with further tissue damage and serious systemic complications. [1]
  • Window entrapment is potentially life-threatening and requires urgent veterinary attention. [1] [2]
