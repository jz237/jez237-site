Glass Frog Transparency — Hiding Blood in the Liver

A detailed scientific plate showing the optical obstacle posed by circulating red blood cells, their resting-state redistribution to the liver, the resulting transparency increase, and the photoacoustic method used to observe the shift.

Research behind the infographic

  • Transparency can function as camouflage by reducing light scattering and absorption through an animal's body. [1]
  • Red blood cells strongly attenuate light, making a blood-filled circulatory system a major obstacle to vertebrate transparency. [1]
  • While resting, glassfrogs remove about 89 percent of their red blood cells from circulation and pack them within the liver. [1] [2]
  • This red-blood-cell redistribution increases a resting glassfrog's transparency by roughly two- to threefold. [1]
  • Photoacoustic imaging was used to track red blood cells in living glassfrogs. [1]
  • Glassfrog transparency combines see-through tissues with an active mechanism that clears respiratory pigments from those tissues. [1] [2]
  • Glassfrogs can regulate the location, density, and packing of red blood cells without clotting, a capability relevant to hemodynamic and blood-clot research. [1]
