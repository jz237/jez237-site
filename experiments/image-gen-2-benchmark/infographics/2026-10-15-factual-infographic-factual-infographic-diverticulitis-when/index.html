Diverticulitis — When a Bowel Pouch Becomes Inflamed

An artwork-led anatomical guide to inflamed colon diverticula, the distinction from diverticulosis, symptoms, care and urgent warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Diverticula are pouches that push outward through weak spots in the colon wall, most often in the sigmoid colon. [1]
  • Diverticulosis means pouches are present; most people with diverticula do not develop symptoms or problems. [1] [3]
  • Diverticulitis means diverticula become inflamed; this is not an inevitable progression of diverticulosis. [1]
  • Symptoms can include severe constant abdominal pain, often in the lower left abdomen, and fever; the location is not universal. [3]
  • Assessment may include blood tests and CT imaging to clarify the diagnosis and distinguish other causes of symptoms. [3]
  • Uncomplicated diverticulitis may be managed at home under clinician guidance; treatment depends on severity and patient factors. [2]
  • Treatment may include clinician-selected pain relief and temporary dietary changes; antibiotics are not needed for every person. [2]
  • Severe disease or complications may need hospital treatment; abscess drainage or surgery can be necessary in selected cases. [1] [2] [3]
  • A colon-wall perforation or abscess is a possible complication, not the normal appearance of every case. [1] [3]
  • Worsening persistent pain, fever or vomiting that prevents keeping fluids down warrants prompt medical assessment. [3]
  • Severe abdominal pain with vomiting, a swollen abdomen or inability to pass stool or gas warrants emergency care. [3]
  • After the acute episode, clinician-advised fiber intake, physical activity and not smoking may lower future risk; no guaranteed prevention is claimed. [2]
  • This infographic provides general education, not a diagnosis or an individualized treatment plan. [2] [3]
