The Moving Pinna — A Sound-Collecting Ear

An artwork-led study of feline pinna mobility, sound collection and direction-dependent acoustic filtering, with a schematic hearing pathway and explicit limits on active-movement claims.

Research behind the infographic

  • The pinna is the external earflap; sound vibrations enter the ear canal, reach the eardrum, and are transmitted by small middle-ear bones to the inner ear. [1]
  • The cochlea receives vibrations and passes nervous-system signals toward the brain, enabling hearing. [1]
  • Cat pinnae can orient independently of head position; pinna movements accompany orienting to sounds and visual targets. [2]
  • The pinna, head and torso attenuate or amplify different sound frequencies depending on sound direction, creating direction-dependent spectral cues for localization. [2]
  • Pinna shape contributes to sound localization, but the precise functional contribution of active pinna movements is not fully resolved; do not imply perfect localization or a quantified improvement. [2]
  • A sound-evoked early pinna movement can precede the eye movement and is asymmetric, larger in the ear on the stimulus side, in the reported experimental conditions; the artwork depicts illustrative orientation, not a universal fixed response. [2]
