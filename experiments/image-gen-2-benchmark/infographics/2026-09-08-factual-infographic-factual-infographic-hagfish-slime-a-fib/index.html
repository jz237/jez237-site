Hagfish Slime — A Fiber Net in Seawater

A direct-canvas marine-biology infographic shows how coiled protein thread skeins and mucin vesicles deploy in seawater into a water-trapping defensive slime network.

Research behind the infographic

  • Hagfish release defensive slime exudate from rows of glands or pores along the sides of the body when provoked or attacked. [1] [2]
  • The exudate has two major functional components: coiled protein thread skeins and mucin-containing vesicles. [1] [2]
  • On contact with water, mucin vesicles swell, rupture, and release mucus. [2]
  • Agitation and mixing stretch mucin strands and help transmit forces that rapidly unravel the coiled thread skeins; adding water alone does not fully explain deployment. [1]
  • The hydrated mucus and unraveled protein-thread network together form a coherent fibrous hydrogel that traps seawater. [1] [2]
  • Hagfish slime functions in defense and can interfere with the mouth and gills of attacking fish. [1] [2]
  • A body-to-pore-to-gland-to-skein-and-vesicle scale sequence accurately represents where the stored slime components originate and how they enter seawater. [1] [2]
