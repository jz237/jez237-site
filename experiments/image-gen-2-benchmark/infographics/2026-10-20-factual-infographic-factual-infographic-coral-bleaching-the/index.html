Coral Bleaching — The Partnership Comes Apart

Artwork-led comparison of coral–algal symbiosis, heat stress, bleaching, and conditional recovery or mortality.

Research behind the infographic

  • Reef-building coral is an animal colony of tiny tentacled polyps that build and cover a hard skeleton. [2]
  • Microscopic symbiotic algae called zooxanthellae live within coral tissue; they provide much of its color and a major food source. [1] [2]
  • Algae use sunlight, carbon dioxide and water for photosynthesis; much of the food they produce feeds the coral, while coral provides a place to live. [2]
  • Heat stress can disrupt the symbiosis and cause loss of algae and/or their coloration; bleaching is not limited to warm-water stress. [1] [2]
  • Loss of algae and their pigments leaves white skeleton visible through nearly transparent living coral tissue. [2]
  • Bleached coral is not necessarily dead but has lost a major source of food and is vulnerable. [1] [2]
  • Recovery is possible if stress eases and algae return, but prolonged stress and algae loss can lead to death. [1] [2]
