How Nacre Turns Cracks Into Detours

A source-verified materials-science infographic showing how staggered aragonite platelets, thin organic interfaces, tortuous crack paths, bridging, friction, pull-out, and cooperative deformation work together across scales to make nacre damage-tolerant.

Research behind the infographic

  • Nacre is a hierarchical biological composite made mostly of aragonite platelets separated by a small organic matrix fraction. [1] [2]
  • The aragonite platelets form a staggered brick-and-mortar-like architecture with thin organic interfaces. [1] [2]
  • Crack deflection, crack blunting, and microcracking help create a tortuous crack path and reduce catastrophic failure. [1]
  • Organic material can bridge opening platelets and dissipate energy as cracks advance. [1]
  • Platelet pull-out and friction associated with interface nanoasperities contribute to nacre's resistance to fracture. [1] [2]
  • Cooperative deformation across co-oriented stacks of aragonite platelets adds a larger hierarchical scale to nacre toughening. [1]
  • Monolithic aragonite tends to form a more direct major crack under indentation, whereas nacre spreads damage and confines cracks near the loaded region. [1]
  • Nacre's toughness emerges from several cooperating mechanisms across scales rather than from one interface feature alone. [1] [2]
