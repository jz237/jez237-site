Autophobia — Intense Fear of Being Alone

Artwork-led educational chart about fear of solitude, distinct from loneliness, with general phobia symptoms and clinical help.

Research behind the infographic

  • Autophobia is an extreme fear of being alone; monophobia is another name. [1]
  • Fear can occur when alone, when anticipating being alone, or when worrying that people will leave. [1]
  • Autophobia differs from loneliness: loneliness concerns the quantity or quality of connections; autophobia involves anxiety about being alone. [1]
  • A phobic fear can occur without an immediate threat; being alone is not in itself proof of danger. This does not negate genuine hazards. [1]
  • Phobia symptoms can include palpitations, trembling, dizziness and severe worry; symptoms vary between people. [2]
  • Thinking about a feared situation can trigger anxiety or panic; avoidance can organise and limit daily life. [3]
  • CBT is an evidence-based talking treatment used for phobias; gradual exposure is used in treatment and should be planned individually, with professional help where needed. [2] [3]
  • Seek clinical support if fear interferes with daily life; educational illustrations are not a diagnosis. [2] [3]
  • Autophobia is described as a specific phobic disorder by Cleveland Clinic; the descriptive name is not itself a separate diagnostic code. [1]
