Epomis circumscriptus — The Larva That Lures Amphibians

A direct-canvas macro-entomology infographic shows how the much smaller Epomis circumscriptus larva reverses the expected predator-prey encounter by luring an amphibian, evading its tongue, and attaching with hooked mandibles.

Research behind the infographic

  • Epomis circumscriptus is a ground beetle in the family Carabidae, and the featured animal is its larval stage. [1] [2]
  • Epomis circumscriptus has three larval instars. [1] [2]
  • Later Epomis circumscriptus larvae can range from pale yellow-brown or whitish with black and orange markings to nearly black with orange markings; legs and terminal urogomphi are yellow, and dark mandibles have reddish tips. [2]
  • Epomis larvae and amphibians co-occur in moist habitat and can share shelters such as stones and wood debris. [1]
  • Unlike the more generalist adults, Epomis larvae are specialist predators of amphibians. [1]
  • The larva lures amphibians while remaining in place by waving its antennae and by making alternating sideways antenna-and-mandible movements. [1]
  • An amphibian may approach, pounce, and extend its tongue toward the moving larva as if the larva were prey. [1]
  • The larva can evade the tongue strike, counter toward the amphibian, and attach before beginning to feed. [1]
  • Specialized double-hooked mandibles help the larva attach firmly to amphibian skin. [1] [2]
  • The larva is much smaller than the amphibian it attacks, creating a predator-prey role reversal. [1]
  • At the end of each larval instar, an Epomis larva leaves the amphibian, molts in a concealed place, and then seeks another amphibian host. [1]
