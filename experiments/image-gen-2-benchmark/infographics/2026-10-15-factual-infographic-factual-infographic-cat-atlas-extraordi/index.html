Cat Atlas — Extraordinary Senses

A sensory atlas connecting the cat to its eye, ear, nose, whisker-follicle and balance mechanisms.

Research behind the infographic

  • Retinal rod photoreceptors and the reflective tapetum contribute to vision in dim light; this does not mean vision in total darkness. [1] [2]
  • The cornea and lens direct incoming light to the retina; the optic nerve carries visual information. [1]
  • The pupil changes size in response to light. [1]
  • Mobile pinnae help collect and locate sound; the ear canal, eardrum and cochlea have distinct roles in hearing. [3] [2]
  • Cats hear higher sound frequencies than humans. [2]
  • Smell provides information about food, places and other animals. [2]
  • Mechanical displacement of a whisker stimulates sensory receptors associated with the follicle, not nerves inside the hair shaft. [4]
  • The semicircular canals are inner-ear balance structures, separate from the cochlea used in hearing. [3]
  • Backward-facing keratin papillae on the tongue contribute to grooming; these visible spines are not taste buds. [5]
