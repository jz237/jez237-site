Yellow Tang — The Reef’s Golden Grazer

Yellow tang morphology, Pacific reef habitat, algal grazing, defensive caudal spine, day–night behavior and carefully labeled aquarium implications.

Research behind the infographic

  • Zebrasoma flavescens is the accepted marine species; Acanthurus flavescens is its synonym. [4] [5]
  • The yellow tang has a laterally compressed oval lemon-yellow body, large dorsal and anal fins, small downturned mouth and white defensive spine on each side of the tail base. [2] [1] [3]
  • Native subtropical Pacific range includes Hawaii and western Pacific islands toward Japan; reef habitat includes depths of 2–46 m. [1] [3]
  • Adults grow to about 15–20 cm (6–8 inches). [3]
  • Flexible comb-like teeth and a small downturned mouth graze algae from rocks and reef framework; algae grazing helps limit algal competition with coral. [2] [1]
  • Individuals may occur alone or in loose groups; juveniles can be territorial. [1] [3]
  • Daytime grazing gives way to solitary resting in reef crevices at night; night color is grayish-yellow with a white lateral band. [2] [1]
  • Tail-base blades are modified scales in slots, exposed through tail flexion, used in defense and competition, and can inflict deep wounds; avoid touching. [2]
  • Aquarium implications explicitly inferred from sourced biology, not a numeric husbandry prescription: plan algae-based feeding and reef-like night refuges; territorial behavior means wild grouping is not a guarantee of peaceful tankmates. Coral-associated grazing alone is not a universal reef-compatibility guarantee. [2] [1] [3]
