Palingenia longicauda — The River's Brief Swarm

A richly illustrated entomology plate centered on Palingenia longicauda, contrasting its three-year clay-bank larval development with its synchronized winged emergence lasting only a few hours.

Research behind the infographic

  • Palingenia longicauda is the long-tailed mayfly, an insect in the order Ephemeroptera and family Palingeniidae. [1]
  • Palingenia longicauda is a lowland river species historically associated with medium-to-large European rivers; documented surviving populations include the Tisza and Raba river systems in Hungary. [1]
  • Its larvae develop for three years almost exclusively in continuously eroding, steep outer riverbanks composed mostly of clay. [1]
  • After the long larval development, mass emergence as subimagos and imagos is highly synchronized and lasts only a few hours. [1]
  • The species can reach about 10 centimetres in total size and is known for spectacular synchronized reproductive swarms. [1]
  • Mayfly development has no pupal stage: an aquatic nymph gives rise to a winged subimago, and the subimago may molt into the adult imago. [2]
  • Adult mayflies have two pairs of wings, with hind wings smaller than forewings, and two or three terminal filaments depending on the taxon. [2]
  • Mayfly nymphs are aquatic, usually have three terminal filaments, prominent forewing pads, ten abdominal segments, and paired articulated gills on at most abdominal segments one through seven. [2]
  • Burrowing mayfly nymphs use digging-adapted legs and tusks in substrates that include clay, while their gills help move water through the burrow. [2]
  • Mayflies as a group transfer nutrients between aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems and provide food for many other animals. [2]
  • Reinforcing eroding riverbanks with riprap or concrete destroys the clay-bank larval habitat used by Palingenia longicauda. [1]
