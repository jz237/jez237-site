Fire Cloud Chimney — How a Pyrocumulonimbus Lofts Smoke

A source-verified museum-quality direct infographic explaining how intense wildfire heat and smoke can build from pyrocumulus into a deep pyrocumulonimbus and, in some events, inject smoke into the upper troposphere or lower stratosphere.

Research behind the infographic

  • A pyrocumulonimbus, or pyroCb, is a thunderstorm-like cloud produced when an intense fire lofts enough heat, smoke, and moisture to drive deep convection. [1] [2]
  • PyroCb development can grow from precursor pyrocumulus convection above a wildfire, but not every pyrocumulus becomes a large pyrocumulonimbus. [2]
  • A strong pyroCb acts as a vertical chimney that carries smoke through the troposphere and can inject it into the upper troposphere and lower stratosphere. [1] [2]
  • Smoke that reaches the stratosphere can spread over very large distances and remain aloft longer than smoke confined to the lower troposphere. [1]
  • PyroCb smoke injection is studied with aircraft remote sensing, in-situ instruments, ground-based sensors, satellites, and atmospheric models. [1] [2]
  • Researchers are investigating which fires produce pyroCbs, what controls direct stratospheric injection and plume magnitude, and how injected smoke changes upper-atmospheric composition and radiation. [2]
  • PyroCb occurrence and injection height depend on the coupled fire and atmospheric environment, so a large wildfire does not automatically produce a stratospheric smoke plume. [1] [2]
  • The visible cloud may show a dark smoke-fed lower column and a brighter ice-cloud tower or anvil above it. [1]
