The Tapetum — A Second Pass for Light

Normal feline tapetal reflection, a second retinal pass, and schematic ordered rodlet microstructure.

Research behind the infographic

  • The cornea admits light, the iris regulates entry through the pupil, and the lens focuses incoming light onto the retina; the optic nerve carries visual information toward the brain. [1]
  • The tapetum lucidum lies behind the retina and reflects light that was not absorbed on its first passage, providing a second opportunity for retinal absorption and improving sensitivity in dim light. [1]
  • The tapetum is a reflector of incoming light, not a light-producing organ; diagram arrows represent incident and reflected light rather than generated light. [1] [2]
  • Cat tapetal cells contain ordered groups of slender rodlets; multilayer reflecting domains account for reflection, with reflected wavelength depending on domain layer spacing. Schematic microscopic studies are not to scale. [2] [3]
  • A tapetal layer is part of the choroidal tissues behind the retina; schematic layers may be simplified and exaggerated for explanation, not represented as a full clinical histology section. [3] [1]
