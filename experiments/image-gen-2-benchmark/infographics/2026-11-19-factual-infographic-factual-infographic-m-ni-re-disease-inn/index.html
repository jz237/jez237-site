Ménière Disease — Inner Ear Episodes

Inner-ear anatomy, endolymphatic hydrops association, symptoms, evaluation, management and stroke warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • The inner-ear labyrinth contains the cochlea for hearing and semicircular canals and otolith organs, including utricle and saccule, for balance; the membranous labyrinth contains endolymph. [1]
  • Endolymph buildup (endolymphatic hydrops) is associated with disrupted hearing and balance signals in Ménière disease; its underlying cause remains unclear. [1]
  • Symptoms come and go: spinning vertigo, hearing loss, tinnitus, ear fullness, and sometimes nausea or vomiting; usually one ear is affected. [1] [2]
  • Diagnosis uses symptom history and hearing testing, with evaluation for other causes; there is no single definitive test. [1] [2]
  • There is no cure yet; clinician-directed medicines may help attack symptoms, hearing aids may help hearing loss, and vestibular rehabilitation may help persistent balance problems. [1] [2]
  • Discuss dietary salt reduction with a clinician; it may help some people, but is not a cure. [1] [2]
  • Do not drive during dizziness or impending vertigo. [2]
  • Vertigo with new facial droop, arm weakness or speech difficulty may be stroke: call emergency services immediately. [2]
