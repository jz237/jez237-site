Hangingfly — The Hunter That Never Stands

A source-backed anatomical and behavioral plate of the hangingfly Bittacus strigosus, centered on suspended predation and the tarsal grasping mechanism.

Research behind the infographic

  • Bittacus strigosus is an insect species in the order Mecoptera and family Bittacidae. [1] [2]
  • Bittacids have long legs and wings, superficially resembling crane flies, but possess a single raptorial tarsal claw. [2]
  • Adult Bittacus hangingflies have three pairs of similarly structured raptorial legs; the grasping apparatus is formed by the fourth and fifth tarsomeres. [3]
  • The grasping foot includes a strong modified pretarsal flexor system associated with the claw-closing action. [3]
  • Bittacus strigosus is diagnosed by clear wings with heavily margined crossveins, a yellow pterostigma, and dark-yellow to light-brown legs and abdomen. [2]
  • Bittacus species are predators that hang from plants rather than stand on the vegetation. [2]
  • In Ontario, Bittacus hangingflies occur in varied habitats and especially moist forests with patches of nettles; B. strigosus is the more common and widespread of the three Ontario species. [2]
  • The unusual visual mechanism is suspension from foliage combined with prey capture by specialized raptorial feet, not a mantis-like foreleg strike. [2] [3]
