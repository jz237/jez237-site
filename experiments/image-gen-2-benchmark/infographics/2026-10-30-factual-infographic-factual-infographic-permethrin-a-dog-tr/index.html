Permethrin — A Dog Treatment That Can Poison Cats

Concentrated dog-only permethrin spot-ons, feline contact exposure, neurological warning signs and urgent veterinary guidance.

Research behind the infographic

  • Concentrated permethrin spot-on flea treatments intended for dogs can cause severe, potentially fatal poisoning in cats; never apply a dog-only flea treatment to a cat, even part of a pipette. [1] [2]
  • Cats can be exposed through close contact with, or grooming, a recently permethrin-treated dog; keep cats away from treated dogs according to the product instructions. [1] [2]
  • Permethrin toxicity affects the feline nervous system and can cause drooling, wobbling, twitching, tremors and seizures. [1] [2]
  • Suspected exposure warrants immediate veterinary contact, even before signs develop; take the product packaging to the vet. [1] [2]
  • Check species suitability and follow the product label; the focus is concentrated dog-only spot-ons, not a claim that every flea product or every permethrin formulation has identical risk. [1] [2]
