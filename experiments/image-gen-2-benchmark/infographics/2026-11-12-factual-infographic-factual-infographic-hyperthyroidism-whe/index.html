Hyperthyroidism — When Thyroid Hormone Runs Too High

Anatomical educational atlas of thyroid overactivity, symptoms, causes, testing, individualized treatment and thyroid-storm warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • The thyroid is a small butterfly-shaped gland in the front of the neck; its hormones regulate energy use and affect nearly every organ. [1] [2]
  • Hyperthyroidism means the thyroid produces too much thyroid hormone, speeding many body functions. [1] [2]
  • Graves disease is an autoimmune cause that stimulates excess hormone production; overactive nodules are another cause. [1] [2]
  • Possible symptoms include heat intolerance, sweating, hand tremor, weight loss despite increased appetite, and rapid or irregular heartbeat; symptoms vary. [1] [2]
  • Diagnosis requires clinical assessment and thyroid blood tests, not symptoms alone; TSH, T4 and T3 help evaluate thyroid function. [1] [2] [3]
  • Treatment is individualized by cause, severity and circumstances; antithyroid drugs reduce production, beta blockers ease symptoms but do not stop hormone production, radioiodine or surgery are options for selected patients. [1] [2]
  • Radioiodine is not used during pregnancy or breastfeeding. [1] [2]
  • Untreated hyperthyroidism can cause heart rhythm problems and bone thinning. [1] [2]
  • Thyroid storm is a rare life-threatening emergency; high fever, pounding rapid heart and confusion in someone with hyperthyroidism warrant calling emergency services. [3]
