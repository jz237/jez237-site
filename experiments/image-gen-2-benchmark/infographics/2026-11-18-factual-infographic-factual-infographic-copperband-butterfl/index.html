Copperband Butterflyfish — The Crevice Forager

Photorealistic species atlas of adult anatomy, crevice feeding, habitat, pairing and cautious aquarium suitability.

Research behind the infographic

  • Chelmon rostratus is the accepted species; copperband butterflyfish and beaked coralfish are common names. [1] [2] [3]
  • Verified synonyms and spelling aliases are Chaelmo rostratus, Chaetodon enceladus, Chaetodon rostratus, Chelmo rostratus and Chelmon lol. [4]
  • Silvery-white body, three broad orange-copper body bars, narrower dark-margined orange eye bar, tall dorsal and anal fins, dorsal ocellus, dark caudal-peduncle bar with blue edging. [1] [2]
  • The adult long snout extracts benthic invertebrates from holes and crevices; it develops after larval settlement. [1] [2]
  • Maximum length is 20 cm (8 inches). [2]
  • Tropical Indo-West Pacific range; rocky shores, coral reefs, estuaries and silty inner reefs; preferred depth 1–25 m. [1] [2]
  • Encountered singly or in pairs; forms pairs during breeding. [2]
  • Difficult to keep in home aquaria; sometimes used for Aiptasia control. [2]
  • Aquarium caveat: its benthic-invertebrate diet and occasional Aiptasia use do not establish blanket reef safety; assess compatibility and feeding rather than promise pest control. This is a cautious husbandry implication of the documented diet, not a claim all individuals eat the same ornamental animals. [2]
