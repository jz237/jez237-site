Gout — Crystals in the Joint

Artwork-led big-toe joint cutaway and enlarged urate crystals, with symptoms, clinical evaluation, flare relief, prevention and joint-infection warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Gout is inflammatory arthritis; urate can accumulate and form needle-shaped crystals in joints, triggering painful inflammation. [1] [2]
  • Urate comes from purine breakdown in body tissues and foods; it normally leaves in urine. Too much production or too little removal can raise blood urate. Not everyone with high urate develops gout. [1]
  • Gout commonly affects the big toe joint but can affect other joints; a flare causes sudden severe pain, warmth and swelling, sometimes redness that may be harder to see on darker skin. [1] [2]
  • Genes, kidney disease, alcohol and some medicines can increase gout risk; gout is not simply a consequence of food choices. [1] [2]
  • First-time symptoms need medical evaluation. Blood urate testing and sometimes joint-fluid crystal testing help evaluate gout. [2]
  • Clinicians treat flares with anti-inflammatory medicines such as NSAIDs or steroids, selected for individual safety; rest, elevation and a wrapped cold pack can help. [2]
  • Urate-lowering medicine may be needed for recurrent attacks or high uric acid; take it regularly even when symptom-free. Healthy habits support prevention but do not replace needed medicines. [2]
  • A suddenly painful swollen joint with worsening pain, fever or chills, or feeling sick needs urgent assessment because joint infection is possible. [2]
