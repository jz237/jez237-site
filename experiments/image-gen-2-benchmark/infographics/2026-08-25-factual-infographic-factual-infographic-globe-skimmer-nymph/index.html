Globe Skimmer Nymph — The Folding Strike Mask

A direct-canvas macro entomology infographic showing how the aquatic globe skimmer nymph unfolds a jointed lower-lip mask, closes paired palpi around small prey, and retracts the catch.

Research behind the infographic

  • Pantala flavescens is commonly called the globe skimmer or wandering glider and has a nearly global, primarily circumtropical distribution. [2]
  • Globe skimmers require freshwater for reproduction and can develop as larvae in temporary rain pools before emerging as migratory adults. [2]
  • Pantala flavescens nymphs are aquatic predators that capture small aquatic prey with a rapidly moving modified labium. [1]
  • The extendable labium includes a postmentum, prementum, and paired labial palpi connected by articulation joints. [1]
  • During a strike, the jointed labium unfolds and protracts, the paired palpi open and then close around the prey, and the captured prey is retracted toward the mouth. [1]
  • High-speed videography documents rapid underwater protraction and retraction of the globe-skimmer nymph's labium. [1]
  • The palpi carry setae and sensilla; these are visible sensory structures, while their exact functions should not be overstated. [1]
  • Globe-skimmer nymphs have been collected from natural and artificial ponds, and mosquito larvae can be among the small aquatic prey they capture. [1]
  • The adult globe skimmer's enlarged hindwing base contributes to efficient gliding during long-distance migration. [2]
