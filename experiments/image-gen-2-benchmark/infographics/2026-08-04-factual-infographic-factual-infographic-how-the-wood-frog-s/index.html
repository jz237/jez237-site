How the Wood Frog Survives Whole-Body Freezing

A real-world non-fiction physiology infographic showing how wood frogs coordinate extracellular ice formation, liver-derived glucose, winter urea accumulation, cellular water balance, metabolic depression, and controlled thawing—and why tolerance varies among populations and conditions.

Archive source notes

Sources verified 2026-08-04: Costanzo et al., Cryoprotectants and Extreme Freeze Tolerance in a Subarctic Population of the Wood Frog, PLOS ONE 10(2): e0117234, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0117234, supports winter urea accrual, glucose mobilization from liver glycogen after freezing begins, organ water loss as ice forms, freeze-thaw enhancement of glucose delivery, and strong population differences. Costanzo et al., Hibernation physiology, freezing adaptation and extreme freeze tolerance in a northern population of the wood frog, Journal of Experimental Biology 216, https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.089342, supports the northern-population comparison and winter-conditioning context. Exact survival limits were deliberately not generalized.
