Hypothyroidism — A Slower Thyroid Signal

Thyroid anatomy and hormone deficiency, primary TSH signaling, common causes and symptoms, evaluation and evidence-based management with essential clinical cautions.

Research behind the infographic

  • Hypothyroidism means the thyroid does not make enough thyroid hormones to meet body needs. [1] [2]
  • The thyroid is a butterfly-shaped gland in the lower front of the neck; it releases thyroid hormones into the blood to reach body tissues. [1] [2]
  • Thyroid hormones help regulate energy use and warmth and support the brain, heart, muscles and other organs; low levels slow many body functions. [1] [2]
  • The pituitary releases TSH, which signals the thyroid to work. In primary thyroid failure TSH usually rises as hormone output falls; pituitary disease is an exception. [2]
  • Hashimoto autoimmune disease attacks the thyroid and is the most common cause of hypothyroidism; thyroid surgery, radiation and some medicines are other causes. [1] [2]
  • Possible symptoms include fatigue, feeling cold, dry skin, constipation and slowed heart rate; symptoms vary and are not diagnostic alone. [1] [2]
  • TSH and free T4 blood tests help diagnose hypothyroidism; history and examination help find the cause. [1] [2]
  • Levothyroxine replaces missing thyroid hormone, with clinician-guided dosing and follow-up blood tests, commonly 6 to 8 weeks after starting or changing a dose. [1] [2]
  • Do not stop thyroid medicine or change its dose without consulting the clinician. [1] [2]
  • Excess iodine, including kelp or iodine supplements, can worsen autoimmune thyroid disease; supplements are not a universal fix. [1]
  • Contact the clinician promptly if pregnant because thyroid hormone needs may change and untreated hypothyroidism can harm mother and baby. [1] [2]
  • Rare severe untreated hypothyroidism can progress to life-threatening myxedema coma, requiring immediate medical treatment. [1]
