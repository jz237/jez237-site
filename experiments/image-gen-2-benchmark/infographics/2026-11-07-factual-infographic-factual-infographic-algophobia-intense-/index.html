Algophobia — Intense Fear of Physical Pain

Source-backed fear-of-pain terminology, triggers, body alarm, fear-avoidance, and evidence-based clinical support.

Research behind the infographic

  • Algophobia is an established clinical term for extreme fear of physical pain; the term is descriptive, not a claim of a separate diagnostic code. [1]
  • Thinking about pain, anticipated movement, and reminders of painful experiences can trigger anxiety and avoidance. [1] [2]
  • Catastrophizing means imagining the worst outcome; hypervigilance means intensely monitoring for pain or threat. [1] [2]
  • Fear-avoidance can reduce activity and participation and may worsen disability and pain; the pattern varies between people. [1] [2]
  • Body alarm can include palpitations, sweating, trembling, and shortness of breath. [1] [3]
  • Actual pain and fear of pain both merit assessment; perceived threat is not a direct measure of tissue injury and fear does not mean pain is imaginary. [1] [2]
  • CBT can help change pain-related thoughts; individualized clinician-guided gradual return to suitable activities can help with fear of pain; treatment should address actual pain too. [1] [3]
  • Seek professional help when fear interferes with daily life; treatment should be planned with the healthcare team, not by forcing painful exposure. [1] [3]
