Geysers — The Boiling Feedback Engine

An artwork-led schematic of recharge, heating under pressure, steam bubbles, pressure-release boiling and eruption shutdown.

Research behind the infographic

  • Geysers are hot springs whose restricted underground plumbing limits free circulation. [1]
  • Surface water seeps underground and is heated by the volcanic geothermal system. [1] [2]
  • Pressure increases with depth; pressurized water can remain liquid above its surface boiling temperature. [1] [2]
  • In many studied geysers, bends or side chambers trap steam; released bubbles heat the overlying water column. [2]
  • Initial boiling and overflow reduce pressure, allowing more hot water to boil and steam to expand. [1] [2]
  • Expanding steam drives liquid water and vapor out of the vent. [1] [2]
  • The visible white plume is largely condensed water droplets, rather than visible pure water vapor. [2]
  • Eruptions end as the reservoir depletes or water cools; replenishment and heating permit another cycle. [1] [2]
  • Subsurface geometries vary; a schematic side-chamber cutaway is not a surveyed map of a particular geyser. [2]
  • Silica deposited from thermal water can build a cone around a geyser vent. [1]
