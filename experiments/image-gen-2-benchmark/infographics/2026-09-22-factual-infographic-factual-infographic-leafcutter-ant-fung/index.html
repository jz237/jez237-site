Leafcutter Ant Fungus Farm — From Green Leaf to Living Garden

A source-verified underground-farm cutaway showing plant harvest, substrate preparation, fungal inoculation and degradation, gongylidia food bodies, enzyme recycling through fecal droplets, bacterial contributions documented in a studied system, garden care, and separate refuse management.

Research behind the infographic

  • Leafcutter ants harvest fresh plant material and use it as substrate for a cultivated mutualistic fungus that is their primary food source. [1] [2]
  • Workers prepare incoming plant material by chewing it, applying fecal droplets, and inoculating it with fungal mycelium before incorporating it into the garden. [2]
  • The cultivated fungus Leucoagaricus gongylophorus is the primary degrader of plant material in the studied garden system. [1]
  • The cultivar forms swollen hyphal tips called gongylidia, clustered in staphylae, that provide food to larvae and most food to adult workers in the studied higher-attine system. [3]
  • Some fungal enzymes eaten with gongylidia remain active through the ant gut and are deposited in fecal fluid onto freshly prepared plant substrate. [3]
  • Resident bacteria participate in the garden ecosystem; in the PNNL study they transformed digested plant polymers into metabolites used as amino acids and vitamins that support fungal growth and degradation. [1]
  • Fungus gardens and refuse dumps are spatially and chemically distinct colony environments with distinct microbial communities. [2]
  • Refuse consists largely of partly degraded plant material removed from the productive garden, creating a plant-biomass degradation gradient across the colony system. [2]
  • Workers protect and maintain the cultivar while supplying nutrients and dispersing fungal material within the colony. [2]
