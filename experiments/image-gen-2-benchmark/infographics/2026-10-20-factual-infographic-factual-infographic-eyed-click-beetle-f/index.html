Eyed Click Beetle — False Eyes and a Loaded Hinge

Species-specific false eyes, scale patterns, range and larval ecology, with explicitly schematic family-level click-jump mechanics.

Research behind the infographic

  • Eyed click beetle is Alaus oculatus, family Elateridae, with elongated black adult body, white scale mottling, six legs, antennae and paired elytra. [1] [2]
  • Large white-ringed black eyespots sit on the prothorax behind the head; they are not eyes. The much smaller true eyes are on the head. [1] [2]
  • Minute scales form the mottled surface pattern. [1]
  • The species occurs in eastern United States and southern Ontario and Quebec, in wooded habitats associated with hardwoods. [1] [2]
  • Adults are about 1 to 1 3/4 inches long. [2]
  • Larvae are sclerotized, yellowish to dark brown, with nearly black head and dark terminal abdominal segments. The last dorsal abdominal segment has conical tubercles and paired forked teeth. [1]
  • Larvae prey on wood-boring beetle larvae in decaying hardwood, including rotting oak, cherry and apple stumps. [1]
  • Larval and adult forms are documented; no duration or intermediate morphology is claimed. [1]
  • General click-beetle mechanics: a thoracic hinge behind the head allows jumping without leg-powered takeoff. A peg holds against a lip while soft tissue deforms; release causes elastic recoil, rapid unbending and an audible click.
