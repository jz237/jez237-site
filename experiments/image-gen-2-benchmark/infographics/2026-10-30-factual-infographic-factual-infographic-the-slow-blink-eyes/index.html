The Slow Blink — Eyes in Social Context

A focused feline body-language atlas connecting slow-blink eyelid movements, experimental evidence and whole-body interaction context.

Research behind the infographic

  • Slow blink sequences typically involve half-blinks followed by prolonged eye narrowing or eye closure; illustrate eyelids moving rather than changing pupil size. [1]
  • In controlled experiments cats half-blinked and narrowed their eyes more following owner slow blinks than no interaction; cats were more likely to approach an unfamiliar experimenter after slow blinking than after a neutral expression. [1]
  • Results suggest slow blinking may be positive cat-human communication, not a guaranteed emotional translation or proof of affection in every instance. [1]
  • Interpret facial and body cues together: backward/flattened ears, tail lashing and dilated pupils can precede petting-induced aggression; stop petting when aggression signs appear, avoiding uninvited handling. [2]
  • A relaxed cat can have upright ears and a tail without flicking or raised hairs; facial and body posture supply context rather than one eye signal alone. [1] [2]
