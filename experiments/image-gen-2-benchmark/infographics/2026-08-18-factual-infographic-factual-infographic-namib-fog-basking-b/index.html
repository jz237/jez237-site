Namib Fog-Basking Beetle — Drinking the Wind

A source-verified insect-first direct-canvas infographic showing how the real Namib fog-basking darkling beetle climbs dune crests, faces fog-laden wind, tips head-down, collects droplets on its elytra, and drinks the steady runoff.

Research behind the infographic

  • Onymacris unguicularis is a Namib Desert darkling beetle in the family Tenebrionidae. [1] [2]
  • During fog, Onymacris unguicularis climbs to dune crests and positions itself in fog-laden wind. [1] [2]
  • The beetle adopts a characteristic head-down fog-basking stance. [1] [2]
  • Fog water collects on the beetle's dorsal surface and runs toward the mouth, where the beetle drinks it. [1] [2]
  • Experimental observations found a steady flow of water from the beetle's elytra rather than episodic cascades. [2]
  • A steady runoff can provide water during short fog-basking periods and lets the exposed beetle leave the dune ridge if disturbed. [2]
  • Fog is an important water source for organisms in the extremely arid Namib Desert. [1] [2]
  • Onymacris unguicularis can emerge from sand and fog-bask outside its usual daytime activity period during nocturnal fog. [1]
  • The evidence does not establish that the beetle is the most efficient possible fog collector or that every desert beetle uses this behavior. [1] [2]
