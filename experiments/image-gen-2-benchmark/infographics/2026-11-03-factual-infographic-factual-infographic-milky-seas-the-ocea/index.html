Milky Seas — The Ocean’s Steady Living Glow

Rare steady marine bioluminescence, bacterial hypothesis and satellite observation.

Research behind the infographic

  • Milky seas are rare expanses of steady whitish marine bioluminescence visible at night. [1] [2]
  • Luminous bacteria are implicated, but their precise ecological relationships and conditions producing milky seas remain uncertain. [1] [2]
  • At high bacterial population density, chemical quorum sensing can trigger bacterial luminescence. [1]
  • A 1985 sampled milky sea contained luminous bacteria associated with microalgae. [1] [2]
  • The glow may persist for days to weeks and drift with surface currents. [1] [2]
  • The VIIRS day-night band can detect the faint glow from orbit under favorable conditions; moonlight and clouds can complicate detection. [1] [2]
  • Unlike a milky sea’s steady glow, mechanically stimulated dinoflagellate bioluminescence typically flashes briefly. [1] [2]
