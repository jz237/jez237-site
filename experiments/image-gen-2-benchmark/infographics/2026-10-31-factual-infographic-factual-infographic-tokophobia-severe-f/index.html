Tokophobia — Severe Fear of Childbirth

A source-backed artistic explanation of tokophobia, its possible triggers and symptoms, avoidance and individualized maternity and mental-health support.

Research behind the infographic

  • Tokophobia is an established term for severe or phobic fear of childbirth, not ordinary uncertainty about birth. [1] [2]
  • Anxiety may occur even when someone wants a child; symptoms and experiences vary. [1]
  • Anticipatory anxiety can increase during pregnancy; thoughts about birth, negative birth accounts and memories of a traumatic previous birth may be associated with fear. [1]
  • Anxiety symptoms can include a rapid heartbeat, muscle tension, disturbed sleep and panic symptoms. [1]
  • Avoidance can involve not talking or thinking about birth and avoiding antenatal education; isolation and feeling unsupported can also occur. [1]
  • Primary tokophobia can occur without prior experience of birth; secondary tokophobia can follow a difficult or traumatic birth. [1]
  • Fears may focus on harm to the parent or baby; maternity teams discuss birth options and develop plans that account for both physical and mental health. Fear alone does not measure individual obstetric risk. [1]
  • People with tokophobia should be offered discussion with a professional experienced in supporting this fear. [2]
  • Support can include speaking with a midwife or obstetrician, perinatal mental-health referral, psychological therapy such as CBT, and a collaborative birth plan including pain-relief and birth options. [1] [2]
  • Treatment is individualized to symptoms and the anxiety condition; education is not an individual diagnosis. [2] [3]
