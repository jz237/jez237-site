Cave Pearls — Stone Grown Around a Grain

An artwork-led cave-pool study of particle nuclei, carbonate deposition, concentric bands and radial calcite growth texture.

Research behind the infographic

  • Cave pearls are concentrically banded mineral concretions that form in shallow cave pools. [1]
  • Water dripping from above or slowly flowing into shallow pools supplies dissolved material for cave-pearl growth. [1]
  • Loss of carbon dioxide to cave air promotes carbonate precipitation around particles in a cave pool. [1]
  • A grain of sand can act as the nucleus of a cave pearl. [1] [2]
  • Successive mineral deposits form concentric bands around the nucleus. [1]
  • A cave-pearl cross section can show crystals arranged radially outward from a central nucleus; the illustrated calcite texture is a generalized schematic. [2]
  • Excess carbonate precipitate can coat the pool floor and build cups or nests around cave pearls. [1]
  • Cave pearls vary in shape; some are rounded while others are irregular. [1]
  • In the NPS explanation, roundness results from growth of the outer layer in all directions in supersaturated water, not polishing by rotation. [1]
  • Dripping water can agitate cave pearls; agitation is not equivalent to tumbling them smooth. [1]
