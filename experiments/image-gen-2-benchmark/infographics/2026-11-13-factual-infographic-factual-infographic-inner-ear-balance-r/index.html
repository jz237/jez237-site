Inner-Ear Balance — Rotation, Motion and Gravity

Normal feline vestibular sensory anatomy and reflex function; focused inner-ear mechanism, not a disease chart.

Research behind the infographic

  • The vestibular apparatus lies deep in the inner ear beside the cochlea and communicates with brainstem centers. [1] [2]
  • Three semicircular canals occupy different planes; each has an ampulla containing a crista with sensory hair cells. [1]
  • Semicircular canal receptors respond to rotational acceleration or deceleration. [1]
  • The utricle and saccule contain sensory maculae that respond to linear acceleration or deceleration and gravity. [1]
  • Vestibular information helps stabilize posture and the eyes during head movement through vestibulospinal and vestibulo-ocular reflexes. [1] [2]
  • The vestibular portion of cranial nerve VIII carries receptor information toward the brainstem. [1]
