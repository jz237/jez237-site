Pyrophobia — The Candle Behind Glass

Eight-panel fire-fear educational plate with a glass-partition candle hero, symptom map, anticipation and avoidance sequence, real safety comparison, CBT and support.

Research behind the infographic

  • Specific phobias involve intense fear out of proportion to actual danger; this plate applies that framework to fear of fire. [1]
  • Anticipating a feared object or situation can cause excessive worry; encountering it can cause immediate intense anxiety. [1]
  • People may actively avoid feared objects and situations or endure them with intense anxiety. [1]
  • Phobia symptoms can include palpitations, chest tightness, trembling, dizziness and nausea. [2]
  • Avoidance can restrict activities; exposure-based CBT helps people engage in activities and situations they have avoided. [1]
  • CBT teaches different ways of thinking, behaving and reacting; exposure therapy is particularly effective for phobias. [1]
  • Relaxation exercises may accompany exposure therapy; breathing exercises and talking to a trusted person may help manage symptoms. [1] [2]
  • Seek a primary care provider when symptoms affect life; a provider can refer to a qualified mental health professional. [1] [2]
  • Open flames are dangerous if candles tip over or are too close to combustible objects; ordinary fire precautions remain necessary. [3]
  • Consider battery-operated flameless candles; lit candles need stable holders, at least 12 inches from anything that burns, and should be extinguished when leaving the room or before bed. [3]
  • Use flashlights rather than candles during a power outage. [3]
