Ornithophobia — Feathers at the Courtyard Gate

Bird-focused specific phobia explained through a fictional courtyard encounter, eight substantive panels and four explanatory diagrams.

Research behind the infographic

  • Ornithophobia means phobia of birds and is an animal-type specific phobia. [2]
  • Specific phobias involve intense fear out of proportion to actual danger; avoidance and distress can interfere with life. [2] [3]
  • Encountering, anticipating, or thinking about a feared object can produce anxiety; pictures can also trigger fear in some people. Bird pictures and courtyard encounters are illustrative examples, not universal triggers. [2] [3]
  • People may overestimate danger and underestimate their ability to cope; uncertainty about a feared encounter may fuel anxious predictions. [3]
  • Possible physical symptoms include fast heartbeat, sweating, trembling, tense muscles, dizziness, chest tightness and nausea; symptoms vary between people. [1] [3]
  • Physical alarm prepares the body for possible danger, the fight-or-flight response; alarm does not itself prove the feared situation is dangerous. [3]
  • Avoidance can reduce anxiety quickly but prevents opportunities to revise feared predictions and can maintain the phobia. [2] [3]
  • Repeated avoidance can restrict everyday activities; skipping the illustrated courtyard is a fictional example of this impact. [1] [3]
  • A frightening event or learned responses may contribute to phobias, but a clear cause is not always identifiable. [1] [2] [3]
  • CBT examines unhelpful thoughts and habits; graded exposure uses small manageable steps, and therapist-assisted exposure can treat specific phobias. Steps are individually chosen, not a universal exposure prescription. [1] [2] [3]
  • Talking to someone trusted and practicing relaxing breathing may help; seek professional help if fear affects daily life. [1] [3]
  • The hero scene and supporting courtyard/picture vignettes are fictional educational illustrations, not a diagnosis or instructions to approach or handle birds. [2] [3]
