Yellow Watchman Goby — The Burrow’s Lookout

Photorealistic reef atlas of the yellow watchman goby, its alpheid-shrimp shared shelter, sandy habitat and cautious aquarium guidance.

Research behind the infographic

  • Accepted binomial Cryptocentrus cinctus (Herre, 1936), original synonym Smilogobius cinctus; marine goby of the eastern Indian Ocean and western Pacific. [2] [3]
  • Yellow watchman goby is also called yellow shrimp goby and banded prawn goby. Yellow coloration with fine pale blue or white spots on head and upper body; faint brown bars can occur. [1]
  • Can reach about 10 cm, approximately 4 inches; yellow and whitish barred phases are the same species. [1] [4]
  • Lives on sandy bottoms of seagrass beds, lagoons and sheltered coral reefs, at 2–15 m depth in Oregon Coast Aquarium account. [1]
  • Alpheid shrimp build and maintain the shared burrow; goby guards and warns of approaching predators, with touch and movement communication. [1]
  • Aquarium diet recommendation: varied meat-based foods; bottom-feeding fish can learn to feed in mid-water. Oregon account lists algae and bivalves, so do not portray it as an obligate herbivore or make universal diet claims. [4] [1]
  • Aquarium manufacturer guidance: generally peaceful and reef-safe; avoid conspecifics except mated pairs. Reef compatibility is guidance, not a guarantee for every individual or community. [4]
  • Provide sand or fine gravel deep enough for burrowing, secure rocks on the tank bottom to prevent settling onto the fish, and use a secure lid because it can jump. [4]
  • Manufacturer minimum 30 US gallons, approximately 114 liters; a minimum recommendation rather than a guarantee for any stocking plan. [4]
