Tail Signals — Read the Whole Cat

Focused feline tail-signal clusters with whole-body and interaction context, rather than isolated emotion labels.

Research behind the infographic

  • Interpret tails together with ear positions, posture and interaction context; a single pose is not a guaranteed emotion or diagnosis. [1] [2]
  • A tail-up approach can be a friendly greeting, particularly in cat-human interactions; show smooth fur, upright ears and an easy walking posture, not a universal invitation to touch. [2] [1]
  • A tucked tail combined with a low crouch and flattened ears can indicate fear; these are clustered cues, not certainty. [1]
  • Tail lashing with ears moving backward during petting can precede aggression; stop petting and allow a no-contact pause. Tail movement can also occur in play, so context matters. [1]
  • Raised tail fur with an arched back and flattened-back ears can accompany defensive/aggressive displays; do not label a puffed tail as a unique emotion. [1]
  • Ear positions were more predictive than tails of cat-cat interaction outcome in this observational shelter study; do not generalize its exact frequencies or treat tails as meaningless. [2]
