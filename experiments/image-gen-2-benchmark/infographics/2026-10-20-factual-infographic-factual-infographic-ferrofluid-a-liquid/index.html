Ferrofluid — A Liquid Shaped by Fields

An oil-based magnetite suspension and the normal-field instability that creates surface spikes.

Research behind the infographic

  • Ferrofluid contains tiny magnetic solid particles suspended in a liquid carrier. [1] [2]
  • An oil-based magnetite ferrofluid can contain magnetite (Fe3O4) nanoparticles coated with an oleic-acid surfactant in oil. [1]
  • The surfactant polar heads attach to magnetite surfaces and hydrocarbon tails extend into the oil; the coating helps keep particles apart and prevents clumping. [1]
  • A magnet beneath a shallow dish can cause the ferrofluid surface to rise into spikes oriented along the magnetic field. [1] [2]
  • Normal-field instability is also called Rosensweig instability. [2]
  • In a sufficiently strong vertical magnetic field, magnetic effects overcome the stabilizing effects of gravity and surface tension and the flat surface develops spikes. [2]
  • A regular spike pattern may have hexagonal arrangement. [2]
  • Field lines and enlarged particles in the illustration are explanatory schematics, not visible structures or a shared physical scale. [1] [2]
