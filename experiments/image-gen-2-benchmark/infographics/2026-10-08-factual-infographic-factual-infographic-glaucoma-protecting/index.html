Glaucoma — Protecting the Optic Nerve

Detailed anatomy and clinical education distinguishing open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma, pressure and optic nerve injury, detection, management and emergency warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that damage the optic nerve at the back of the eye; lost vision cannot be restored by glaucoma treatment. [1] [2]
  • The optic nerve contains many nerve fibers; loss of these fibers causes blind spots. [2]
  • Aqueous fluid in the front of the eye normally leaves through the drainage angle; impaired drainage can increase eye pressure. [2]
  • Open-angle glaucoma develops gradually when the eye does not drain fluid well despite an open angle; it is painless and often has no obvious early symptoms. [2]
  • High eye pressure increases risk, but some people with high pressure do not develop glaucoma and some have glaucoma at normal pressure. [1] [2]
  • In angle-closure glaucoma, the iris can block the drainage angle; sudden complete blockage can rapidly raise eye pressure and is an emergency. [2]
  • Later glaucoma can cause peripheral vision loss and blind spots; illustrative field loss is variable, not a diagnostic simulation. [1] [2]
  • Older age, a family history of glaucoma and high eye pressure are risk factors, not guarantees of disease. [1] [2]
  • Diagnosis needs a comprehensive eye examination; pressure alone is insufficient. Examination includes optic nerve assessment, visual field testing and drainage angle inspection; corneal thickness may be measured. [1] [2]
  • Prescription drops lower pressure by reducing aqueous production or improving outflow; treatment choice depends on the person and disease type. [2]
  • Laser trabeculoplasty improves drainage in open-angle glaucoma; laser iridotomy makes a small iris opening for angle-closure glaucoma. [2]
  • Glaucoma surgery can create a new drainage route or use a drainage tube; pressure reduction aims to limit further nerve damage, not restore lost vision. [1] [2]
  • Follow prescribed treatment, report side effects, attend regular follow-up and do not stop glaucoma medicines without discussing with the ophthalmologist. [1] [2]
  • Sudden intense eye pain, red eye, blurred vision or nausea can signal angle closure; halos, headache and vomiting can occur. Seek urgent eye or emergency care immediately. [1] [2]
