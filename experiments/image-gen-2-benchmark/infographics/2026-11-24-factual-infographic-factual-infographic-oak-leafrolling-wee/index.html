Oak Leafrolling Weevil, the Leaf-Bedroll Builder

How the oak leafrolling weevil Synolabus bipustulatus rolls an oak leaf into a nidus for its egg and larva

Research behind the infographic

  • The oak leafrolling weevil, Synolabus bipustulatus (synonym Attelabus bipustulatus), is a small, stout, shiny black weevil with one red spot on each elytron. [1] [2]
  • Adults are less than 1/4 inch long. [1] [2]
  • The adult chews slits on either side of the leaf midrib and lays an egg near the leaf tip. [1] [2]
  • The leaf is folded along the midrib and rolled into a neat cylinder, with the egg near the center of the roll. [1] [2] [3]
  • The finished leaf roll is called a nidus; nidus is Latin for nest. [2] [3] [4]
  • The egg is tiny and shaped like a rugby ball. [2]
  • The larva is a legless, curved, plump grub with a brown head that feeds on the leaf tissue inside the roll, out of reach of predators. [1] [2] [3]
  • The larva pupates inside the leaf roll and a new adult emerges; there may be more than one generation per year. [1] [2]
  • Leafrolling weevils have been recorded on 16 species of oak and 2 species of chestnut. [1] [2]
  • They are rarely pests and typically do not cause noticeable damage to host trees. [1] [2]
  • It is recorded in eastern US oak woodlands including Massachusetts, Connecticut and North Carolina; its distribution is slightly more northerly than Homoeolabus analis and it is not found in Florida. [1] [2] [3] [4]
  • A similar oak leaf-rolling weevil, Homoeolabus analis, is orange-red with a black head and legs. [1] [2] [4]
  • The thief weevil Pterocolus ovatus, an egg predator and nidus kleptoparasite, uses attelabid hosts including A. bipustulatus. [4]
  • Generalized insect body plan (simplified schematic, not a species dissection): the gut has foregut, midgut and hindgut; most digestion and absorption occur in the midgut; Malpighian tubules for excretion join the gut at the midgut-hindgut junction. [5] [6]
  • Generalized insect body plan: the dorsal vessel (heart) pumps blood through an open body cavity; air enters through spiracles and travels through branching tracheae. [5] [6]
  • Generalized insect body plan: the brain in the head processes sensory information, with a ventral nerve cord of ganglia; the fat body stores energy. [5] [6]
