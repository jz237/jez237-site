Colobopsis explodens — The Ant That Ruptures to Defend

A source-verified insect-first museum infographic showing the exploding ant Colobopsis explodens, its body-length mandibular-gland reservoirs, the close-contact autothysis sequence, caste-specific nest defense, honest scale, rainforest habitat, and conservative secretion chemistry.

Research behind the infographic

  • Colobopsis explodens is a real ant species in the Southeast Asian Colobopsis cylindrica species group and was described from material associated with Borneo, Thailand, and Malaysia. [1]
  • Field observations of Colobopsis explodens were made in lowland dipterocarp rainforest in Brunei, where colonies occupied trees and wooden nest cavities. [1]
  • Minor workers are reddish-brown, measure about 4-7 mm, and carry enlarged mandibular-gland reservoirs extending through much of the body. [1] [2]
  • During close territorial conflict, a minor worker can rupture the integument of its gaster, an irreversible self-sacrificial behavior called autothysis. [1] [2]
  • Autothysis releases bright yellow, sticky mandibular-gland reservoir contents onto an arthropod opponent, helping restrain, repel, or kill the opponent. [1] [2]
  • The defensive sequence is a close-contact mechanism, not a fireball or pressure explosion: the gaster ruptures and transfers adhesive secretion to the opponent. [1] [2]
  • Major workers have enlarged plug-shaped heads and can block entrances to wooden nests, a defensive behavior known as phragmosis. [1] [2]
  • Chemical analyses found a complex mixture of volatile compounds in the minor workers' mandibular-gland reservoir contents, including alkanes, carboxylic acids, and phenolic compounds. [2]
  • Researchers proposed that likely irritant and antibiotic phenolic constituents may contribute to defense against arthropod opponents or microbes, but the functions of many individual compounds remain unresolved. [2]
  • The self-sacrificial rupture behavior belongs to minor workers and should not be implied to occur in every caste or every encounter. [1] [2]
