Reading the Cat Food Adequacy Statement

An artwork-led guide to nutritional adequacy statements, substantiation routes and the distinction from ingredients and treats.

Research behind the infographic

  • The nutritional adequacy statement identifies whether food is complete and balanced and the intended life stage. [1] [2]
  • Complete and balanced food is intended as a sole diet; treats, snacks and supplements typically are not. [2]
  • A complete and balanced claim requires meeting an AAFCO nutrient profile or passing a feeding trial using AAFCO procedures. [2]
  • Ingredient lists are ordered by decreasing weight and are not the nutritional adequacy statement. [1]
  • Cats need protein, fats, vitamins, minerals, fatty acids and amino acids; more supplementation is not automatically better. [1]
  • Choose food appropriate to the cat with veterinary guidance; this chart is general education, not an individual diet plan. [1]
