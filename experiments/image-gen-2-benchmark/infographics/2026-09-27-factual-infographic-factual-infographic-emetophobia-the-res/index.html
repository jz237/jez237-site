Emetophobia — The Restaurant Table

Educational portrait infographic on emetophobia using an illustrative restaurant setting, seven distinct panels, and three explanatory close-ups.

Research behind the infographic

  • Emetophobia is a marked fear or anxiety of vomiting oneself or seeing others vomit. [1] [3]
  • Unfamiliar restaurant food, nausea or stomach sensations, and noticing possible illness in others can be triggers; the restaurant scene is illustrative, not a dangerous incident. [1] [3]
  • Fear may involve uncertainty about being sick, losing control, or being unable to leave a situation. [1] [3]
  • Anxiety may bring increased heart rate, altered breathing, nausea, and light-headedness; these sensations do not by themselves prove vomiting is imminent. [1] [3]
  • People may monitor internal sensations or others for illness signs and interpret ambiguous sensations as evidence vomiting is near. [1] [3]
  • Avoidance of restaurants or certain foods, date-checking, excessive cleaning, and reassurance seeking may occur; these behaviours may narrow daily life. [1] [3]
  • A phobia response can be out of proportion to the actual danger, while ordinary food-safety caution remains reasonable. [2] [3]
  • Avoidance can bring short-term relief yet maintain fear and interfere with daily activities. [1] [2] [3]
  • CBT, including clinician-guided exposure approaches adapted to emetophobia, is an evidence-based treatment; a qualified professional can help individualize care. [2] [3]
  • The graphic offers education, not diagnosis or instructions to induce vomiting, eat unsafe food, or abandon hygiene. [2] [3]
