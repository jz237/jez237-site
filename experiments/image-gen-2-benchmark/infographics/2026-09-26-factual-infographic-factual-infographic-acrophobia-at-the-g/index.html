Acrophobia — At the Glass Skywalk

Acrophobia and height-related fear illustrated through a guarded public skywalk, variable bodily symptoms, avoidance, actual-risk appraisal and evidence-based help.

Research behind the infographic

  • Fear of heights is a specific-phobia example; phobic fear can be disproportionate to actual danger. [1] [2]
  • A height situation can trigger severe anxiety and worry. [1] [2]
  • Possible phobia symptoms include dizziness, noticeable heartbeat, nausea and shaking; symptoms vary. [2]
  • A phobia can prompt avoidance and immediate anxiety at the feared situation. [1]
  • Avoidance may bring short-term relief yet make a feared situation harder to face later. [3]
  • The fight-or-flight response can be heightened in situations that are not actually dangerous. [3]
  • CBT is an established phobia treatment; exposure therapy can involve planned, gradual confrontation of feared situations. [1] [2] [3]
  • Someone whose phobia symptoms affect life can seek help from a GP or qualified mental health professional. [1] [2]
  • Actual height hazards require safety precautions; protection features matter in assessing actual danger. [1] [3]
