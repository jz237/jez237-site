Wheel Bug, the Cog-Crested Predator

How the wheel bug Arilus cristatus uses its piercing beak to digest insect prey

Research behind the infographic

  • Wheel bug Arilus cristatus is an assassin bug with a coglike toothed crest on the adult thorax and a gray to brown body. [2]
  • Adults measure about 1 to 1.25 inches and occur across North America, including the United States, Mexico and Guatemala. [1]
  • Wheel bugs live among vegetation including trees and shrubs, and prey on insects including caterpillars. [1]
  • Enlarged front legs seize prey; the piercing beak injects digestive enzymes and the bug sucks up liquefied tissue. [1]
  • Egg clusters are laid on twigs in fall, overwinter and hatch in spring; red and black nymphs lack the adult crest. [1]
  • Simplified generalized insect anatomy: spiracles lead to branching tracheae; the gut includes foregut, midgut and hindgut; a dorsal vessel moves hemolymph; a ventral nerve cord links ganglia. [3]
