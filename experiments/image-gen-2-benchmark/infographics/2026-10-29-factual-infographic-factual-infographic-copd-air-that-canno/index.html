COPD — Air That Cannot Easily Leave

Anatomy-led explanation of COPD airflow limitation, chronic bronchitis and emphysema, symptoms, exposures, spirometry, supportive care and urgent warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • COPD means chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; lung and airway damage restrict airflow and make breathing difficult. [1] [2]
  • Airways carry air to elastic air sacs (alveoli); normal air sacs fill on inhalation and deflate on exhalation. [1] [3]
  • Chronic bronchitis involves inflamed swollen airway lining and excess mucus that narrow or clog airways. [1] [2] [3]
  • Emphysema damages walls between alveoli and reduces elasticity, making it harder to move air out; most people with COPD have a varying mixture of emphysema and chronic bronchitis. [1] [3]
  • Symptoms include breathlessness, especially with activity, chronic cough with or without mucus, wheezing and fatigue. [2] [3]
  • Long-term tobacco smoke, air pollution and occupational dust or fumes can contribute to COPD; not everyone with COPD has smoked. [1] [2] [3]
  • Spirometry is a breathing test used to confirm COPD; symptoms alone are not a diagnosis. [2]
  • COPD has no cure, but treatments can improve symptoms and ability to stay active. [1] [2] [3]
  • Quitting smoking, avoiding lung irritants, appropriate vaccinations and pulmonary rehabilitation are parts of COPD care. [2] [3]
  • Bronchodilator inhalers relax airway muscles and help keep airways open; correct inhaler technique matters and care is individualized. [2] [3]
  • Oxygen therapy is used when indicated for low blood oxygen, not routinely for everyone with COPD. [3]
  • A sudden worsening is a flare-up (exacerbation); contact a healthcare provider right away for suddenly worse symptoms. [2] [4]
  • Get emergency care for severe trouble catching your breath or talking. [3]
