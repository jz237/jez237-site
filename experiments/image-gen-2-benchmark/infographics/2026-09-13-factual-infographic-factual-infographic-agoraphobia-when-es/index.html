Agoraphobia — When Escape Feels Out of Reach

A detailed real-world educational infographic explaining how agoraphobia can connect escape or help concerns across public transport, open and enclosed spaces, lines or crowds, and being outside home alone; it maps body alarm, avoidance, and evidence-based professional help without diagnosing the viewer.

Research behind the infographic

  • Agoraphobia involves fear of situations where escape might be difficult or help might not be available if something goes wrong. [1] [2]
  • Triggering situations can include public transport, open spaces, enclosed spaces, standing in line or crowds, and being outside the home alone. [1] [2]
  • A stressful triggering situation can produce immediate intense anxiety and panic-like symptoms such as rapid heartbeat, rapid breathing, sweating, feeling hot, or nausea. [1] [2]
  • People may actively avoid triggering situations or leave home only with a trusted companion. [1] [2]
  • Avoidance may follow an association between panic attacks and the places where they occurred, although not everyone with agoraphobia has a history of panic attacks. [1]
  • Phobic fear may be out of proportion to the actual danger presented by the situation. [2]
  • Cognitive behavioral therapy teaches different ways of thinking, behaving, and reacting to help reduce anxiety and fear. [1] [2]
  • Exposure therapy is a CBT method that gradually addresses feared situations and activities that have been avoided; it should be presented as a supported treatment pathway, not an abrupt self-directed challenge. [2]
  • A primary care provider or qualified mental health professional can assess symptoms and help identify appropriate treatment options. [1] [2]
