Belostoma flumineum — The Egg-Bearing Father

Species-specific illustrated account of dorsal paternal egg brooding, freshwater anatomy, respiratory posture, feeding apparatus, development, range and comparison with Lethocerus.

Research behind the infographic

  • Belostoma flumineum is a valid insect species in Hemiptera, family Belostomatidae; giant water bug is the family common name. [1] [2]
  • Belostoma flumineum is a relatively narrow, dark, freshwater giant water bug, at least 20 mm and under 30 mm in the University of Florida identification key. [1]
  • Females of Belostoma glue eggs to males’ backs; egg-bearing males carry the dorsal clutch. [1]
  • The eggs develop and hatch into aquatic nymphs, which grow toward winged adults. [1]
  • Giant water bugs live in freshwater ponds and ditches and hang below the surface to breathe through paired abdominal appendages. [1]
  • Giant water bugs are aquatic predators that seize prey with strong front legs and use a piercing beak. [1]
  • Belostoma flumineum has a North American range extending from southern Canada and New England into parts of the southern United States. [1]
  • Belostoma and Abedus carry eggs on males’ backs; Lethocerus deposits eggs above water on vegetation or other objects. [1]
  • The adult insect has six legs, a head, thorax and abdomen, and folded true-bug forewings. [1] [2]
