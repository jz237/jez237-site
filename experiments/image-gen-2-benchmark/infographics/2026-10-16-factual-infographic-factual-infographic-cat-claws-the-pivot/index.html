Cat Claws — The Pivot Beneath the Fur

Normal forepaw claw anatomy, active protrusion, passive retraction and superficial horn-cap shedding.

Research behind the infographic

  • The illustrated mechanism is a normal domestic cat forepaw digit 2–5; middle and distal phalanges form the distal interphalangeal joint. [1]
  • The distal phalanx overlaps the middle phalanx during passive retraction rather than telescoping straight into the toe. [1]
  • Elastic ligaments passively retract the claw; the lateral elastic ligament is the most important. [1]
  • Contraction of the deep digital flexor protrudes the claw through its attachment to the flexor process of the distal phalanx; digital extensors stabilize more proximal joints. [1]
  • Retraction protects the claw from wear when not in use, with the claw sheltered in a protective skin fold. [1] [2]
  • The cornified claw sheath surrounds living tissues supported by the distal phalanx; the horny sheath is not the same thing as the bone. [2]
  • Superficial horn caps can shed without injury to the underlying living tissues; this maintains sharp tips. A shed cap is not the entire claw or toe bone. [2]
