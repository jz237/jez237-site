Psoriasis — Skin Turnover and Inflammation

Artwork-led clinical atlas explaining psoriasis, its immune-mediated mechanism, symptoms and individualized management, with essential clinical warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Psoriasis is a chronic immune-mediated condition; immune overactivity causes skin cells to multiply too quickly, producing scaly inflamed patches. [1] [2]
  • Psoriasis is not contagious. [2]
  • Dry, itchy, sore, flaky plaques commonly affect elbows, knees and scalp, although other sites may be affected. [1] [2]
  • Plaques may look pink or red with light scales on white skin, and grey, white, raised and scaly on dark brown skin. [2]
  • Symptoms can flare and then improve or remit. [1] [2]
  • Genetics and environmental factors contribute; stress and skin injuries can trigger flares. [1] [2]
  • Treatment is individualized by type, severity and location; moisturizers and prescribed topical treatments can help, while more severe disease may need medically supervised light therapy or systemic medicines. [1] [2]
  • See a clinician for suspected psoriasis, ineffective treatment or side effects; persistent recurrent joint pain, stiffness and swelling warrant assessment for possible psoriatic arthritis. [1] [2]
  • Seek emergency care for rapidly reddening skin with merging pus-filled bumps; fever, shivering or a fast heartbeat may accompany a serious condition or infection. Redness can be harder to see on darker skin. [2]
  • The conceptual skin cutaway contrasts ordinary skin with increased cell production and a thick scaly surface in psoriasis; it is a schematic rather than a diagnostic specimen. [1] [2]
