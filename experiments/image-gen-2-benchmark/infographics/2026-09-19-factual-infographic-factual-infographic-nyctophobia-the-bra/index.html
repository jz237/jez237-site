Research behind the infographic

  • A phobia is an intense fear or aversion focused on a particular object or situation, and the fear can be out of proportion to the actual danger. [1]
  • People with a phobia may worry excessively about encountering the trigger, avoid it, feel immediate intense anxiety when encountering it, or endure it with intense anxiety. [1]
  • Phobia symptoms can include severe anxiety, dizziness, a noticeable heartbeat, chest tightness, nausea, trembling, and intense fear of fainting or losing control. [3]
  • Specific phobias can center on particular situations; this plate applies that framework to darkness and night as the feared situation called nyctophobia. [1] [3]
  • Avoidance is a common response to phobic fear, while exposure therapy focuses on safely confronting feared situations so a person can re-engage with activities they have avoided. [1]
  • Cognitive behavioral therapy is an established treatment approach for phobias and teaches different ways of thinking, behaving, and reacting to reduce anxiety and fear. [1]
  • A university anxiety clinic describes CBT for specific phobias as education about phobias, exposure to feared sensations and situations, and techniques that challenge unhelpful thoughts and beliefs. [2]
  • Breathing exercises may help relaxation, and people should seek professional help when phobia symptoms affect daily life. [3]
  • A qualified health professional can assess symptoms and recommend appropriate treatment; exposure work should be presented as supported care rather than an unsafe do-it-yourself challenge. [1] [2]
