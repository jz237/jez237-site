Lily Exposure — Pollen, Petals and Vase Water

A botanical feline hazard atlas connecting three lily-exposure routes to kidney risk and urgent veterinary contact.

Research behind the infographic

  • True lilies (Lilium species) and daylilies (Hemerocallis species) can cause potentially fatal kidney failure in cats. [1] [2]
  • All parts of these plants are hazardous, including stems, leaves, flowers and pollen; vase water is also hazardous. [1]
  • Cats can be poisoned by chewing plant material, licking pollen from fur while grooming, or drinking lily vase water; small exposures can cause severe harm. [1]
  • The specific kidney-toxic substance has not been identified; do not depict a known molecule or a specific molecular mechanism. [1]
  • Early signs may include vomiting, drooling, loss of appetite and reduced activity; do not wait for signs before seeking help. [1] [2]
  • Suspected ingestion of lily material, pollen or vase water warrants immediate veterinary or pet poison control contact; early veterinary treatment improves prognosis. [1] [2]
  • Keep these lilies out of cat households; bring a plant photograph or sample to help identification when seeking veterinary care. [1] [2]
  • Do not induce vomiting unless specifically directed by a veterinarian or poison control professional. [2]
  • An Easter lily (Lilium longiflorum) is a dangerous true lily; daylilies belong to Hemerocallis, a distinct genus. [1]
  • Not every plant called lily has the same toxin or risk: peace lilies and calla lilies cause calcium-oxalate irritation rather than this true-lily kidney toxicity; they are not safe to chew. [1]
