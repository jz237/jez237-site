Chronic Sinusitis — Inflamed Drainage Pathways

Source-backed schematic of sinus inflammation, drainage, symptoms, evaluation and management.

Research behind the infographic

  • Sinuses are air spaces behind the cheekbones and forehead that connect to the inside of the nose. [1]
  • Swollen sinus lining interferes with mucus drainage into the nose and throat. [1]
  • Symptoms can include facial pressure or tenderness, blocked nose, nasal discharge and reduced smell. [1]
  • Chronic sinusitis is driven more by inflammation than infection; antibiotics are not always helpful. [2]
  • Allergies, nasal polyps and asthma can accompany sinusitis and make it worse. [2]
  • Saline nasal irrigation and nasal steroid sprays are main treatments for chronic sinusitis symptoms. For home irrigation, NHS describes boiling water then cooling it before use. [1] [2]
  • Prolonged or repeated symptoms warrant clinical evaluation; CT may be needed for prolonged symptoms or suspected complications, not routinely for uncomplicated sinusitis. [1] [2]
  • Endoscopic surgery may help selected chronic cases not controlled by medicines by widening natural sinus drainage pathways. [2]
  • Seek urgent medical help if very unwell, symptoms worsen or painkillers do not help, or immunity is weakened. [1]
