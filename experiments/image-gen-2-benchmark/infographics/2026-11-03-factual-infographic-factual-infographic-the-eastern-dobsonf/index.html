The Eastern Dobsonfly’s Hellgrammite

The eastern dobsonfly’s aquatic larval anatomy, predation, respiration and complete metamorphosis.

Research behind the infographic

  • The eastern dobsonfly is Corydalus cornutus, a megalopteran in the family Corydalidae, found in eastern North America. [1]
  • Its aquatic larva, called a hellgrammite, is flattened, dark and segmented, with three pairs of thoracic legs and sharp mandibles. [1]
  • The larval abdomen has eight pairs of lateral filaments with cottony gill tufts at their bases, and terminal hooked appendages help resist stream current. [1]
  • Insect biological gills allow dissolved oxygen to diffuse into the body and are usually outgrowths of the tracheal system; the internal breathing diagram is generalized. [3] [1]
  • Aquatic larvae live beneath rocks or logs in streams and prey on aquatic animals. [2]
  • Complete metamorphosis proceeds from egg to aquatic larva to land pupa to winged adult; larval development takes several years. [2]
  • Adults have long antennae, two pairs of prominently veined wings held over the body at rest, and three pairs of legs. [1] [2]
  • Male mandibles are long and sickle-shaped; female mandibles are much shorter. [1] [2]
  • Adults can exceed 3.5 inches in length and larvae are approximately 2–3 inches long. [1]
