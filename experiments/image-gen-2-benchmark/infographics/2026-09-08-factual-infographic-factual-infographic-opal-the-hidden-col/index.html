Opal — The Hidden Color Lattice

A direct-canvas gemology infographic reveals how ordered hydrated-silica spheres and intervening voids diffract light, support interference, and create angle-dependent play-of-color in precious opal.

Research behind the infographic

  • Opal is hydrated silica and is amorphous rather than having a definite crystal structure. [2]
  • Precious opal displays play-of-color, while common opal does not. [1] [2]
  • Precious opal contains a regular three-dimensional array of submicroscopic, similarly sized hydrated-silica spheres separated by tiny voids. [1] [2]
  • The ordered sphere-and-void structure acts as a three-dimensional diffraction grating that separates white light into spectral colors. [1] [2]
  • Interference among diffracted wavelengths reinforces some colors and cancels others, producing play-of-color. [2]
  • The size and spacing of the silica spheres influence which visible wavelengths are reinforced. [1] [2]
  • Small dislocations in the ordered stacking create bounded color domains whose appearance changes with the angle of incident light. [2]
  • Common opal lacks play-of-color because its silica-sphere structure is not sufficiently regular and the spheres vary in size. [2]
  • Opal can form when silica-bearing water deposits silica in cracks and spaces in rock as water is lost. [1]
  • White, black, crystal, fire, and boulder opal describe different combinations of body color, transparency, matrix, and play-of-color; fire opal does not necessarily show play-of-color. [1] [2]
  • Because opal contains water, sudden or extreme changes in temperature or humidity can cause cracking. [2]
