Tortoiseshell — A Mosaic of Active X Chromosomes

A focused genetics atlas connecting a tortoiseshell coat to random X inactivation, pigment-cell lineages and orange-associated ARHGAP36 regulation. Schematic studies are explicitly labeled.

Research behind the infographic

  • Tortoiseshell cats have mottled orange and non-orange fur, and are nearly always female. [1] [2]
  • In a typical XX cat carrying orange and non-orange variants, one X chromosome is randomly inactivated in each cell early in development. [1] [2]
  • Descendant cells retain different active X chromosomes, producing a mosaic of pigment-cell areas and orange or black coat patches. [1] [2]
  • The orange-associated deletion is in a non-coding region at the X-linked ARHGAP36 locus; orange patches show increased ARHGAP36 activity in melanocytes. [1] [2]
  • Orange patches favor pheomelanin and black patches eumelanin; the cell and chromosome artwork is a simplified mechanism schematic, not a literal patch map. [2]
  • A coat-color pattern does not establish an individual cat’s personality; behavioral links are not established by these findings. [1] [2]
