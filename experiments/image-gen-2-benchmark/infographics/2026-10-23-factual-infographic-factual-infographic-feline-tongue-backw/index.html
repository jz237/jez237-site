Feline Tongue — Backward-Facing Papillae

Focused normal feline tongue microanatomy and the capillary transfer of saliva during grooming.

Research behind the infographic

  • Licking is a normal feline grooming behavior; show a healthy domestic cat grooming its foreleg. [2] [1]
  • The feline tongue bears backward-facing keratin spines called filiform papillae; depict them pointing toward the throat, not toward the tongue tip. [1]
  • The large grooming papillae have a scoop-shaped, U-shaped cavity at the tip which wicks saliva by surface tension; this is not a hollow syringe connected to a salivary duct. [1]
  • During tongue expansion the grooming papillae rotate to stand more erect and increase contact with fur. [1]
  • The tongue presses and compresses fur during the grooming stroke; papillae deliver saliva deeper into the coat than the tongue surface alone. [1]
  • Saliva wicked into papilla cavities is released onto hairs by contact during grooming. [1]
