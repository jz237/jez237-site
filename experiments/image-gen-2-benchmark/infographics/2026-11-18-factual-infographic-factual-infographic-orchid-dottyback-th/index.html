Orchid Dottyback — The Red Sea’s Violet Crevice Hunter

Photorealistic reef atlas connecting Red Sea refuge and plankton feeding, male cave-nest care and carefully qualified aquarium guidance.

Research behind the infographic

  • Accepted species is Pseudochromis fridmani Klausewitz, 1968; marine Pseudochromidae; WoRMS synonym endpoint returns no synonyms. [6] [7]
  • Known only from the Red Sea; marine reef-associated fish on vertical rock faces or beneath overhangs, taking refuge in small holes. [1] [2]
  • Recorded maximum total length is 6.3 cm, about 2.5 inches. [1]
  • Purple/magenta fish with a diagnostic black line through the eye; aquarium illumination shows purple, while red-light filtering can make wild deeper fish appear blue. [3] [4] [2]
  • Darts from reef ledges to feed on passing zooplankton and returns to shelter. [2]
  • Aquarium foods include mysid and brine shrimp, krill, pellets and flakes for cultured fish. [2] [4]
  • Moderately territorial; provide many hiding places; may fight conspecifics and similar-looking fish. More peaceful than many other dottybacks does not mean aggression-free. [2] [4]
  • Generally compatible with corals, but may chase very small shrimp; reef-safe is not a guarantee for every small mobile invertebrate. [4] [3]
  • Biota suggested tank size 30 US gallons, approximately 114 liters; this is husbandry guidance rather than a wild biological threshold. [3]
  • Egg mass held by filamentous threads is deposited in nooks or caves and tended by the male. [2]
  • Captive breeding is established; one published captive study found approximately 96 hour incubation at 27 ± 1 degrees Celsius, not a universal timer. [5]
