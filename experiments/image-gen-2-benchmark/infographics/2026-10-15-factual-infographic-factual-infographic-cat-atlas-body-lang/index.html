Cat Atlas — Body Language

A contextual guide to feline ears, eyes, whiskers, posture and tail, with comfort, tension, pupil and belly-display distinctions.

Research behind the infographic

  • Cat body signals should be interpreted together and in context, not as a fixed one-signal emotional code. [1] [2]
  • An upright tail with a softly curved tip and relaxed body often accompanies a friendly greeting. [1] [3]
  • Soft narrowed eyes and slow blinking can accompany comfort; experiments link slow-blink interactions with positive cat–human communication. [3] [4]
  • Ears directed forward and whiskers oriented toward an object can accompany interest or attention, not necessarily happiness. [1]
  • A crouched tense body, ears sideways or back and a closely held tail can form a cluster of unease signals. [1] [2]
  • During contact, body tension, ears turning back and tail lashing can indicate that interaction should pause. [5] [2]
  • A relaxed exposed belly is not automatically an invitation to rub it. [3]
  • Pupil size changes with lighting and can also change with arousal; one pupil size does not identify one emotion. [6] [1] [2]
