Achilles Tendinopathy — The Calf-to-Heel Connection

Achilles tendon anatomy, midportion and insertional symptoms, evaluation, tailored management and rupture warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • The Achilles tendon connects calf muscles to the heel bone (calcaneus) and is used during walking, running, jumping and standing on tiptoes. [1]
  • Repetitive stress, a sudden increase in exercise and tight calf muscles can contribute to Achilles tendon problems. [1] [2]
  • Noninsertional disease affects the middle tendon; insertional disease affects its heel attachment. [1]
  • Affected tendon fibers can develop tiny tears, degeneration, swelling and thickening; this is not the same as a complete rupture. [1]
  • Symptoms include morning stiffness, pain along the tendon or heel worsened by activity, and swelling. [1]
  • Assessment starts with history and examination; imaging may be selected and MRI is not always needed for diagnosis. [1]
  • Reducing aggravating activity, supportive footwear and physical therapy including supervised strengthening can help; treatment must be tailored to the individual. [1] [2]
  • Recovery can take months; persistent symptoms should be assessed by a clinician. [1] [2]
  • Sudden severe pain or a pop at the back of the calf or heel may signal rupture and requires urgent medical assessment. [1] [2]
