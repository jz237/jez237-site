Acanthaspis petax — The Ant-Carcass Backpack

An insect-first direct-canvas infographic showing how the real assassin-bug species Acanthaspis petax carries a mask of ant carcasses and how controlled jumping-spider presentations support a conservative visual-defense interpretation.

Research behind the infographic

  • Acanthaspis petax is a valid insect species in the assassin-bug family Reduviidae and the true-bug order Hemiptera. [1]
  • The Acanthaspis petax individual shown is a juvenile nymph carrying camouflage rather than an invented adult life stage. [2] [3]
  • Acanthaspis petax covers itself with a mask or backpack made from carcasses of its preferred ant prey. [2]
  • The cited Acanthaspis petax and jumping-spider experiment used animals from the Lake Victoria region of East Africa. [2]
  • Researchers compared live or lure bugs with the carcass mask intact against bugs with the mask removed. [2]
  • In the experiment, jumping spiders made predatory responses significantly more often to naked bugs than to masked bugs. [2]
  • The experiment supports the conservative interpretation that the carried carcass mask can hinder recognition by vision-guided jumping-spider predators; it does not establish perfect protection against every predator. [2]
  • A separate refereed Animal Behaviour study documents nymphal backpack camouflage in West African Acanthaspis and Paredocla assassin bugs, providing broader behavioral context without being treated as species-specific proof for every Acanthaspis species. [3]
