Coral Fluorescence — Proteins That Shift Light

A source-verified direct-canvas museum infographic showing how coral GFP-like host pigments shift light, where they occur in coral tissue, how cyan, green, red and non-fluorescent chromoprotein families differ, and why proposed biological roles remain species- and context-dependent.

Research behind the infographic

  • Many coral host pigments are green-fluorescent-protein-like pigments, and fluorescent forms span cyan, green, and red emission colors. [2]
  • A fluorescent protein absorbs higher-energy light and emits lower-energy light at a longer wavelength. [1] [2]
  • Non-fluorescent chromoproteins can absorb visible light strongly while producing little or no fluorescence, and commonly contribute purple-blue coloration. [2]
  • The pigment types and their expression can differ among coral species and among individuals of the same species. [2]
  • Coral host pigments can alter the local light field inside coral tissue; proposed photobiological effects include photoprotection or light enhancement depending on species and environment, but their precise biological roles are not fully resolved. [2]
  • In four Hawaiian mesophotic Leptoseris species studied at 65-125 m, cyan and green fluorescence was localized mainly in the coenosarc and/or oral disc, and fluorescent and non-fluorescent colonies co-occurred across the investigated depths. [1]
  • In that Leptoseris study, cyan fluorescent protein was more common at shallower sampled depths, green fluorescent protein at deeper sampled depths, and both occurred at intermediate sampled depths. [1]
  • The Leptoseris study found similar symbiont genotypes, abundance, pigments, photosynthetic efficiency, photosynthetic rates, and chlorophyll excitation spectra in fluorescent and non-fluorescent corals, so those fluorescent proteins were unlikely to significantly enhance symbiont photosynthesis in the studied corals. [1]
  • Coral tissue can contain intracellular dinoflagellate photosymbionts in the endodermis, while host fluorescent pigments occupy species-dependent tissue locations and modify the light microenvironment around them. [1] [2]
