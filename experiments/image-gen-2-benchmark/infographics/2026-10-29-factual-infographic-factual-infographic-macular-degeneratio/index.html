Macular Degeneration — The Center of Sight

Macular anatomy, dry and wet AMD, central-vision symptoms, prompt assessment and type-specific clinical care.

Research behind the infographic

  • The macula is part of the light-sensitive retina at the back of the eye and supports sharp straight-ahead central vision. [1]
  • AMD damages the macula with aging; its exact cause is unknown, and age, family history and smoking increase risk. [1] [2]
  • Dry AMD can feature yellow drusen beneath the retina and progress to retinal atrophy; wet AMD features abnormal vessels beneath the retina that may leak or bleed and distort retinal structure. These are different disease patterns, not a universal linear sequence. [2]
  • Early AMD may have no symptoms; advanced disease can cause wavy straight lines and blurred or blank central vision, with peripheral vision generally preserved rather than complete blindness. [1] [2]
  • New wavy lines or central vision changes require prompt eye-doctor assessment; straight lines looking wavy warrant seeing an eye doctor right away. [1] [2]
  • A comprehensive dilated eye examination and OCT imaging can help evaluate AMD. [1] [2]
  • Treatment depends on type and stage: anti-VEGF eye injections treat wet AMD and can preserve vision; selected patients with intermediate AMD may benefit from clinician-recommended AREDS-type supplements, not a universal supplement recommendation. [1] [2]
  • Quitting smoking, regular exercise and healthy eating may help lower risk or slow AMD-related vision loss; low-vision devices and rehabilitation can support daily activities. [1]
