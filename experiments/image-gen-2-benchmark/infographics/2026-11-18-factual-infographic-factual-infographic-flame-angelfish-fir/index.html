Flame Angelfish — Fire in the Reef’s Shelter

Photorealistic reef atlas of the flame angelfish: verified markings, Pacific reef shelter, grazing and social biology with producer-labeled aquarium guidance and qualified reef compatibility.

Research behind the infographic

  • The accepted WoRMS name is Centropyge loriculus; Centropyge loricula, Centropyge flammeus and Holacanthus loriculus refer to the same species. [3] [4] [2]
  • Typical fish have orange-red bodies, orange-yellow midbody, black vertical bars and a dark blotch behind the pectoral fin; blue/purple streaks highlight posterior dorsal and anal fins. Caudal rays are light orange-yellow with translucent membranes; pelvic fins are orange-red. [1] [5]
  • Occurs across scattered western and central Pacific reef localities, including Palau, Marshall and Society Islands; rich lagoon and seaward coral reefs at 5–60 m are habitat. [1]
  • Secretive fish stay close to shelter; benthic algae and detritus are primary wild foods. [1]
  • Flame angelfish are protogynous; females can change into males, and wild males maintain harems; spawning typically occurs at dusk. [1]
  • Biota describes typical captive size about 4 inches (approximately 10 cm) and recommends adult aquaria of at least 65 US gallons (approximately 246 liters); this is a producer guideline, not a universal threshold. [5]
  • Biota recommends varied nutritious food and frequent feeding; fish also pick rockwork for algae and sponges. They are territorial with their own species and other dwarf angels. [5]
  • Reef safe with caution: individuals may pick soft corals, clams and fleshy large-polyp stony corals. Feeding does not establish guaranteed coral safety. [5]
  • Shelter-rich rockwork and algae-based food reflect the species wild shelter association and grazing biology; aquarium space and compatibility need individual planning. Never release aquarium fish: USGS identifies probable aquarium release among introduction pathways. [1] [5]
