Plantar Fasciitis — Load at the Heel

An artwork-led foot atlas explaining plantar fascia anatomy, load-related heel pain, assessment, supportive care and important alternative-injury warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • The plantar fascia is a strong band on the underside of the foot connecting the heel to the front of the foot and supporting the arch. [1] [2]
  • Excess loading can irritate and damage the plantar fascia, causing plantar heel pain; the cause is not always clear. [1] [2]
  • Pain typically occurs under the heel or arch, is worse on first steps after rest, may ease with initial movement, and can worsen with prolonged standing or walking. [1] [2]
  • Increased activity, long standing on hard surfaces, tight calves, and poor shoe support can increase risk. [1] [2]
  • A clinician assesses symptoms and examines the foot; imaging may be used to exclude other causes but is not routinely needed. [1]
  • Initial care includes reducing aggravating activity, cushioned supportive shoes or inserts, and gentle calf and plantar fascia stretching; a physiotherapist can guide exercises. [1] [2]
  • Heel spurs are not the cause of plantar fasciitis pain; many people with heel spurs have no pain. [1]
  • Seek clinical assessment for worsening or persistent pain, tingling or loss of feeling, or foot pain with diabetes. [2] [3]
  • Severe heel pain after injury, a snap or pop during injury, or inability to walk warrants urgent assessment and may indicate a different injury rather than plantar fasciitis. [3]
  • This is general medical education and does not diagnose the cause of heel pain; people should not self-diagnose heel pain. [3]
