Asthma — The Narrowing Airway

How variable airway inflammation and narrowing affect breathing, with symptoms, triggers, evaluation and personalized management.

Research behind the infographic

  • Asthma is a long-term condition affecting the lung airways; airways can become inflamed and narrowed, making exhalation harder. [1]
  • An asthma flare can narrow the airway through swelling or tightening; compare a wide airway with a narrowed inflamed airway. [6]
  • Symptoms can include wheeze, cough, shortness of breath and chest tightness and often vary over time. [4]
  • Cold air, pollen, exercise and viral infections may trigger symptoms, with triggers varying by person. [1] [3]
  • Secondhand smoke, dust mites, air pollution and mold are possible triggers for some people. [3]
  • Spirometry measures how much and how fast a person exhales; bronchodilator responsiveness compares airflow before and after medicine that opens airways. [5]
  • A personalized asthma action plan made with a clinician guides monitoring and responses to worsening symptoms. [2]
  • Daily controller medicine is continued in the green/yellow action-plan zones; reliever medicine is added for worsening symptoms according to the personal plan. [2]
  • Severe breathing difficulty or symptoms not relieved by medicines during an attack need emergency help. [6]
  • There is no cure for asthma, but treatment and an action plan can help manage it. [1]
