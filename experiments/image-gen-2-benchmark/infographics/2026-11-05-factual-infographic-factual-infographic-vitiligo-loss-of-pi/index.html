Vitiligo — Loss of Pigment-Producing Cells

Vitiligo pigment-cell anatomy, immune involvement, skin changes and management

Research behind the infographic

  • Vitiligo is a chronic condition with pigment loss caused by loss of working melanocytes, the cells that make melanin. [1] [2]
  • In common non-segmental vitiligo the immune system attacks melanocytes; white patches often appear on both sides, including hands and face. [1] [2]
  • Hair in affected areas can turn white; extent and progression vary. [2]
  • Vitiligo is not an infection and is not contagious. [2]
  • Family history and other autoimmune conditions can be associated with vitiligo. [1] [2]
  • Diagnosis uses skin examination; a Wood lamp can help distinguish pigment loss, and selected patients may need thyroid testing. [2]
  • Affected skin is vulnerable to sunburn; high-SPF sunscreen and sun protection are important. [2]
  • Treatment can include clinician-supervised topical medicines; camouflage is optional. Response varies and no cure is guaranteed. Support for emotional impact can help. [1] [2]
  • New pale patches should be assessed rather than self-diagnosed. [2]
