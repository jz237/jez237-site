Royal gramma

A photorealistic species atlas of diagnostic anatomy, ledge-oriented behavior, plankton feeding, male egg care and cautiously framed aquarium implications.

Research behind the infographic

  • Accepted species Gramma loreto Poey, 1868; royal gramma and fairy basslet are common names, no scientific synonyms reported by USGS. [1] [2]
  • Elongate compressed body, large eye, short snout; purple anterior and yellow posterior with a black spot at front of dorsal fin and yellow lines radiating from the eye; elongated pelvic rays. [1] [2]
  • Reaches approximately 8 cm total length (about 3 inches); western Atlantic and Caribbean reef fish. [1] [2] [3]
  • Lives close to reef ledges, walls, caves and overhangs; may swim upside down with belly facing roof. [1] [3]
  • Feeds primarily on planktonic crustaceans; may also remove ectoparasites from other fishes; not an obligate cleaner. [1] [3]
  • Territorial reef groups have size-based dominance hierarchies; larger fish occupy prime feeding positions. [1] [3]
  • Males build nests in crevices and care for demersal eggs; sexes are separate after differentiation, not routine female-to-male sex change. [1] [3]
  • Aquarium planning implications, not tested minimum standards: provide shelter and feeding access consistent with ledge habitat and plankton diet; territorial behavior means wild aggregations do not imply aquarium group compatibility; small fish and invertebrates can be prey, so reef-safe is not an absolute guarantee. [1] [3]
  • Species has been bred in captivity. Never release aquarium fish: occurrence outside native range has been associated with possible aquarium release as well as natural expansion. [1]
