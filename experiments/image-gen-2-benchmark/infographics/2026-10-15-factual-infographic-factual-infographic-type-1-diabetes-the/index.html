Type 1 Diabetes — The Missing Insulin Signal

How autoimmune beta-cell destruction reduces insulin, raises blood glucose, produces symptoms and requires insulin-based care, with urgent assessment and DKA warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune disease: immune activity destroys insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas, causing little or no insulin. [1]
  • Insulin helps glucose enter body cells for energy; insufficient insulin causes blood glucose to rise. The tissue and glucose symbols are schematic and not to scale. [1]
  • Type 1 diabetes can develop at any age; family history raises risk. [1] [2]
  • Common symptoms include frequent urination, thirst, fatigue, unexplained weight loss and blurred vision. [1] [2]
  • Suspected type 1 diabetes needs urgent medical assessment; diagnosis uses blood glucose tests, with autoantibody tests helping establish the type. [1] [2]
  • Insulin treatment is essential; insulin pens or pumps and regular glucose monitoring support individualized care with a diabetes team. Healthy eating and activity support management but do not replace insulin. [1]
  • Too much insulin can cause low blood glucose, so treatment and monitoring require an individualized care plan. [1]
  • Without enough insulin, fat breakdown produces ketones that can accumulate and cause diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), a medical emergency. [1]
  • Vomiting or abdominal pain, fast deep breathing, fruity breath, sleepiness or confusion may signal DKA; seek emergency care immediately, especially with thirst and frequent urination. [1] [2]
