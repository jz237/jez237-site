Arachnophobia — The Spider at the Curtain Fold

Source-backed educational infographic distinguishing arachnophobia, anticipation and physical symptoms from actual danger, with avoidance and evidence-based professional support.

Research behind the infographic

  • Arachnophobia is fear of spiders, a type of specific phobia. [1] [3]
  • Phobic fear can be intense and out of proportion to actual danger. [1]
  • Seeing the feared object can cause immediate anxiety; anticipation and even thinking about it can also cause anxiety. [1] [3]
  • Some people with arachnophobia avoid touching a spider; some cannot look at a spider picture. [3]
  • Physical symptoms can include palpitations, chest tightness, trembling, dizziness and nausea; experiences vary. [2]
  • People may actively avoid feared objects and organise life around avoidance, limiting daily activities. [1] [3]
  • A repeated encounter can provoke anxiety again; avoidance and intense anxiety are common features, not proof of actual danger. [1] [3]
  • It is not always clear why a phobia develops; frightening experiences and learned fear can be involved. [1] [2]
  • CBT is an effective treatment; exposure therapy is a CBT method particularly effective for phobias. [1]
  • Exposure treatment can involve gradually becoming exposed to the feared object; plans differ and a specialist can help. [3]
  • Breathing exercises and talking to a trusted person may help manage symptoms, and triggering images may be distressing. [2]
  • Seek a primary care or qualified mental health professional when symptoms interfere with life. [1] [2] [3]
