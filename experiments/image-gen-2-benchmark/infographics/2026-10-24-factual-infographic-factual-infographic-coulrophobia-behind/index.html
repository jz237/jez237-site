Coulrophobia — Behind the Painted Smile

An artwork-led clinical-education chart distinguishing the established clown-fear term from a separate diagnosis, qualifying proposed triggers and explaining phobia symptoms, avoidance and evidence-based help.

Research behind the infographic

  • Coulrophobia is an established term for fear of clowns, not a separately named DSM diagnosis. [1]
  • Clown-fear research suggests concealed emotional cues, unpredictable behaviour and frightening media portrayals may contribute; these are proposed contributors, not a universal cause. [1]
  • Anticipatory anxiety means anxiety before an encounter; even thinking about a feared object can provoke anxiety. [3] [4]
  • Phobia symptoms can include palpitations, trembling and feeling sick; symptoms vary between people. [2]
  • Avoidance can limit daily activities and lead people to organise their lives around avoiding the feared object. [3]
  • In specific phobia, intense fear may occur when the object presents little or no actual danger; a strong alarm is not a measurement of danger. [4]
  • CBT is a treatment for phobias; gradual exposure is used for simple phobias, with a plan adapted to the person and specialist help available. [2] [3]
  • Seek professional help if fear interferes with daily life; general education is not a personal diagnosis. [2] [3]
