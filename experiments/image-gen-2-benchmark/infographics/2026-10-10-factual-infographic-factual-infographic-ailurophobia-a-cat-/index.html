Ailurophobia — A Cat Across the Landing

Cat-focused specific phobia illustrated through a fictional stair-landing encounter, with eight source-backed panels and four explanatory diagrams.

Research behind the infographic

  • Ailurophobia means fear of cats and is an animal-focused specific phobia. [2]
  • Phobic fear can be intense and out of proportion to actual danger; symptoms can interfere with daily life. [1] [2] [4]
  • Encountering or thinking about a feared animal can trigger anxiety; an illustrated cat encounter and anticipation are examples, not universal triggers. [1] [2] [4]
  • Phobic thinking can overestimate harm or likelihood of encountering the feared object and underestimate coping; the next event may be imagined as threatening. [4]
  • Body alarm may include pounding heart, shaking, sweating, tight muscles, dizziness and nausea; not everyone experiences the same symptoms. [2] [3] [4]
  • Avoidance can quickly reduce anxiety but prevent learning that a situation can be manageable, maintaining phobic fear. [4]
  • Avoidance can change routes and interfere with social activities and everyday life. [2] [4]
  • Feeling afraid is not itself a measure of actual danger; the depicted cat scene is fictional and does not prove animal safety. [1] [2]
  • CBT and gradual exposure under controlled circumstances at an individual pace can help specific phobias. [1] [2]
  • Breathing exercises and talking to a trusted person can help manage anxiety; seek professional support when fear affects daily life. [1] [2] [3]
  • Treatment illustration is conceptual, not an instruction to approach, touch, restrain or provoke an animal; care planning is individualized. [1] [2]
