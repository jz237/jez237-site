Colorpoints — Pigment Meets Temperature

A focused artistic atlas of temperature-sensitive TYR-associated coat pigmentation, with relative warm-body/cool-point regions and a simplified melanin pathway.

Research behind the infographic

  • Variants in TYR are associated with temperature-sensitive Siamese-type colorpoint pigmentation; this is a pigment-production mechanism, not a surface stain. [1] [2]
  • TYR encodes tyrosinase, an enzyme involved in the initial steps of melanin pigment synthesis. [2]
  • In the Siamese-type pattern, cooler extremities have darker fur, while warmer body regions have lighter fur because pigment production is temperature-sensitive; illustrate face, ears, paws and tail as points, without numerical temperatures or an actual thermal scan. [2]
  • The Siamese-type allele is recessive to the full-color wild-type allele; cats with two copies can express this pointed pattern. Other loci also influence coat appearance. [2] [1]
  • Different TYR variants are associated with Siamese and Burmese pigmentation, and a distinct TYR variant is associated with complete albinism; the pictured point pattern should not be equated to complete pigment absence. [1] [2]
