Coccolithophores — Building Ocean Chalk

Eight-panel source-backed educational plate explaining intracellular coccolith formation, overlapping calcite architecture and marine carbonate pathways.

Research behind the infographic

  • Coccolithophores are unicellular marine planktonic algae; photosynthesis produces organic carbon and calcification produces inorganic carbon. [2]
  • Coccoliths are microscopic calcium carbonate plates made of calcite, CaCO3. [1] [2]
  • Coccoliths are formed inside the cell, generally one at a time, in a compartment called the coccolith vesicle. [2]
  • Coccolith formation begins with a protococcolith ring at the periphery of an organic base-plate scale. [2]
  • Noelaerhabdaceae coccoliths have multiple calcite crystal segments radially distributed around a central grid; they have proximal and distal shields. [2]
  • Completed coccoliths are extruded from the cell to form an overlapping external covering called a coccosphere. [2]
  • Additional coccoliths enter surrounding seawater when cells multiply, die or produce excess plates. [1]
  • Dense clouds of coccoliths can give bloom waters an opaque turquoise appearance. [1]
  • Coccolith carbonate contributes to seafloor sediment; do not imply all plates reach the bottom or portray a net climate benefit. [1]
  • Coccolith size and mass vary among species and even within a single coccosphere; the study used three-dimensional X-ray imaging to separate plates and measure geometry. [2]
  • Coccolithophores inhabit the upper ocean; the plates and cells illustrated are microscopic, strongly enlarged and not shown to a common scale. [1] [2]
