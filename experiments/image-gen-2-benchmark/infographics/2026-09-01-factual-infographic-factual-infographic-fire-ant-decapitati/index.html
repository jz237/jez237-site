Fire-Ant Decapitating Fly — The Head-Capsule Nursery

An insect-first direct-canvas museum infographic showing the real fire-ant decapitating fly Pseudacteon tricuspis, its rapid aerial oviposition attack, larval migration from thorax to head, and transformation of the detached ant head capsule into a puparial chamber.

Research behind the infographic

  • Pseudacteon tricuspis is a real phorid fly species whose larvae are solitary parasitoids of imported fire-ant workers, including the red imported fire ant Solenopsis invicta. [1] [2]
  • The adult female is a small hump-backed fly with reduced wing venation and a specialized external ovipositor. [1] [2]
  • A female can hover several millimeters above a fire-ant worker and make a rapid aerial strike that inserts an egg into the worker's thorax. [1] [2]
  • After hatching, the first instar develops in the thorax; a later instar migrates into the ant's head. [1] [2]
  • The third instar consumes tissue in the head capsule before pupariation, killing the worker. [1] [2]
  • Near pupariation, the late larva appears to release a substance, likely an enzyme, that degrades membranes holding the ant exoskeleton together and allows the head to detach. [1] [2]
  • The larva uses the detached ant head capsule as a protective chamber for pupariation; its hardened anterior plate occupies the oral opening and paired respiratory structures can extend from the puparium. [1]
  • The adult fly eventually emerges through the detached ant head capsule. [1] [2]
  • Pseudacteon flies are much smaller than their fire-ant hosts, so the infographic should show the fly hovering close to the ant rather than at monster scale. [1] [2]
  • The presence of attacking Pseudacteon flies can interrupt imported fire-ant foraging by triggering defensive or evasive behavior, but this does not imply universal or complete colony control. [1] [2]
  • Pseudacteon tricuspis is native to South America and was introduced in the United States as a biological-control agent against imported fire ants. [1] [2]
