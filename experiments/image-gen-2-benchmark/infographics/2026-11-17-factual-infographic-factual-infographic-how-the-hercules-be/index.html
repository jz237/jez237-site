How the Hercules Beetle Uses Its Horns

How the Hercules beetle Dynastes hercules uses horns in male contests and develops in decaying wood

Research behind the infographic

  • Dynastes hercules is a scarab beetle; males bear head and thoracic horns, females lack horns. [1]
  • Male horns squeeze and lift rivals in contests for access to females. [1]
  • Male elytra can be olive with dark spots; females are usually black. [1]
  • Larvae feed on dead wood in decaying logs; adults generally feed on fallen rotting fruit. [1]
  • The species undergoes complete metamorphosis: egg, larva, pupa, adult. [1]
  • Its range includes southern Mexico to Bolivia and the Lesser Antilles, including rainforest. [1]
  • Generalized insect section includes heart, gut, nerve cord, spiracle and trachea. [2]
