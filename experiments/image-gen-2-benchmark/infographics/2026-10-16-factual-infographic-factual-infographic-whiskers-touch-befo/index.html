Whiskers — Touch Before the Muzzle Arrives

Focused feline whisker contact mechanics, receptor location and qualitative adaptation responses.

Research behind the infographic

  • Cat maxillary whisker movement evokes impulses in sensory afferent fibres of the infraorbital nerve. Contact bends the shaft and mechanical deformation reaches the follicle, activating sensory receptors. [1] [2]
  • Sensory receptors are at the whisker base within the follicle, not distributed along the exposed shaft; depict no nerves or sensors in the exposed whisker. [2]
  • Cat whisker afferents include slowly adapting responses to maintained displacement and rapidly adapting responses to whisker movement. Diagrammatic traces are qualitative, without invented quantitative scales. [1]
  • Whiskers allow tactile contact with surroundings; the illustrated whisker touches an object before the muzzle reaches it, not remote sensing without physical input. [1] [2]
  • The follicle cutaway is a simplified schematic, not to scale. Avoid claiming a particular intrafollicular S-bend is demonstrated in cats: the Northwestern simulation used rat data. [2]
