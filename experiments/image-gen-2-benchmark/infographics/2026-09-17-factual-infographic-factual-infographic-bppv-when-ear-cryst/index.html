BPPV — When Ear Crystals Enter a Balance Canal

A source-verified medical anatomy infographic explaining vestibular anatomy, displaced otoconia, position-triggered vertigo, clinical positional testing, canalith repositioning, fall safety, and urgent neurological warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • The semicircular canals are three fluid-filled ducts arranged roughly at right angles; fluid motion bends the cupula and sensory stereocilia to signal head rotation. [1]
  • The utricle and saccule contain calcium-carbonate otoconia that help signal head position and linear movement relative to gravity. [1]
  • In BPPV, loose otoconia can enter a semicircular canal and produce incorrect positional signals that cause brief, intense vertigo with particular head movements. [1] [2]
  • Common provoking movements include rolling over in bed, bending down, and looking up or over a shoulder. [1] [2]
  • Posterior-canal BPPV is the most common canal form, and diagnosis typically uses a head-hanging positional test that looks for characteristic positional nystagmus. [2]
  • Canalith-repositioning maneuvers such as the Epley maneuver use a sequence of head and body positions to move displaced particles out of the canal and back toward the utricle. [1] [2]
  • Therapeutic repositioning maneuvers have better outcomes than no treatment or sham maneuvers, although symptoms may persist or recur and some people need repeat treatment. [1] [2]
  • Because dizziness has many causes, evaluation should distinguish BPPV from other inner-ear, medication-related, cardiovascular, and brain causes; this infographic is general education, not diagnosis. [1]
  • Sudden dizziness or loss of balance accompanied by trouble walking, vision change, one-sided weakness or numbness, confusion, speech difficulty, or sudden severe headache can be a stroke warning and requires immediate emergency help. [3]
  • Fall-risk precautions and individualized clinical guidance are appropriate when dizziness makes walking, stairs, bathing, driving, or exercise unsafe. [1]
