Pajama Cardinalfish — The Twilight Coral Refuge

A photorealistic species atlas linking diagnostic markings with branching-coral shelter, nocturnal crustacean feeding and careful aquarium guidance.

Research behind the infographic

  • Accepted marine species Sphaeramia nematoptera (Bleeker, 1856), family Apogonidae; Apogon nematopterus is an unaccepted synonym of this same species. [4] [5]
  • Common names Pajama cardinalfish and Coral cardinalfish; yellow head, two dorsal fins, vertical black bar beneath the first dorsal fin, large brown spots on the posterior body; large red eyes. [1] [2]
  • Maximum reported length 8.5 cm, about 3.3 inches. [1] [2]
  • Western Pacific: lagoons, bays and sheltered coastal coral reefs; Oregon records depths to 14 m. [1] [2]
  • Lives among branching corals including Porites cylindrica, Acropora and Anacropora; forms social groups, hides by day and leaves coral protection to feed at night. [1] [2]
  • Carnivore feeding on small shrimps, crabs and other small crustaceans. [1] [2]
  • Male incubates fertilized eggs in his mouth and does not feed during incubation; no precise incubation duration asserted. [1] [2]
  • Aquarium guidance: generally peaceful towards other tankmates and described as reef safe; this does not imply safety for every small crustacean, since crustaceans are natural prey. Groups can develop dominance hierarchies and need a larger aquarium with ample cover. [3] [1]
  • Aquarium feeding: varied meaty diet; some initially refuse dry foods, can be coaxed with frozen food and later weaned to dry; try nighttime feeding for reluctant feeders. [3]
  • Coralife lists a 20 US gallon minimum (approximately 76 liters using 3.78541 liters per US gallon); not presented as a universal group-housing recommendation; groups need more room and cover. [3]
