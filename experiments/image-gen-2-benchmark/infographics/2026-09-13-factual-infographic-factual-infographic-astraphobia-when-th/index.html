Astraphobia — When Thunder Becomes a Threat

A detailed real-world educational infographic explaining astraphobia, its thunder-and-lightning triggers, immediate anxiety and physical symptoms, avoidance feedback, the distinction between perceived and actual danger, and evidence-based professional help.

Research behind the infographic

  • Astraphobia is an intense fear of thunder and lightning and is also called brontophobia. [1]
  • Astraphobia can affect children and can persist into adulthood. [1]
  • People with astraphobia may feel extreme anxiety while preparing for a thunderstorm, repeatedly monitor weather reports, or have panic attacks during a storm. [1]
  • A specific phobia involves intense fear or anxiety about an object or situation that is out of proportion to the actual danger. [2]
  • Encountering a phobic trigger can produce immediate intense anxiety, and people may take active steps to avoid the feared situation. [2]
  • Phobia symptoms can include severe anxiety, dizziness, palpitations, chest tightness, nausea, trembling, and fear of fainting or losing control. [3]
  • Avoidance can keep people from activities and situations associated with the feared trigger. [2]
  • Cognitive behavioral therapy is an established treatment for phobias, and exposure therapy is a CBT method that confronts feared situations to help people re-engage with activities they have avoided. [2]
  • Exposure therapy may be paired with relaxation exercises and should be presented as a supported treatment pathway rather than a self-directed high-intensity challenge. [2]
  • A person should seek professional help when phobia symptoms are affecting daily life. [2] [3]
