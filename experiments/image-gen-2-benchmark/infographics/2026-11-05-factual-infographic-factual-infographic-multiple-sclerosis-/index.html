Multiple Sclerosis — Myelin and Signal Disruption

Central nervous system demyelination, symptoms, diagnosis, management and stroke-related warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • Multiple sclerosis is an immune-mediated disorder affecting the central nervous system: brain and spinal cord; immune attacks damage myelin around axons and may damage axons themselves, disrupting communication. [1] [2]
  • Myelin is the protective coating around nerve fibers; demyelinated lesions can occur in the brain and spinal cord and can be seen with MRI. [1] [2]
  • Symptoms vary by person and lesion location: fatigue, visual problems, numbness or tingling, muscle weakness or stiffness, and balance problems can occur. [1] [2]
  • Some people have relapses and remissions; others experience progressive worsening. The course varies and cannot be predicted from a single illustration. [1] [2]
  • The exact cause is unclear; genetic susceptibility and environmental factors contribute, and smoking and prior Epstein-Barr virus infection are associated with increased risk, not certainty of disease. [1] [2]
  • No single test diagnoses MS: a neurologist combines history and examination with MRI and sometimes spinal fluid or other tests, excluding other causes. [1] [2]
  • There is no current cure. Disease-modifying therapies can reduce relapses and slow progression for appropriate patients; treatment depends on disease type and individual needs. [1] [2]
  • Corticosteroids may speed recovery from a relapse but do not change the long-term course; physiotherapy, tailored exercise, fatigue management and symptom medicines can help. [1] [2]
  • Sudden arm weakness or numbness, sudden vision loss or sudden balance problems can signal stroke and require emergency medical help; do not assume they are MS. [2]
  • MS-like symptoms can have other causes and should be checked by a clinician; general education is not diagnosis. [1] [2]
