Driving Fear — Alarm Behind the Wheel

An artwork-led educational overview of driving fear, associated symptoms and avoidance, clinical context, real road safety, and evidence-based support.

Research behind the infographic

  • Driving fear can involve intense fear and avoidance of driving; specific phobia is one possible clinical explanation, while PTSD and other presentations also occur. [1] [3]
  • In a phobia, fear is out of proportion to actual danger; this does not mean driving is risk-free. [1]
  • Anticipating feared situations can cause excessive worry; highways and unfamiliar routes may be avoided by people with driving fear. [1] [3]
  • Body alarm can include a racing or noticeable heartbeat, trembling and dizziness. [2]
  • Anxiety and avoidance can restrict driving in general or particular routes; these are characteristic responses, not a prediction of a crash. [1] [3]
  • CBT is an established treatment for phobias, and exposure is a particularly effective CBT method for phobias generally. [1] [2]
  • Driving-fear care can use an individually planned anxiety hierarchy and professional assessment; simulator exposure has promising but limited pilot evidence, not a guaranteed cure. [3]
  • Seek a clinician when symptoms affect life; professional assessment can distinguish specific phobia and trauma-related symptoms. [1] [2] [3]
  • Safety framing: clinician-guided treatment is not a directive to practise alone in traffic or ignore road hazards; the cited driving study used assessment and professionally supervised driving. [3]
