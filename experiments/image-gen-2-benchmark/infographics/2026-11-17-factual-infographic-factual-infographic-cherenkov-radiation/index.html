Cherenkov Radiation — Outrunning Light in Water

An artwork-led physics atlas connecting a glowing reactor pool to the particle-and-medium mechanism, wave analogies and measurement uses.

Research behind the infographic

  • Cherenkov radiation occurs when an electrically charged particle travels faster than light travels in the surrounding transparent medium. [1] [2]
  • Light travels more slowly in water than in vacuum; this does not allow a particle to exceed the speed of light in vacuum. [1] [2]
  • High-energy electrons are an example of charged particles that can produce Cherenkov radiation. [1] [2]
  • The moving charged particle interacts with the medium, and the emitted electromagnetic waves reinforce into a light shock wave. [1] [2]
  • Cherenkov emission can produce the blue or violet glow seen in water around a nuclear reactor core. [1] [2]
  • The process can be compared with a sonic boom or a boat bow wave; these are analogies, not identical phenomena. [1] [2]
  • Cherenkov light is used in nuclear-material accounting and in particle-physics measurements of particle energy and trajectory. [1]
