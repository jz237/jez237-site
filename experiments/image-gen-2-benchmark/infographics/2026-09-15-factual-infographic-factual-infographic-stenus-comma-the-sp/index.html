Stenus comma — The Spring-Loaded Sticky Lip

Species identity, waterside habitat, diagnostic adult form, scale, predatory role, and the genus-defining haemolymph-driven labial prey-capture apparatus with adhesive paraglossal pads.

Research behind the infographic

  • Stenus comma is an accepted species of rove beetle in the family Staphylinidae; no English common name is asserted. [2] [3]
  • The adult is approximately 5.5-6 mm long, primarily dark, with black legs and conspicuous orange spots on the elytra. [3]
  • Stenus comma is associated with the edges of water bodies and is fairly widespread in England and Wales. [3]
  • Stenus species are obligate predators in ground-based microhabitats and vegetation and prey particularly on springtails. [1]
  • All Stenus share large, globular, protruding compound eyes associated with active predation. [1]
  • The Stenus prey-capture apparatus is an elongated labium projected toward prey by haemolymph pressure. [1]
  • At the tip of the labium, the paraglossae are modified into sticky pads that produce adhesive secretion. [1]
  • A scientifically conservative mechanism sequence may show prey detection, labial projection, adhesive-pad contact, and prey retrieval toward the mouth, without an exact speed label. [1]
  • Predaceous Stenus beetles have published life-history variation associated with waterside environments; species-specific generation duration is not established here and is omitted. [4]
