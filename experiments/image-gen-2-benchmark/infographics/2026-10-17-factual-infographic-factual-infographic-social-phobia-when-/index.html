Social Phobia — When Attention Feels Like a Spotlight

Artwork-led clinical overview distinguishing social anxiety disorder from shyness, illustrating triggers, variable physical symptoms, avoidance and evidence-based care.

Research behind the infographic

  • Social anxiety disorder, also called social phobia, is persistent overwhelming fear of social situations involving possible scrutiny, negative judgment, humiliation or rejection; it is more than ordinary shyness and can impair daily life. [1] [2]
  • Possible triggers include meeting new people, speaking publicly and eating while others are present; some people have anxiety mainly during performance. [1] [2]
  • Anticipatory worry before social events and self-critical review afterward can occur. [1] [2]
  • Symptoms may include blushing, sweating, trembling, rapid heartbeat, nausea and the mind going blank; responses differ among people. [1] [2]
  • Fearful predictions about others judging or rejecting a person may be distorted or untrue; anxiety is not proof that rejection is occurring. [1]
  • Avoiding feared social situations can feel helpful briefly, but anxiety is likely to remain without treatment. [1]
  • CBT with a therapist is a well-supported treatment, addressing patterns of thinking and behavior; graduated exposure is a CBT method that progressively approaches feared activities. [1] [2]
  • Treatment is tailored with a clinician; medication such as an SSRI may also help some people. [1] [2]
  • Seek professional assessment when anxiety disrupts school, work, relationships or daily life; an educational chart is not a diagnosis. [1] [2]
