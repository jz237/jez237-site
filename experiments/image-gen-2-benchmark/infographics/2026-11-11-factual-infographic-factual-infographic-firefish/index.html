Firefish

Current-facing plankton feeding, distinctive dorsal fin and burrow refuge biology, with explicitly inferred aquarium planning and no blanket reef-safety guarantee.

Research behind the infographic

  • Nemateleotris magnifica is the accepted marine species; Nemateleotris magnificus and Nemaeleotris magnifica are unaccepted spelling aliases returned by WoRMS, included in duplicate protection, not distinct species. [5] [6] [4]
  • Firefish / Red Firegoby has an elongate white first dorsal fin, yellow snout, white head, and white body grading to bright orange-red posteriorly. [1]
  • Coral-reef fish of tropical Indo-Pacific and Central Pacific, including East Africa to Pitcairn; outer reef slopes at 6–61 m. [1] [4] [3]
  • Clearwater Marine Aquarium gives maximum size up to 3 inches (about 7.6 cm); this is an approximate reported upper size, not a precise universal adult measurement. [3]
  • Often hovers within 0.5 m above the bottom, facing into current to eat zooplankton, especially copepods and other crustacean larvae. [1] [3]
  • Uses substrate holes as burrows and retreats when disturbed; adults solitary or paired, while multiple juveniles may share a hole. [1] [3]
  • Raises and waves elongated first dorsal fin; aquarium/university sources describe fin signaling. [1] [2] [3]
  • Aquarium recommendations inferred from cited biology: supply accessible refuges and appropriately small planktonic foods; avoid large predators (Clearwater lists triggerfish, lionfish and large angelfish as threats). Wild pairing is not a guarantee of arbitrary group compatibility; plankton feeding alone does not establish universal reef safety. No numeric tank minimum or blanket coral/invertebrate safety guarantee is asserted. [1] [3] [2]
