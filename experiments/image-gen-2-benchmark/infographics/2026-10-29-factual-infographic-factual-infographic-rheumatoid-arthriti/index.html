Rheumatoid Arthritis — An Inflamed Joint Lining

An anatomical guide to rheumatoid synovial inflammation, symptoms, clinical evaluation, individualized disease-modifying treatment and urgent joint-infection warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • RA is a chronic autoimmune disease in which the immune system mistakenly attacks the lining of joints, causing inflammation, pain, stiffness and swelling. [2] [3]
  • Joint inflammation can over time damage cartilage and nearby bone; the schematic joint shows lining, cartilage and bone, not inevitable damage in every patient. [3]
  • Small joints in hands, wrists and feet are commonly affected; symptoms often occur symmetrically on both sides, but presentations vary. [1] [2]
  • Common symptoms include tender warm swollen joints, prolonged stiffness after waking or rest, difficulty making a fist and fatigue. [1]
  • The immune trigger is not fully understood; smoking and family history are risk factors, not guarantees of disease. [3]
  • Early clinical evaluation matters; diagnosis uses examination, blood tests and imaging rather than symptoms alone. [2] [3]
  • Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment can ease symptoms and reduce joint damage; no current cure. [2] [3]
  • Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) are commonly prescribed; a rheumatologist individualizes treatment and follow-up. [2]
  • Low-impact exercise and physiotherapy or occupational therapy can support strength, mobility and daily activities; supplements do not replace RA medicines. [2] [3]
  • Sudden severe joint pain with swelling, difficult movement or fever needs urgent assessment for possible joint infection, not an assumption of an RA flare; fever may be absent. [4]
  • General education is distinct from professional medical diagnosis and individualized treatment. [2]
