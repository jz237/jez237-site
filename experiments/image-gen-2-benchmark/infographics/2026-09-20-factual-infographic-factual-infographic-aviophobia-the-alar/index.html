Research behind the infographic

  • Fear of flying is an example of a specific phobia, an intense fear or anxiety focused on a particular situation. [1]
  • In a phobia, fear may be out of proportion to the actual danger posed by the situation. [1] [2]
  • Thinking about or anticipating a feared situation can produce anxiety before the person encounters it. [1] [2]
  • Encountering a phobic trigger can produce immediate intense anxiety and physical alarm symptoms such as rapid heartbeat, shortness of breath, sweating, trembling, nausea, dizziness, muscle tension, or feeling on edge. [1] [2]
  • People may actively avoid a feared situation or endure it with intense anxiety, and avoidance can interfere with daily life and activities. [1] [2]
  • Fear responds to an immediate perceived threat, while anxiety can involve anticipation of a future concern. [2]
  • Psychotherapy is the primary treatment approach for phobias, and cognitive behavioral therapy teaches ways of thinking, behaving, and reacting that can reduce fear and anxiety. [1] [2]
  • Exposure therapy is a CBT method for phobias that focuses on confronting feared situations so people can re-engage with activities they have avoided; this should be presented as gradual and professionally guided, not as forced exposure. [1] [2]
  • A primary care provider can refer someone with mental-health concerns to a qualified mental-health professional for assessment and next steps. [1]
