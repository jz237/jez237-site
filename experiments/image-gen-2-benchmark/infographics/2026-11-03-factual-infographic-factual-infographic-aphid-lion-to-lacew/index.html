Aphid Lion to Lacewing

The predatory larva, life cycle, and adult of the green lacewing Chrysoperla rufilabris.

Research behind the infographic

  • Chrysoperla rufilabris is a green lacewing found in North America; humid habitats include irrigated crops and greenhouses. [1]
  • Adults are pale green, with golden eyes, long antennae and transparent wings; adult size is roughly 12–20 mm. [1]
  • Eggs are laid singly on silken stalks on foliage. [1] [2]
  • Larvae have three instars, legs and large curved paired mouthparts; they impale aphids and suck body contents. [1] [2]
  • Mature larvae spin a round silken cocoon; the pupa develops before the adult emerges. [1] [2]
  • Chrysoperla adults feed on nectar, pollen and honeydew while larvae are predators. [1] [2]
  • Generalized insect body plan: spiracles admit air into branching tracheae; the alimentary canal carries food and the ventral nerve cord runs along the body. [3]
