Hymenopus coronatus — A Flower on a Leaf

Juvenile female orchid mantis morphology and visual predatory deception on foliage, including documented bee approach and capture.

Research behind the infographic

  • Hymenopus coronatus is an orchid mantis (Mantodea: Hymenopodidae), a predatory insect with six legs and raptorial forelegs. [1] [3]
  • The species is reported from tropical Southeast Asian habitats including Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia; observed juvenile mantises rested or hunted on leaves. [1] [3]
  • The white and pink orchid mantis has broad abdomen and petal-like lobes on the mid- and hind-leg femora, producing a generalized flowerlike silhouette rather than copying one particular orchid species. [1] [2] [3]
  • The petal-like femoral lobes have bilaterally symmetric shape patterns in juvenile and adult females. [2]
  • Orchid mantis body white primarily absorbs ultraviolet light; visual comparison found low color contrast among body parts and can make the insect resemble UV-absorbing flowers to hymenopteran observers. [2] [3]
  • In field observations, a juvenile mantis on foliage contrasted with nearby leaves in ultraviolet-sensitive imaging. [3]
  • Field observations found juvenile females on leaves and adult females in some cases on flowers; juveniles bend the abdomen upward while adult wings constrain that posture. [3]
  • Oriental honeybees were observed approaching juvenile female mantises as though to land on a flower; juveniles captured visiting bees with their predatory forelegs. [3]
  • The depicted sequence is approach to flowerlike visual signal, hover near juvenile mantis, then foreleg capture; this is observed behavior, not nectar exchange or pollination by the mantis. [3]
  • Juvenile and adult female forms differ in wing development and posture; a small stage comparison is qualitative and not a timed life-cycle chart. [3]
