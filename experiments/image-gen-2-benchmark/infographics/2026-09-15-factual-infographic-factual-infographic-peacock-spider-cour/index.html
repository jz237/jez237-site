Peacock Spider Courtship — Signal in Motion

A direct-canvas educational plate explaining the anatomy, visual and vibratory channels, observed behavioral elements, measurement methods, receiver perspective, and cautious interpretation of Maratus volans courtship.

Research behind the infographic

  • Maratus volans males use a multi-modal courtship display combining visual signals with substrate-borne vibratory signals. [1] [2]
  • During courtship, a male can unfurl brightly colored flaps around the abdomen and display them toward a female. [1]
  • The expanded abdominal display is coordinated with movements of the ornamented third pair of legs. [1]
  • Researchers characterized courtship movement with video and measured substrate vibration with laser vibrometry. [1] [2]
  • The documented courtship repertoire contains distinct, stereotyped behavioral elements rather than one continuous undifferentiated dance. [1]
  • Pedipalp flickering can appear during courtship but was also observed in other contexts, so it is not necessarily courtship-specific. [1]
  • Male abdominal bobbing is associated with vibratory signaling, making visible motion and substrate vibration linked parts of the display. [1]
  • Experimental mating trials found male mating success depended on a combination of visual and vibratory displays. [2]
  • The evidence concerns controlled observations and mating trials, so the infographic should not imply that every female responds identically or that one display element guarantees mating. [1] [2]
