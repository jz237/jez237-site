GERD — The Reflux Pathway

Anatomical cutaway of the esophagus–stomach junction showing reflux mechanism, symptoms, associated factors, evaluation, treatment categories, and warning signs.

Research behind the infographic

  • The lower esophageal sphincter and diaphragm form barriers at the esophagus-stomach junction that usually limit backflow of stomach contents. [1] [4]
  • GERD can develop when the lower esophageal sphincter is weak or relaxes inappropriately, permitting stomach contents to flow into the esophagus. [1] [4]
  • Reflux is movement of stomach contents upward, not necessarily excess stomach acid production. [4]
  • Common symptoms include heartburn behind the breastbone and regurgitation into the throat or mouth; not everyone has both. [1] [4]
  • GERD can also present with swallowing difficulty or pain, chronic cough, or hoarseness; these symptoms are not specific to GERD. [1]
  • Obesity, pregnancy, smoking, and hiatal hernia can increase GERD likelihood or symptoms; not every person with these factors develops GERD. [1]
  • Clinicians often evaluate symptoms and history first; tests may be used for possible complications, alternative causes, or persistent symptoms. [2] [4]
  • Upper GI endoscopy uses a flexible camera to inspect the esophagus and upper digestive tract and can check for complications or other problems. [2] [4]
  • Esophageal pH monitoring measures reflux-related acid exposure in the esophagus using a catheter or wireless capsule. [2] [4]
  • Selected lifestyle steps may include weight loss when appropriate, avoiding an individual trigger, changing meal timing, elevating the head for nocturnal symptoms, and smoking cessation. [3] [4]
  • Antacids may relieve mild symptoms, while H2 blockers and proton pump inhibitors reduce stomach acid production; medication choices require clinical context. [3] [4]
  • Persistent vomiting, swallowing difficulty, bleeding signs, unexplained weight loss, or chest pain warrant medical evaluation; chest pain should not be assumed to be reflux. [1] [4]
