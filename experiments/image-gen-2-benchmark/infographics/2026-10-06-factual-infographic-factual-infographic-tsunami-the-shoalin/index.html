Tsunami — The Shoaling Water Column

Source-backed mechanism plate on tsunami shoaling and coastal water-column change.

Research behind the infographic

  • Tsunamis propagate through the entire water depth, unlike wind waves whose motion is concentrated near the surface. [1]
  • In deep ocean water, a tsunami has a long wavelength and often small surface height; it may be barely noticeable. [1] [2]
  • Tsunami propagation speed depends on water depth: deeper water permits faster propagation; shallower water slows it. [1] [2]
  • For a long wave, propagation speed c is approximated by sqrt(g h), where g is gravitational acceleration and h water depth. [1]
  • As a tsunami enters shallower water, its wavelength decreases. [1] [2]
  • Wave speed equals frequency times wavelength; slowing propagation does not itself change frequency, so wavelength becomes shorter. [2]
  • Shoaling is the increase in wave amplitude as a wave moves into shallow water; tsunami waves can grow much taller near the coast. [1] [2]
  • Currents intensify as tsunamis approach shallow coastal water. [1]
  • Even after shoaling, tsunami wavelengths remain relatively long; water can continue flowing inland for many minutes. [2]
  • Run-up refers to the maximum height reached inland above normal sea level, distinct from horizontal inundation extent. [2]
