How Volcanic Plumes Make Lightning

A direct-canvas museum infographic tracing how ash fragmentation, particle collisions and clustering, charge separation, mixed-phase ice processes, and plume dynamics can combine to produce volcanic lightning and support eruption monitoring.

Research behind the infographic

  • Lightning is often observed during explosive volcanic eruptions and is commonly associated with ash-plume formation. [1]
  • Volcanic lightning can occur in the gas-thrust region near the vent, the convecting rising column, and the spreading umbrella region. [1]
  • Collisions and clustering among ash particles can generate and concentrate electrical charge in volcanic plumes. [1] [2]
  • Magma fragmentation, particle convection and buoyancy, and hydrometeor formation can all contribute to plume electrification. [1]
  • Size-dependent triboelectrification of colliding ash particles is a principal volcanic-plume charging pathway. [2]
  • Collisions among in-plume hydrometeors can add an ice-related charging pathway when water and ice form aloft. [1] [2]
  • Turbulence can preferentially concentrate particles into denser patches, increasing collision opportunities and favorable conditions for charge concentration. [2]
  • Volcanic lightning patterns and rates can provide monitoring information about otherwise obscured plume activity, although the relationship depends on plume conditions and charging mechanisms. [2]
