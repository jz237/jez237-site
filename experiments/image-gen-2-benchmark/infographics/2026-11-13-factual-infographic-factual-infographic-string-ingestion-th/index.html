String Ingestion — The Linear Foreign-Body Hazard

Anchoring, intestinal plication, potential perforation, signs and urgent veterinary assessment of swallowed string in cats.

Research behind the infographic

  • Swallowed string can act as a linear foreign body in cats and cause intestinal obstruction. [2] [1]
  • String can anchor around the base of the tongue or at the pylorus (stomach outlet). [2]
  • Peristaltic movement gathers the intestine into pleats along the anchored string; this is plication. [2]
  • The string can erode through the intestinal wall, causing leakage and life-threatening peritonitis. [2] [1]
  • Vomiting, appetite loss and abdominal pain are possible signs; presentation varies. [1] [3]
  • Suspected foreign-object ingestion needs immediate veterinary care; veterinarians use history, examination and imaging to assess it. [3]
  • Some upper digestive foreign bodies can be removed endoscopically; linear foreign bodies often require surgery, and severely damaged bowel may require resection. [1] [2]
