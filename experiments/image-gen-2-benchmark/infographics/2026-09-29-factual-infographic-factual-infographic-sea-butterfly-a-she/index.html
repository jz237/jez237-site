Sea Butterfly — A Shell in Changing Seawater

How Limacina helicina swims with a modified foot, captures food with mucus, and shows aragonite-shell dissolution under low-saturation conditions.

Research behind the infographic

  • Shelled pteropods are planktonic marine snails; sea-butterfly swimming wings are a modified gastropod foot. [1] [2]
  • Limacina helicina has a delicate aragonitic calcium-carbonate shell with an outer periostracum. [2]
  • Limacina helicina uses a mucus web to collect suspended food; shelled pteropods can capture plankton particles in this web. [1] [2]
  • Pteropods are prey in marine food webs, including fish in the North Pacific. [2]
  • Absorbed carbon dioxide changes seawater carbonate chemistry and reduces carbonate-ion availability and aragonite saturation. [2]
  • When aragonite saturation is below one, dissolution of calcium carbonate is favored; shell building may still occur while exposure damages existing shell. [2]
  • Puget Sound experiments with L. helicina found greater shell dissolution under aragonite-undersaturated conditions than oversaturated treatments. [2]
  • California Current field sampling found severe pteropod-shell damage correlated with the extent of aragonite-undersaturated habitat. [3]
  • Observed shell damage does not establish that all exposed animals die; survival effects varied by treatment in the Puget Sound experiment. [2]
  • Coastal upwelling can bring carbon-dioxide-rich, low-saturation water toward the surface in the northeastern Pacific. [2]
