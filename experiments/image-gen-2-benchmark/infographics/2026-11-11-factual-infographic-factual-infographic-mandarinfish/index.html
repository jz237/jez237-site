Mandarinfish

Photorealistic species atlas connecting benthic anatomy and microprey feeding to qualified aquarium care.

Research behind the infographic

  • Accepted species Synchiropus splendidus; synonyms Callionymus splendidus, Neosynchiropus splendidus and Pterosynchiropus splendidus. [3] [4]
  • Western Pacific tropical coral reefs and shallow lagoons; bottom dwelling among coral and rubble. [1] [2]
  • Small adult fish; Aquarium of the Pacific reports 6–9 cm (2.4–3.5 inches); ADW reports maximum 6 cm, so do not claim a universal maximum. [2] [1]
  • Broad depressed head, small mouth, blue-green and orange wavy markings, male elongated first dorsal spine and fanlike pelvic fins used at the bottom. [1] [2]
  • Scaleless skin with a thick mucus coating; secretion may deter predators. Do not depict invented scales or injection apparatus. [1]
  • Daytime bottom feeding on small crustaceans and other tiny invertebrates, including copepods and worms; food from substrate/live rock. [1] [2]
  • Usually shy and mostly passive, but aquarium males can fight; avoid pairing males. [1]
  • Aquarium feeding can be difficult; many individuals do not accept prepared food. Husbandry implication: verify sustained intake of appropriately tiny food, not mere presence of live rock; small body size does not mean easy care. [1]
  • Reef compatibility is diet-specific: tiny invertebrates are prey; do not promise universal reef safety. This caution is an inference from documented diet, not a blanket safety classification. [1] [2]
