Snow Crystals — A Cloud History Written in Ice

A detailed atmospheric-microphysics plate explaining ice-crystal nucleation, vapor growth, sixfold structure, condition-dependent morphology, branching, path history, and aggregation.

Research behind the infographic

  • Snow crystals form in below-freezing clouds around tiny airborne particles. [1] [2]
  • Water vapor joins the growing ice crystal as it falls through the cloud. [1]
  • The organization of water molecules in ice produces the crystal's six-sided structure. [1] [2]
  • Temperature and atmospheric moisture conditions influence whether snow crystals grow as plates, columns, prisms, needles, or branched forms. [1] [2]
  • Changes in temperature or moisture along a crystal's path can change how its arms grow and branch while preserving sixfold organization. [1]
  • The arms of one crystal can resemble one another because they experience the same local atmospheric conditions as the crystal grows. [1]
  • A snowflake may be one ice crystal or an aggregate of multiple ice crystals fused together. [2]
  • Different paths through a cloud expose crystals to different growth histories, so their detailed forms vary. [1]
