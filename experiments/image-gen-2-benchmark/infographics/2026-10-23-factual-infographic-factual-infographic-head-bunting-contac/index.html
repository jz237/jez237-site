Head Bunting — Contact and Scent

Facial scent marking and the contextual social interpretation of head bunting, with scent-region and contact studies.

Research behind the infographic

  • Bunting is a cat approaching and rubbing its head on a person; cats can also bunt one another. [1] [2]
  • Cats have scent glands around their cheeks, lips and forehead; facial rubbing deposits chemical signals. [1]
  • Pheromones are chemical signals used in communication between members of the same species. [1]
  • Cats rub their faces on familiar objects including doorways; facial scent marking occurs in places where cats are comfortable. [1]
  • Bunting toward people is interpreted as social affiliation and scent marking, not proof of a specific thought or a universal emotional meaning. [2] [1]
  • We cannot directly know a cat's thoughts; engagement, seeking company and other behavior provide context for interpreting contentment. [2]
  • Allorubbing is when two cats rub their bodies against each other; this is related to but distinct from a head bunt. [1]
