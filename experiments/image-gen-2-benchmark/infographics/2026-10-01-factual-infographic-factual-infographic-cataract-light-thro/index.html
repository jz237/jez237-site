Cataract — Light Through a Clouded Lens

Source-backed medical infographic explaining cataract anatomy, symptoms, risk factors, diagnosis, early functional aids, surgery and separate retinal-detachment warnings.

Research behind the infographic

  • A cataract is clouding of the natural lens; the normally clear lens bends incoming light to help vision. [1] [2]
  • The cornea, iris and lens are front-of-eye structures; the pupil is widened by dilation, and the retina and optic nerve are examined at the back of the eye. [2]
  • The retina is the light-sensitive tissue layer at the back of the eye. [3]
  • Age-related changes cause lens proteins to break down and clump, creating cloudy areas; most age-related cataracts develop gradually, at variable rates. [1] [2]
  • Cataracts can affect one or both eyes but do not spread between eyes. [1]
  • Cloudy or blurry vision, faded or yellowish colors, glare or halos, difficulty seeing at night and ghosted or double images in the affected eye can occur; symptoms can also reflect other eye problems. [1] [2]
  • Older age, smoking, diabetes, sun exposure, steroid use, family history and previous eye injury are cataract risk factors; these do not establish an individual diagnosis. [1] [2]
  • Evaluation includes visual acuity or refraction, slit-lamp examination of front-of-eye structures, and a dilated examination to check the eye including retina and optic nerve. [1] [2]
  • For mild symptoms, updated glasses, brighter reading light and magnification can help function but do not remove the cataract. [1] [2]
  • Only surgery removes a cataract; the cloudy natural lens is removed and replaced by an artificial intraocular lens (IOL). [1] [2]
  • Surgery is considered when cataracts interfere with everyday activities; immediate surgery is not always needed, and benefits and risks should be discussed with an ophthalmologist. [1] [2]
  • UV-protective sunglasses and a brimmed hat, stopping smoking and protective eyewear can help reduce avoidable eye risks; these measures do not guarantee cataract prevention. [1] [2]
  • Sudden new floaters, flashes, or a curtain or shadow across vision can signal retinal detachment rather than gradual cataract change; seek an eye doctor or emergency room immediately. [3]
