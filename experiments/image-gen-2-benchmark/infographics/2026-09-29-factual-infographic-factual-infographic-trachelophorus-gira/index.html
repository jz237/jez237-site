Trachelophorus giraffa — The Rolled Leaf Nursery

A source-verified macro-biology plate of the Madagascar giraffe weevil, emphasizing female egg provisioning within a rolled leaf and the male/female neck difference.

Research behind the infographic

  • The accepted common and scientific names are giraffe weevil and Trachelophorus giraffa; this is an insect beetle in the leaf-rolling weevil family Attelabidae. [1] [2]
  • The species is endemic to forest in Madagascar and lives on trees, feeding on leaves. [1] [3]
  • The adult has a black head, neck, body, six legs and antennae, with red-orange hardened wing covers on its back. [2] [3]
  • Males have substantially longer necks than females; the zoo reports approximately two to three times as long. [1] [3]
  • The long male neck likely aids competition, but the graphic does not claim a single proven purpose. [1]
  • A female rolls a leaf into a tube around one egg. [1] [3]
  • The female separates the completed leaf packet from the tree, and it falls to the forest floor. [1] [3]
  • The rolled leaf provides food for the newly hatched larva in its first days. [1] [3]
  • The female and male are small beetles relative to the broad leaf they occupy; illustrations are magnified, not life-size. [1] [3]
  • The life-cycle sequence shown is adult female, one egg within a rolled leaf, larva feeding in the packet. [1] [3]
