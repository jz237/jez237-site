Masklophobia — Fear of Masks and Costumed Characters

Source-backed educational chart separating a descriptive fear name and preliminary cognitive research from general phobia symptoms and treatment.

Research behind the infographic

  • MASKLOPHOBIA is a descriptive term used in cognitive-science research for fear of costumed characters; fear of masks is a related documented presentation. Display definition: Fear of masks and costumed characters. [1] [2]
  • Masklophobia is not listed as a separately named DSM-5 disorder in the cited research; a descriptive name is not a standalone diagnosis. [1]
  • Masks and costumed characters can be fear triggers; imagined encounters with masked people, occupied costumes and unoccupied costumes were examined in the research. Responses vary. [1] [2]
  • Preliminary questionnaire research suggests face concealment and movement may contribute to fear of costumed characters; these are possible contributors, not a proven universal cause. [1]
  • Phobia symptoms can include severe anxiety and worry, palpitations, trembling, chest tightness and nausea. The chart shows racing heart, trembling and worry as possible symptoms, not universal ones. [2] [3]
  • Phobic fear can lead to avoidance, including avoiding places where feared objects may be encountered; avoidance can restrict daily life. The chart path is cue, anxiety, avoidance, and restricted activities, not a claimed brain circuit. [2]
  • A person can know a costume is not actually dangerous and still feel fear; the feeling of alarm does not establish that the object is a threat. [1]
  • CBT is a treatment for phobias. Therapist-supported gradual exposure is a general phobia treatment, not a masklophobia-specific trial result or a self-directed exposure prescription. [2] [3]
  • Seek professional help when phobia symptoms affect everyday life; educational information does not diagnose an individual. [2] [3]
