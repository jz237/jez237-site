Scoliosis — A Curve With a Twist

Source-backed anatomical guide to adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, trunk asymmetry, evaluation, individualized management and warning symptoms.

Research behind the infographic

  • Scoliosis can form a sideways C- or S-shaped curve; vertebrae also twist or rotate. [1] [2]
  • Thoracic and lumbar regions may be involved; shoulder, rib, waist and hip asymmetry can be signs. [2]
  • Most adolescent scoliosis is idiopathic: its exact cause is unknown; it can run in families. [1] [2]
  • Poor posture and heavy backpacks do not cause idiopathic scoliosis. [2] [3]
  • Small curves are often painless and unnoticed; growth spurts can worsen a curve. [2]
  • Clinical examination includes the forward bend test to reveal rib asymmetry; X-rays locate and measure the curve. [1] [2]
  • Treatment depends on curve size, location and remaining growth: observation for many mild curves; bracing for selected growing children helps prevent progression, not straighten an existing curve; surgery may be considered for severe or progressing curves. [1] [2]
  • Severe back pain or pain with limb weakness or numbness warrants prompt medical evaluation for other causes. [3]
  • Most children and teens can lead normal active lives with appropriate follow-up. [1] [3]
