Living Gears of the Issus Nymph

A museum-quality direct-canvas infographic showing how real juvenile Issus planthoppers use paired biological gear teeth on their hind legs to synchronize a jump, and how the gears vanish at the final molt.

Research behind the infographic

  • Issus coleoptratus is a jumping planthopper insect. [1] [2]
  • In Issus coleoptratus nymphs, opposing toothed strips on the two hind-leg trochantera interlock as functional gears. [1] [2]
  • The interlocking gears mechanically synchronize the propulsive hind legs during a jump. [1] [2]
  • High-speed video showed the hind legs beginning their propulsive movement within about 30 microseconds of one another. [1] [2]
  • Synchronized propulsion limits unwanted yaw that could spin the insect during takeoff. [1] [2]
  • Each juvenile gear strip is approximately 400 micrometres long and carries 10 to 12 teeth. [1] [2]
  • The two gear strips have matching tooth counts and therefore a 1:1 gearing ratio. [1] [2]
  • The teeth are asymmetric and curved because they transmit motion in the jump-launch direction rather than in both rotational directions. [1] [2]
  • Rounded tooth bases reduce stress concentration and help resist shearing. [1] [2]
  • The functional gears occur in juvenile nymphs and are lost at the final molt to adulthood. [1] [2]
  • A damaged juvenile gear may be replaced at a later molt, whereas an adult has no later molt to repair rigid cuticle. [2]
  • Adults synchronize their hind legs without the juvenile interlocking gear teeth. [1] [2]
  • The nymph is a small six-legged insect with the standard head, thorax and abdomen body regions of an insect. [1]
