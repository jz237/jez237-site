The Scorpionfly’s Nuptial Gift

Panorpa communis courtship food gifts, anatomy and woodland scavenging, with a clearly generalized internal insect schematic.

Research behind the infographic

  • Panorpa communis is a scorpionfly in the order Mecoptera found in UK woodland edges and hedgerows on nettles and brambles. [1]
  • The male presents a collected insect as a nuptial gift to a female during courtship; scorpionflies may also offer secreted saliva. [1] [2]
  • The male’s curved bulbous abdominal tip is genitalia used in mating, not a sting; the female abdomen tapers. [1] [3]
  • Panorpa have four patterned wings, a long beak bearing chewing mouthparts, and a yellowish-brown body. [2] [3]
  • Adults scavenge dead invertebrates and may take insects from spider webs. [1] [2]
  • Eggs are laid in a moist environment; larvae scavenge and pupate in soil, as described for Panorpa. [1] [2]
  • A generalized insect digestive tract processes food and absorbs nutrients; spiracles connect to branching tracheae carrying air; a ventral nerve cord connects ganglia. [4]
  • Scorpionflies are small insects; the 3/8 inch body-length reference applies to Panorpa generally rather than a precise species measurement. [2]
