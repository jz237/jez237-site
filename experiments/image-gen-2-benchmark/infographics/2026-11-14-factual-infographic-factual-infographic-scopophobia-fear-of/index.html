Scopophobia — Fear of Being Watched or Stared At

Named fear of being watched, contextualized with social-anxiety mechanisms and care.

Research behind the infographic

  • Scopophobia is a term for fear of being watched or stared at, particularly by unfamiliar people. [1]
  • Fear of scrutiny and negative judgment can occur in social anxiety; this descriptive fear name alone is not a diagnosis. [1] [2]
  • Anxiety can arise before a social situation; possible bodily symptoms include sweating, trembling and rapid heart rate. [2]
  • Avoidance may feel helpful in the short term while anxiety remains without treatment. [2]
  • Anxiety can occur even when there is no current threat; feeling scrutinized does not establish actual danger or others’ hostile intentions. [2]
  • CBT is research-supported for social anxiety; exposure therapy progressively confronts feared situations as part of treatment. [2]
  • Seek professional help when anxiety interferes with everyday life; treatment should be tailored with a qualified clinician. [2] [3]
