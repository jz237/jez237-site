Flehmen — The Cat’s Chemical-Sampling Pause

Flehmen, paired vomeronasal anatomy and fluid-borne chemical sensing, with limits on what is known.

Research behind the infographic

  • Cats have a paired vomeronasal organ near the roof of the mouth, connected with oral and nasal cavities via nasopalatine canals. [2]
  • Flehmen follows investigation of scent material and aids access of fluid-borne chemical cues to the vomeronasal organ. [2]
  • The vomeronasal duct has sensory receptor epithelium medially and respiratory epithelium laterally in its middle portion; glands, vessels, nerves and supporting cartilage surround it. [1]
  • Vomeronasal sensory neurons send axons to the accessory olfactory bulb. [2]
  • The main olfactory epithelium detects inhaled volatile chemicals; the accessory system is associated with fluid-borne chemical cues, but their functions are not completely exclusive. [2]
  • Chemical communication participates in social recognition and reproduction; detailed feline central processing remains incompletely understood. [2]
