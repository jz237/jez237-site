Research behind the infographic

  • Claustrophobia is an intense fear of confined or enclosed spaces; fear can be out of proportion to actual danger. [1] [2]
  • Lifts, tunnels and enclosed rooms can trigger claustrophobia; even thinking about a confined situation can trigger anxiety. [1]
  • The same enclosed-space setting can be physically ordinary while felt danger is intense; distinguish perceived alarm from assessed situational danger. [1] [2]
  • Claustrophobia may produce sweating, trembling, shortness of breath and rapid heartbeat. [1]
  • A person may feel dizzy, confused or afraid of losing control during an intense reaction. [1]
  • Avoiding enclosed spaces can reinforce fear and restrict daily activities. [1] [2]
  • A person may anticipate a feared encounter before entering a confined space. [1] [2]
  • Cognitive behavioural therapy explores thoughts, feelings and behaviour and develops ways of responding to the phobia. [1] [2]
  • Exposure therapy is a CBT method that gradually approaches feared situations, sometimes with professional help. [1] [2]
  • A GP or qualified mental-health professional can help a person plan care when fear affects daily life. [1] [2]
