Trypophobia — Clusters of Holes

Artwork-led botanical explanation of trypophobia, supported visual-pattern findings, fear maintenance and carefully qualified general phobia support.

Research behind the infographic

  • Trypophobia is a name used in scientific research for fear or aversion to images of clusters of holes. [1] [2]
  • A lotus seed pod, soap bubbles and aerated chocolate are examples of visual triggers; responses vary between people. [2] [3]
  • Research found relatively high contrast energy at midrange spatial frequencies in triggering images; this describes visual pattern structure, not a proven universal cause or a diagnostic test. [1] [2]
  • Some people report sweating, panic and increased heart rate when viewing clustered holes. [2]
  • Fear can activate a heightened fight-or-flight response even in situations that are not really dangerous; an alarm response does not establish that an ordinary pictured seed pod is dangerous. [4]
  • Avoiding feared situations can bring short-term relief while maintaining or increasing fear over time; negative reinforcement is the technical label for this relief-maintained avoidance loop. [4]
  • For phobias in general, CBT and gradual exposure can help; trypophobia-specific treatment benefit is not established by these sources. A clinician can assess symptoms and life impact and plan appropriate support. [3] [4]
  • Seek help when symptoms affect daily life; triggering images online may be distressing and people should not be pushed into viewing them. [3]
