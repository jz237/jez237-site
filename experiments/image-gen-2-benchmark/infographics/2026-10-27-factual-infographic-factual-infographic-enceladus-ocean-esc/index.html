Enceladus — Ocean Escaping Through Ice

Enceladus ocean structure, plume transport and a qualified model of tidal heating and hydrothermal circulation.

Research behind the infographic

  • Enceladus is an icy moon of Saturn, about 500 kilometers across. [1] [2]
  • A global salty liquid-water ocean lies beneath its icy crust, above a rocky core. [1] [2]
  • South-polar fractures informally called tiger stripes release jets of water vapor and ice grains from the subsurface ocean. [1] [2]
  • Some ejected material falls back to the surface; some escapes to supply Saturn’s E ring. [1]
  • Cassini sampled the plume and detected water, salts, organic materials and silica particles; these are chemical clues, not evidence that life has been found. [1] [2]
  • Silica particles support hot water–rock interactions in the interior; hydrothermal circulation is inferred rather than photographed. [1] [2]
  • Saturn’s tides deform Enceladus as it follows an elliptical orbit; tidal friction is a proposed internal heat source. [2]
  • In a proposed porous-core model, ocean water enters deformable rock, is heated by tidal friction and rises back toward the ocean; the detailed interior and heat distribution remain modeled. [2]
  • The cutaway and orbit are schematic, with layer thicknesses, orbit eccentricity, jet size and particle size not to scale. [1] [2]
  • No life has been confirmed on Enceladus; potential habitability is not a detection of organisms. [1] [2]
