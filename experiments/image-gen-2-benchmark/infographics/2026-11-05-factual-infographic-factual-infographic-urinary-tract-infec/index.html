Urinary Tract Infection — Bladder and Ascending Infection

Source-backed urinary anatomy, bladder infection mechanism, symptoms, risk context, care and urgent warnings.

Research behind the infographic

  • The urinary tract includes two kidneys, two ureters, a bladder and a urethra; kidneys produce urine, ureters carry it to the bladder, the bladder stores it, and the urethra carries it outside. [3]
  • Bacteria, usually from the bowel, can enter through the urethra and multiply in the bladder; an untreated bladder infection can develop into a kidney infection. [1] [2]
  • Burning urination, urgency, frequency, lower abdominal discomfort and sometimes blood in urine are symptoms; smell or dark urine alone does not establish UTI. [1] [2]
  • Catheters, impaired bladder emptying, pregnancy and menopause can increase risk. [2]
  • Clinical assessment uses symptoms and history and may include urine testing; urine testing is not always needed. [1] [2]
  • Antibiotics are often used for bacterial bladder infection; take prescribed treatment as directed. Rest and appropriate fluids may help symptoms, but cranberry products do not treat an established infection. [1] [2]
  • Fever or chills, back pain below the ribs, blood in urine, pregnancy, or rapid worsening warrant urgent medical advice; new confusion, marked drowsiness or difficulty speaking warrant emergency help. [2]
  • Not holding urine, taking time to empty the bladder and drinking appropriate fluids may help urinary health; follow clinician fluid limits when applicable. [2] [3]
